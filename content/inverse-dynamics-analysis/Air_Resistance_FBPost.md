A factor that is overlooked in the golf swing is air resistance. It's pretty much always there and there isn't much you can do about it, so why look? It turns out that while it cannot really be manipulated, ignoring it can influence our interpretation of data.
In the attached calculation I took the impact values from Sasho's video on User Reference frame
(https://vimeo.com/sashomackenzie/userframevsnesbitframe) for the forces and torques for what I believe is a 7 iron. I assumed 37" length and midpoint of hands location 3.5" from the end of the grip and COM location 10" from end of the club as that seems to be about where my 7 iron balances.
I then assumed the air drag force of 2 lbs was acting 1" from the end of the club. This is based on a paper on Ping's turbulators from Paul Wood and Erik Henrickson where the drag force was measured between 2 and 9 N for a driver at 100 mph. I figured a 7 iron at 95 would be similar as the 7 iron is probably less aerodynamic (https://www.researchgate.net/.../274150302_Experimental...)
Next I calculated what the moment of force from this force and the remaining force at the handle (now 5.5 lbs. vs. 7.5 lbs.). This moment of force was much less positive and as a result, the negative couple in the alpha plane would be significantly lower. In this case it was only 60% of what it would be without factoring in air resistance. This seems very significant in size and implies air resistance probably shouldn't be ignored.
