AffineDrift’s name comes from nonlinear control theory – where we try and use math to tame chaotic systems. A “control-affine” system is one in which a nonlinear system is still “linear” in control. An affine system can be separated into a drift component and an actuated component. In the drift component, the history of the system is expressed as the current state – positions and velocities of every component. These positions and velocities can create forces and torques that are seen in the present. They are echoes from the actions of the past. Marcus Aurelius was right after all. 

What are the implications for the practitioner? The present must be understood in terms of the past. History matters. The state of the system plays a large role in what is observed in terms of kinetics in the present. What we do is a combination of what we are doing, and what has already been done.
