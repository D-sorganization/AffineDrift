Key Insights

Limitation 1: The Equivalent Couple Problem

Inverse dynamics cannot distinguish between different force/couple combinations that produce identical motion. This spatial ambiguity means that the same observed motion can result from fundamentally different force applications, making it impossible to uniquely determine the forces acting on the system from motion data alone.

Limitation 2: The Control-Affine Decomposition Problem

Traditional inverse dynamics cannot separate active muscular control from passive mechanical dynamics (drift). The control-affine framework provides a mathematical structure for this decomposition, revealing that what we observe is a combination of what we're actively doing and what the system's history (momentum, gravity, constraints) is doing.

Limitation 3: The Air Resistance Problem

Neglecting air resistance introduces systematic, speed-dependent errors. At impact speeds, drag forces of 1.5-2 lbs can cause 40% errors in calculated couples. This limitation is particularly significant because it affects the spatial distribution of forces (couples more than forces) and is most pronounced near impact.
