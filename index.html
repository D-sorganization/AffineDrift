"What we do now echoes in eternity"

— Marcus Aurelius

AffineDrift's name comes from nonlinear control theory – where we try and use math to tame chaotic systems. A "control-affine" system is one in which a nonlinear system is still "linear" in control. An affine system can be separated into a drift component and an actuated component. In the drift component, the history of the system is expressed as the current state – positions and velocities of every component. These positions and velocities can create forces and torques that are seen in the present. They are echoes from the actions of the past. Marcus Aurelius was right after all.

What are the implications for the practitioner? The present must be understood in terms of the past. History matters. The state of the system plays a large role in what is observed in terms of kinetics in the present. What we do is a combination of what we are doing, and what has already been done.

What is Affine Control Theory?

An affine control system is a fundamental concept in nonlinear control theory. These systems are characterized by being nonlinear in the state variables but linear (affine) in the control inputs.

Mathematical Definition

A control-affine system takes the general form:

\[\dot{x}(t) = f_0(x) + \sum_{i=1}^{m} u_i(t) \cdot f_i(x)\]

where:

  • \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n\) is the state vector
  • \(u = (u_1, \ldots, u_m) \in \mathbb{R}^m\) are the control inputs
  • \(f_0(x)\) is the drift vector field (system dynamics without control)
  • \(f_i(x)\) are the control vector fields

Key Properties

Linearity in Control

Control inputs appear linearly, making the system affine rather than fully nonlinear

Drift Dynamics

The \(f_0(x)\) term represents inherent system behavior independent of control

Controllability

Lie brackets of vector fields determine the system's controllable subspace

Geometric Structure

Natural interpretation on manifolds via differential geometry

The Golf Swing as an Affine System

The golf swing is a remarkably sophisticated example of human motor control. When viewed through the lens of affine control theory, the swing reveals itself as a high-dimensional system where muscular inputs (controls) interact with biomechanical constraints and gravitational dynamics (drift).

System Components

State Space

Joint angles, angular velocities, club position and orientation in \(\mathbb{R}^n\)

Drift Term \(f_0(x)\)

Gravity, inertial forces, passive tissue dynamics

Control Inputs \(u_i\)

Muscle torques at joints, force generation patterns

Control Vector Fields \(f_i(x)\)

Moment arms, biomechanical coupling between muscles and joints

Why This Matters

Modeling the golf swing as an affine control system enables:

  • Optimal Control: Computing theoretically optimal swing trajectories
  • Sensitivity Analysis: Understanding how small changes in muscle activation affect ball flight
  • Learning Algorithms: Designing practice routines based on controllability analysis
  • Biomechanical Insights: Identifying critical control points in the swing sequence
