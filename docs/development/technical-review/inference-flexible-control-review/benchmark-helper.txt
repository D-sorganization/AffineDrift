Here is the independent verification and mathematical review of the three proposed textbook benchmark problems for issue #4905.

---

### Benchmark 1: Bayesian Scalar Estimation & SDOF Free-Decay Identifiability

#### 1. Scalar Gaussian Posterior
* **Setup:** Prior $x \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2)$ with $\mu_0 = 0$, $\sigma_0^2 = 1$ (prior precision $\Lambda_0 = 1$).
  Observations $y = (y_1, y_2) = (1, 3)$ with i.i.d. noise variance $R = 1$ (observation precision $\Lambda_{\text{obs}} = R^{-1} = 1$).
* **Posterior Precision:**
  $$\Lambda_{\text{post}} = \Lambda_0 + \sum_{k=1}^N R^{-1} = 1 + (1 + 1) = 3$$
* **Posterior Variance:**
  $$\sigma_{\text{post}}^2 = \frac{1}{\Lambda_{\text{post}}} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.333333$$
* **Posterior Mean:**
  $$\mu_{\text{post}} = \sigma_{\text{post}}^2 \left(\frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2} + \sum_{k=1}^N \frac{y_k}{R}\right) = \frac{1}{3} \left(0 + 1 + 3\right) = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.333333$$
* **Verification:** **Correct.** Precision = $3$, Variance = $1/3$, Mean = $4/3$.

#### 2. Free Decay Identifiability ($m\ddot{q} + c\dot{q} + kq = 0$)
* **Equation:** Dividing through by $m > 0$ yields the monic form:
  $$\ddot{q} + 2\zeta\omega_n \dot{q} + \omega_n^2 q = 0, \quad \text{where } 2\zeta\omega_n = \frac{c}{m}, \; \omega_n^2 = \frac{k}{m}$$
* **Identifiability:** Since the trajectory $q(t)$ is uniquely generated by the initial value problem $(q(0), \dot{q}(0))$ and the coefficients $(c/m, k/m)$, observing $q(t)$ only identifies the ratios $\theta_1 = c/m$ and $\theta_2 = k/m$.
* **Invariance:** Any scaling of system parameters $(m, c, k) \mapsto (s m, s c, s k)$ for scalar $s > 0$ yields identical homogeneous ODE dynamics and identical $q(t)$. Mass $m$, damping $c$, and stiffness $k$ cannot be uniquely disentangled without external forcing $u(t)$ with known magnitude ($m^{-1} u(t)$).
* **Limitations:** Boundary/singular cases: if $q(0) = \dot{q}(0) = 0$, $q(t) \equiv 0$ and no parameters are identifiable. If $k/m \le 0$ or $c/m < 0$, motion is unstable/non-oscillatory, though ratios remain mathematically identifiable over short finite horizons if noise is bounded.

### Benchmark 2: Euler–Bernoulli Cantilever & Tip Invariants

#### 1. Continuous Cantilever Benchmark Values
* **Parameters:** Length $L = 1$, flexural rigidity $EI = 1$, mass per unit length $\mu = 1$.
* **Characteristic Equation:** Fixed-free boundary conditions require:
  $$\cos(\beta) \cosh(\beta) + 1 = 0$$
* **Spatial Roots ($\beta_n$):**
  * $\beta_1 \approx 1.8751040687$
  * $\beta_2 \approx 4.6940911330$
  * $\beta_3 \approx 7.8547574382$
* **Natural Frequencies:** $\omega_i = \beta_i^2 \sqrt{\frac{EI}{\mu L^4}} = \beta_i^2$ (rad/s), so cyclic frequencies $f_i = \frac{\beta_i^2}{2\pi}$ (Hz):
  * $f_1 = \frac{(1.8751040687)^2}{2\pi} \approx \frac{3.516015269}{2\pi} \approx 0.559599\text{ Hz}$
  * $f_2 = \frac{(4.6940911330)^2}{2\pi} \approx \frac{22.03449157}{2\pi} \approx 3.506894\text{ Hz}$
  * $f_3 = \frac{(7.8547574382)^2}{2\pi} \approx \frac{61.69721434}{2\pi} \approx 9.819416\text{ Hz}$
* **Verification:** Roots and formulas are **exact and verified**.

#### 2. Tip Mass Perturbation & Rayleigh Quotient Invariance
* **Generalized Mass Modification:** Adding a concentrated tip mass $m_t \ge 0$ at $x = L$ updates the mass operator / discretized mass matrix:
  $$M_{\text{new}} = M_0 + m_t \boldsymbol{e}_{\text{tip}} \boldsymbol{e}_{\text{tip}}^\top$$
  where $\boldsymbol{e}_{\text{tip}}$ is the kinematic extraction vector for tip translational displacement.
* **Frequency Monotonicity:** Because $m_t \boldsymbol{e}_{\text{tip}} \boldsymbol{e}_{\text{tip}}^\top \succeq 0$, $M_{\text{new}} \succeq M_0$. For any admissible mode shape $\boldsymbol{\phi}$, the Rayleigh quotient satisfies:
  $$R(\boldsymbol{\phi}) = \frac{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\top K \boldsymbol{\phi}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\top M_{\text{new}} \boldsymbol{\phi}} \le \frac{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\top K \boldsymbol{\phi}}{\boldsymbol{\phi}^\top M_0 \boldsymbol{\phi}}$$
  By the Courant–Fischer min-max theorem, all ordered natural frequencies are non-increasing: $\omega_i(m_t) \le \omega_i(0)$.

#### 3. Static Compliance
* Tip deflection under concentrated tip load $P$: $\delta_{\text{tip}} = \frac{P L^3}{3 EI}$. Tip compliance $C_{\text{tip}} = \frac{\delta_{\text{tip}}}{P} = \frac{L^3}{3 EI}$. For $L=1, EI=1$, $C_{\text{tip}} = 1/3 \approx 0.333333$. **Verified.**

#### 4. Modeling Limitations & Finite Linear Taper Caveat
* **Moving Base:** The model assumes an inertial clamp ($\delta(0) = \theta(0) = 0$). Moving base kinematics (accelerations, rotational frame coupling) are omitted.
* **Finite Element Taper Approximation:** For Euler–Bernoulli Hermite cubic beam elements, evaluating $EI(x)$ at the element midpoint $\bar{x}_e$ evaluates the element stiffness as $K_e \approx EI(\bar{x}_e) \int_{0}^{l_e} \mathbf{N}''(s)^\top \mathbf{N}''(s)\, ds$. When $EI(x) = E(I_0 + \alpha x)$, $I(x)$ varies linearly, but element stiffness requires $\int_{0}^{l_e} EI(s) \mathbf{N}''(s)^\top \mathbf{N}''(s)\, ds$. Because Hermite second derivatives $\mathbf{N}''(s)$ are linear in $s$, the integrand contains products of linear shape derivative terms and linear $EI(s)$ (quadratic polynomials in $s$). Midpoint evaluation (1-point Gauss integration) under-integrates the quadratic term; exact stiffness requires at least a 2-point Gauss quadrature rule or analytical polynomial integration.

### Benchmark 3: Antagonistic Rotor Dynamics & Invariant Set

#### 1. Closed-Form Trajectory under Constant Input and Zero Damping/Gravity
* **System Equations:**
  $$J \ddot{q} = r F_0 (a_1 - a_2) - b \dot{q} - g_{\text{coef}} \sin(q)$$
  $$\dot{a}_i = \frac{u_i - a_i}{\tau}, \quad i \in \{1, 2\}$$
* **Assumptions:** $b = 0$, $g_{\text{coef}} = 0$, constant $u = (u_1, u_2)$, initial conditions $a(0) = a_0$, $q(0) = q_0$, $\dot{q}(0) = v_0$.
* **Definitions:**
  $$A = \frac{r F_0}{J}, \quad \Delta u = d = u_1 - u_2, \quad \Delta a_0 = a_{0,1} - a_{0,2}, \quad c = \Delta a_0 - d$$
* **Differential Equation for Net Activation $\Delta a(t) = a_1(t) - a_2(t)$:**
  $$\dot{\Delta a}(t) = -\frac{1}{\tau}(\Delta a(t) - d) \implies \Delta a(t) = d + c\, e^{-t/\tau}$$
* **Angular Acceleration:**
  $$\dot{v}(t) = A \Delta a(t) = A \left[d + c\, e^{-t/\tau}\right]$$
* **Velocity Integration:**
  $$v(t) = v_0 + \int_0^t A \left[d + c\, e^{-s/\tau}\right] ds = v_0 + A \left[ d\, t + c \tau \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \right]$$
* **Position Integration:**
  $$q(t) = q_0 + \int_0^t v(s)\, ds = q_0 + v_0 t + A \left[ \frac{d\, t^2}{2} + c \tau \left( t - \tau \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \right) \right]$$
* **Verification:** Formulas for $v(t)$ and $q(t)$ match the benchmark statement **identically**.

#### 2. Total Mechanical Energy and Dissipation Rate
* **Candidate Lyapunov/Hamiltonian Energy:**
  $$E(q, v) = \frac{1}{2} J v^2 + g_{\text{coef}} (1 - \cos q)$$
* **Time Derivative:**
  $$\dot{E} = J v \dot{v} + g_{\text{coef}} \sin(q) v = v \left[ J \ddot{q} + g_{\text{coef}} \sin(q) \right] = v \left[ r F_0(a_1 - a_2) - b v \right] = r F_0 (a_1 - a_2) v - b v^2$$
* **Verification:** Exact match. For unforced/passive systems ($a_1 = a_2$), $\dot{E} = -b v^2 \le 0$, confirming standard dissipation.

#### 3. Invariance Analysis and Numerical Discretization Limitations
* **Continuous Invariance of $[0, 1]$:**
  * For $\dot{a}_i = \frac{u_i - a_i}{\tau}$ with constant/bounded $u_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ and initial state $a_i(0) \in [0, 1]$:
    $$\left.\dot{a}_i\right|_{a_i=0} = \frac{u_i}{\tau} \ge 0, \qquad \left.\dot{a}_i\right|_{a_i=1} = \frac{u_i - 1}{\tau} \le 0$$
  * By Nagumo's theorem / vector field subtangency, the hypercube $[0, 1]^2$ is positively invariant under the continuous-time dynamics.
* **Numerical Limitations:**
  * Explicit time-stepping schemes (e.g., Forward Euler: $a_k^{n+1} = a_k^n + \frac{\Delta t}{\tau}(u_k^n - a_k^n)$) do **not** preserve $[0, 1]$ invariance unless $\Delta t \le \tau$. If $\Delta t > \tau$, numerical overshoot/alternating oscillations occur. If $\Delta t > 2\tau$, the discretization becomes unstable.
  * Higher-order Runge–Kutta schemes (e.g., RK4) can also exhibit overshoot on stiff transients. Numerical enforcement requires either step-size constraints ($\Delta t \le \tau$), implicit integration (Backward Euler), or explicit metric clamping ($a \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}(a, 0, 1)$).
