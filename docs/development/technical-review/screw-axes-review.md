## Confirmed Findings

- **P1 — Motion, Displacement and Uniqueness:** the epigraph, theorem, intuition and summary confuse one finite displacement or an instantaneous velocity field with an arbitrary full trajectory about one fixed axis. Pure translation has no unique finite axis; identity and half-turn sign/branch ambiguity are omitted. A pure translation has zero rotation, not a finite translation achieved by an unexplained infinitesimal angle. The formal axis-point equation inserts an erroneous cross product before the restricted inverse. The proof sketch calls axial displacement pitch without dividing by angle. Direction sign is not uniquely determined by positive angle at pi. Distinguish finite displacement, instantaneous screw and a constant-twist trajectory.
- **P1 — Convention and Frame Direction:** the angular-first convention is wrongly attributed to both Murray–Li–Sastry and Lynch–Park; Featherstone is incorrectly said to use the reversed order. Explicitly define T_AB as coordinates B to A, spatial/body derivatives and the reference point of the linear component. Adjoint direction reverses in the theorem, wrench sections disagree, and Python applies Ad and Ad-transpose in the same direction, violating power. Re-expression of the same physical motion is not a change of physical observer: power is not invariant under Galilean boosts. A wrench belongs to the dual algebra, not se(3), and need not share the motion screw's axis.
- **P1 — Twist and Wrench Mechanics:** se(3) matrices are not generally skew-symmetric4x4 matrices. The revolute-joint point velocity is conflated with the twist's linear coordinate and is incorrectly claimed equal on axis. The force at r=(0,0,d) along +x has +y moment, not −y. Define signed pitch by axial projection, units, normalization and the zero-angular/zero-force exceptions. Lie bracket is a commutator/second-order composition effect, not simply relative velocity.
- **P1 — Reciprocity and Constraints:** a twist–wrench power pairing differs from the reciprocal bilinear form between two same-ordered geometric screws. Ordinary Euclidean6D orthogonality is not a correct universal definition; geometric reciprocal axes need not be perpendicular. A revolute constraint annuls relative admissible motion only for an ideal reaction; motors/friction can do work. Force and moment reference points remain necessary despite spatial notation. Six joint freedoms do not determine six physical bodies or an automatic count of force equations.
- **P1 — Four-Bar Statics and Wrench Sets:** the joint twist uses(qx,qy) instead of(qy,−qx), and its reciprocal equation is dimensionally invalid. A planar pin transmits two force components; force along a bar follows a separate unloaded two-force-member assumption. Ground attachment, link free-body balance, equal/opposite internal forces, passive/actuated joints and external moments are missing. Summing all joint wrenches on one free body is invalid. Bilateral reaction subspaces, unilateral friction cones and bounded wrench polytopes are different sets; equilibrium requires the negative external load and all link balances, not merely a convex hull assertion.
- **P1 — Exercises and Code:** exercise12 asks for a false reciprocity conclusion (moment−2 about z is not null to free z rotation). Exercise6 places(1,0,0) 'on the z-axis' and asks for an undefined bounded polytope. Exercise9 ambiguously calls a force vertical in an xy mechanism and asserts out-of-plane reactions absent without a planar model. T=I+hat(V)epsilon is first-order, not an exact SE(3) displacement. Transform directions, units, reciprocal types and boundary conditions need explicit definitions in all15 exercises. Validate executable examples with independent point velocity, moment, power, finite displacement and free-body calculations.
- **P2 — Exposition and Paired Scope:** the web lacks most of the substantive print treatment. Preserve all useful topics and15 exercises while reconciling both editions, simplifying duplicate wrench material without losing its examples. Homogeneous embedding does not rescue a violated law of linear algebra: translation is affine, directions have homogeneous weight0, and rotation/translation arrays remain legitimate computational representations. Remove universal speed/engine implementation claims and unsupported historical priority details unless primary sources verify them. Redraw the geometry consistently and connect the lesson to grip wrenches, club velocity, moving frames and testable golf interpretation.

## Independent Derivations and Editorial Decisions

The common convention is T_AB mapping B point coordinates to A. Substituting r_A=Rr_B+p in u=v+omega cross r yields v_A=Rv_B+p cross R omega_B; direct point-force moments give n_A=Rn_B+p cross Rf_B. Consequently V_A=X V_B and F_A=X^-T F_B, while F_B=X^T F_A. Tests compare these with direct Cartesian velocities, force moments and applied power, not merely two mutually compensating matrix identities. Body coordinates describe laboratory-relative motion expressed on the body; they are not the derivative of constant material coordinates. Observer boosts subtract physical velocity and change force power by minus f dot U.

For a finite nonidentity displacement, d=s dot p and (I-R)q=p_perp with q perpendicular to s. On that plane Jx=s cross x and J²=-I; the inverse is half[I+cot(theta/2)J]. No extra cross product precedes the restricted inverse. The finite pitch is d/theta. The geometric line is unique but q along the line is not; half-turn orientation and added-full-turn branches qualify signed parameters. Translation and identity do not have a unique rotational axis. The 1/theta conditioning near translation is stated explicitly. Interpose displacement is T1 inverse(T0), not absolute T1. For Rz(pi/2), p=(1,-1,.2), the example recovers q=(1,0,0), h=.4/pi=.1273239544735163 m/rad.

The instantaneous axis q=omega cross v/||omega||² and h=omega dot v/||omega||² are verified by vector identities and point velocities. Twist magnitude is not a mixed-unit Euclidean norm. The hat matrix has only its rotational block skew-symmetric. A constant twist integrates by one exponential; general time-varying motion requires ordered increments. The commutator is a second-order composition effect, not velocity subtraction. The generic finite-axis routine rejects angles at/below1e-8rad as a documented example numerical contract, not a universal sensor threshold.

Wrench central-axis q_F=f cross n/||f||² and h_F=f dot n/||f||² are separate from the motion screw. Pure force, offset force, pure couple and zero cases are distinguished. The moment signs at (0,0,d) with +x force and (0,2,0) with +x force are independently checked as +y and -z. Sum of point-load power produces F^TV, with common frame and origin required. Grip interaction may transfer subsystem power while equal/opposite ideal interface powers cancel on the combined system. Slip/compliance changes that conclusion through relative motion; net wrench alone does not identify pressure distribution, hand force sharing or neural objective.

Twist–wrench reciprocity uses the dual pairing. Two same-ordered geometric screws use the cross-block bilinear form, yielding (q1-q2) dot(s1 cross s2) for zero pitch, not an ordinary six-dimensional dot product or a perpendicular-axis rule. An ideal reaction annuls every admissible relative twist; coincidental zero power on one measured velocity is weaker. Motors and friction are separate load channels. Reaction subspaces, unilateral cones and bounded polytopes have different hypotheses; support requires minus the external load and all body balances.

The four-bar is fully specified: A=(0,0), B=(1,1), C=(3,1), D=(4,0)m; massless rigid planar links, fixed ground, frictionless passive B/C/D pins, optional motor at A, downward W at E=(x_E,1). Forces b/c are on the coupler, so -b/-c act on the side links. CD moment gives cx=-cy; BC moment about B gives 2cy=(x_E-1)W; coupler force gives b=(cy,W-cy); AB moment gives tau_A=by-bx=W(2-x_E), with metre lever arms retained in interpretation. W100/x2 gives b(50,50),c(-50,50),tau0; x2.5 gives b(75,25),c(-75,75),tau-50. AB is not a two-force member when the motor couple is nonzero. Unit AB angular speed gives v_B=(-1,1),v_C=(-1,-1),omega_BC=-1,v_E,y=2-x_E; virtual power independently recovers the motor torque. Passive centered equilibrium is not claimed stable or a rigid mechanism.

The bounded support example instead concerns one body with two vertical bilateral force actuators at x0 and xL, each bounded by Fmax. Resultant (n_z,f_y)=(Lu2,u1+u2) is a parallelogram, not an undefined joint polytope. For L1/Fmax10, four vertices and the feasible15N versus infeasible25N centered loads are supplied; unilateral upward-only bounds alter the set. This support example is not the four-bar free-body model.
