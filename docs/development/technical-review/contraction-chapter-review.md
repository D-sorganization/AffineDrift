## Findings and Required Derivations

The finite-distance proof must transport one initial connecting curve through the flow. Its tangent then obeys the variational equation, but the transported curve is not generally a minimizing geodesic at later times. The original unit-speed assertion on a fixed [0,1] interval is false for arbitrary length; its quadratic derivative double-counts terms. Use length and an infimum over admissible curves, so a minimizing geodesic need not exist. Uniform metric bounds control physical distance, and a straight initial segment or explicit intrinsic-distance factor is needed for the Euclidean initial-chord bound. Geodesic convexity alone does not justify the latter. Forward completeness, invariant connecting paths and the common implemented input/policy must be stated.

For A=[[-2,1],[0,-3]], A^T M+MA=[[-4a,a-5b],[a-5b,2b-6c]]. The symmetric-part eigenvalues are(-5 plus/minus sqrt2)/2; identity rate is(5-sqrt2)/2, not half that value. PSD ordering is not entrywise. A fixed-rate metric search is an LMI; simultaneous rate/metric optimization is generally bilinear and can use bisection under suitable normalization. A Lyapunov-equation example and generalized eigenvalue calculation will make the units and rate check explicit.

A triangular cascade with contracting diagonal blocks needs bounded transformed coupling and a weighted metric. For rates a,b and target lambda<min(a,b), weight theta>k²/[4(a-lambda)(b-lambda)] suffices; the denominator without the target-rate subtraction proves only some positive rate. Large theta increases the second block's weight. A generalized block Jacobian must not be transformed twice. A pure position integrator retains offsets despite velocity damping; exact minimum rate need not be attained for a Jordan cascade. Biological chains have bidirectional inertia/contact coupling and are not automatically dynamical cascades.

For delta u=-K delta x and W=M^-1,Y=KW, the dual residual is -Wdot+AW+WA^T-GY-Y^TG^T+2lambda W. A includes sum u_i Dg_i and Mdot follows f+Gu. Differential feedback is not a finite controller u=-K delta x. A complete CCM formulation requires all input directions/values or explicit cancellation constraints, path integration and feasible target/feedback assumptions. State/time-dependent W means this is a differential inequality, not independent pointwise algebra. Grid success is a search diagnostic; certified margins plus coverage/regularity or domain SOS certificates are needed between samples. A neural factor must be a matrix with rank/positive-floor conditions.

Discrete transport applies to a curve tangent, not an exact difference of arbitrary nonlinear trajectories. Domain mapping, metric comparison and actual numerical/controller map matter. Riccati certificates have no universal ranking against contraction. The Van der Pol equation has no feedback. Any diagonal metric with constant first entry has residual H11=2lambda a>0 for lambda>0, immediately refuting the advertised ansatz everywhere. The correct off-diagonal entry is m1+(-1-2epsilon x1 x2)m2. A nonconstant periodic orbit obstructs uniformly nondegenerate same-time full contraction; an invariant positive-dimensional set alone does not. Transverse orbital convergence permits phase offsets and requires its own domain/theorem. No successful transverse metric search will be invented.

Disturbance claims must compare nominal versus perturbed solutions, or two inputs via their difference. General state-dependent metrics need contraction uniformly over the admitted interpolated inputs and a bound on M^(1/2)G; a nominal drift certificate alone does not make every forced field contracting. State-independent metrics permit a clean additive-noise/input proof. State boundedness additionally requires a bounded nominal solution; xdot=-x+t shows contraction without bounded trajectories. Ordinary stability is not attraction; asymptotic is not necessarily exponential; ISS is stronger than merely bounded input/bounded state. The finite Lyapunov function is squared intrinsic distance, not generally (x-x*)^T M(x)(x-x*).

The biomechanics section currently identifies muscle stiffness with a certificate and asserts expert golfers learn an optimal metric without evidence. Replace those claims with a specified second-order joint model, what damping/stiffness can prove in that model, delay/activation/contact limitations, and a finite-horizon output-sensitivity experiment. A positive stiffness does not make mechanical energy a strictly contracting full-state metric; instantaneous drift eigenvalues are insufficient, and passive conservative dynamics cannot erase all state uncertainty. State scaling, torque policy, shaft deformation, impact timing and observed output must be treated together. No empirical contraction rates or neurological objectives are inferred from segment ordering or EMG alone.

The stochastic attribution misspells Nicolas Tabareau as Paulo Tabuada, including the shared bibliography. Pham/Tabareau/Slotine's paper explicitly treats independent Wiener processes and state-independent metrics. Derive the constant-metric independent-noise bound with both diffusion traces, then give exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck common-noise, independent-pair and single-trial variances. Multiplicative common noise can change mean-square stability; deterministic contraction alone is insufficient. RMS separation and expected distance are different. Observer guarantees require a specified error/noise/measurement model.

Tube radii use intrinsic distance; only state-independent metrics give exact ellipsoids from an endpoint quadratic. Disturbance radius is r0 exp(-lambda t)+(d/lambda)(1-exp(-lambda t)). A prescribed boundary needs phi'+lambda phi>=d, initial containment, domain invariance and feasible control. A generic singular gain formula is not a controller theorem. Output robustness at impact needs the delivery-map derivative and, for state-triggered events, event sensitivity. Preserve all eight exercise topics while correcting unattained optima, unbounded coupling, noise comparison and the dimensionally invalid first-order muscle equation.
