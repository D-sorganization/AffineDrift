## Findings and Argument

The opening omits modal velocities and internal muscle states while treating algebraic reactions as states; input affinity and independent ground actuation are conflated. Static control authority, potential-energy changes from stance, all-muscle stretch, torso/hip relative rotation, transition duration and force peaks are asserted without a suitable definition or measurement. Wrist articulation, lag angle and shaft deformation are different observables. Angular momentum depends on mass and velocity, not lag angle alone. The print and web editions make different unsupported neuroscience claims.

Repeated numerical DCR phase bands have no declared metric, input set, calculation or source support. In particular, the cited Nesbit 2005 article's full text contains no occurrence of “drift.” Its reconstructed golfer motions and fitted support treatment cannot validate a forward neural control law. The article's methods, Table 3 and relevant work/shaft passages were read; the complete paper was not read in this slice. Source tables also do not support the chapter's claimed universal 65m/s driver speed or shoulder-rate ranges.

The core mathematical error is equating a large instantaneous drift norm with small input gain, small delivered-state sensitivity and reduced metabolic cost. The correction will derive constrained input gain and transported endpoint/event sensitivity, distinguish actual torque from available torque sets, and show explicit counterexamples. Observability concerns distinguishable hidden states from measurements, not which phase controls the outcome. A forward zero-channel intervention must evolve from a complete common state under the declared contact/input convention; it is neither a muscle-silencing experiment nor a cost optimum.

Shaft flexibility is assigned a universal catapult benefit; maximum modal velocity is confused with maximum restoring force and acceleration. A free harmonic mode already disproves that equivalence. Constant-force versus constant-displacement loading gives opposite stiffness/storage trends. The material label steel/graphite does not specify the shaft's mass, flexural/torsional rigidity, damping, geometry or golfer adaptation. Ideal constraint reactions transmit momentum and redistribute subsystem energy while doing zero total work in stationary no-slip contact. Dissipation must be modeled separately.

Print equates 1−COR with a lost-energy fraction; web wrongly calls 0.83 a ball COR limit and misstates retained club speed. A declared central two-mass collision will supply consistent velocity and energy fractions, with separate caveats for off-center contact, spin, grip/shaft impedance, turf, and flight. The final claims that relinquishing control necessarily improves power/consistency contradict both mechanics and the corrected energy budget.

## Exercise Derivations and Answers

1. Substitute the mechanical equation into J vdot + Jdot v=0, solve the full-rank Schur system for lambda, and substitute back to get Dc and Bc. For vdot=(a,−a), subtract the two dynamic equations: (2−0.4−0.4+1)a=tau1−tau2 plus the corresponding bias difference. The denominator is 2.2; equal generalized inputs are normal to the allowed direction and disappear from the reduced acceleration. Bias changes the offset, not the derivative under these fixed-configuration assumptions.
2. Available-input DCR uses a supremum over a declared feasible set; actual-input DCR uses the realized input and can diverge when that input is zero despite substantial capacity. The orthogonal two-state example has norm ratio 1000 and complete authority over its second coordinate. To establish a phase trend, use consistent scaling, actual reconstructed states, an admissible torque model, feasible contacts and uncertainty; compute the trend rather than imposing it. Coordinate changes, input conventions or contact changes can alter the result.
3. For a differentiable output h, h(T)−h(t0)=integral h_x f dt + integral h_x G u dt along the actual trajectory, with an extra h_t term if explicitly time dependent. For club velocity, h includes geometry and flexible rates. Each evaluated integrand changes on a different forward trajectory; reusing it is not a finite counterfactual decomposition. An integral of acceleration-like contributions has velocity units, not energy units.
4. Linearize F around the nominal trajectory. Variation of constants gives Phi times initial-state error plus the transported input integral. At contact, differentiate g(x(T),T)=0, giving the event shift. For p0=.2, v=2, target1, T=.4, perturbation directions delta p0=.3 and delta v=−.2 give fixed-time delta p=.22 and delta T=−.11; at the event the position variation cancels. Initial modal rates and activation/tissue states are necessary when they affect F.
5. Rotor angle sensitivity to a torque impulse is (T−s)/I and rate sensitivity is 1/I. Thus the earlier impulse affects final angle more but neither has a rate advantage. A stable scalar mode weights input by exp[−10(T−s)]; equal .1s pulses at the ends of a one-second interval have late/early ratio exp9. A desired speed gain, without feasible controls, timing, objective and competing delivery constraints, cannot locate an optimal phase.
6. Invert the positive-definite two-coordinate inertia: off-diagonal gain is −b/(ac−b²). Both signs of b are compatible with positive definiteness. A model intervention must start from the same complete state, declare zeroed channel(s), retain/recompute feasible contacts and track comparable peak definitions. Persistence of an ordering is a prediction for that model/state; it does not identify measured muscle activation, conscious intention or a unique energy pathway.
7. Parameterize initial errors and phase inputs, collect the endpoint derivatives into K and write delta y≈K delta p. If K has a nontrivial nullspace, distinct causes have the same output. More synchronized state/force/shaft observations can reduce ambiguity; designed perturbations can discriminate models. Regularization selects an estimate under its prior/cost, not a unique causal history. Ball curvature additionally depends on impact and flight variables.
8. During the first quarter period, maximum positive displacement occurs at t=0, speed magnitude at pi/(2 omega), and positive elastic power at pi/(4 omega). Pe=k A² omega cos(omega t)sin(omega t). Force is maximal at a displacement turning point and zero at the speed maximum. Driving, damping, changing modal coupling and preload invalidate that free-oscillator timing, so a real shaft requires its forced dynamics.
9. Integrate F=k eta over loading displacement. Fixed eta gives U=k eta²/2; fixed static force gives U=F²/(2k). At k100/200, eta.1 gives .5/1J, while fixed F10 gives .5/.25J. A material substitution changes more than k unless geometry/mass/rigidity/damping are controlled. Fixed input, prescribed hand motion and reoptimized control are different estimands; changed hand motion can offset a relative kick-speed component.
10. Solve MV=MVc+mvb and vb−Vc=eV; square each final speed to obtain its kinetic-energy fraction. Their sum differs from one by m(1−e²)/(M+m). For the declared masses and V45, vb=66.9702761m/s and Vc=29.6202761m/s. Spin/off-center impact additionally needs inertias, contact geometry, friction/deformation and any support/shaft impulse coupling; a universal whole-body effective mass cannot be inserted without a matching model.
11. Sum external forces for COM acceleration and moments about COM for Hdot. A stationary contact has power F dot vcontact=0 while its moment about COM can be nonzero. Individual-foot normal/shear loads and free moments have different peak times. Muscle loads enter the segment equations and support feasibility, but cannot be read directly from one resultant ground-force trace.
12. Use Ef−Ei=Wact+Wext−D with signed actuator work and separately represented dissipation, retaining gravity/elastic storage consistently. Internal constraint powers cancel only in the combined system with matched point velocities. One segment can gain energy while another loses it. Net joint absorption does not resolve individual muscle work, biarticular transfer or metabolic heat.
13. The comparison needs matched delivery/feasibility, actual state-dependent muscle force/activation estimates and separate mechanical and metabolic measurements/models. High activation can coexist with low net torque through co-contraction; prior preparation has a cost. Robustness needs response/uncertainty measurements or a model with validated disturbance behavior. The affine decomposition alone supplies no faster/cheaper/more-consistent theorem.
14. The difference equation has norm growth bounded by L times the separation plus b; integrate the scalar comparison inequality from zero initial error. For L0 the bound is b delta t; otherwise it is b(exp(L delta t)−1)/L. The trajectories must remain in a common smooth valid region. A missed impact, changed contact mode or grazing event requires a different comparison and possibly discontinuous sensitivity.
15. Choose decision variables for initial state, an admissible input schedule and equipment parameters; minimize a stated delivery/effort/load/robustness cost subject to dynamics, state bounds, torque/activation constraints and feasible contacts. A small delivery vector normally leaves many decisions undetermined. Alternative minima and model mismatch require sensitivity analysis, held-out data and independent intervention validation before physical recommendations.
