### Part I: Errors Proved by Mathematics and Kinematics

1. **Incorrect Sign/Direction on Attack Angle Comparison**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"The same geometry tilts the face-center velocity slightly upward relative to the CG --- a shallower attack angle --- because the face center sits ahead of the CG on an arc that is turning upward through the bottom."`
   * *Inaccuracy:* Tilting velocity *upward* corresponds to a **steeper positive** (higher) attack angle, not a shallower one. Conversely, prior to low point (hitting down), being ahead on a downward arc would produce a *steeper negative* angle, while being past low point tilting upward increases positive attack angle. Describing an upward tilt as "a shallower attack angle" is kinematically contradictory.

2. **Oversimplified 2D Spin Vector Projection**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"$S_{\mathrm{side}} = S \sin\theta_{\mathrm{axis}}, \qquad S_{\mathrm{back}} = S \cos\theta_{\mathrm{axis}}, \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{axis}} = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left( S_{\mathrm{side}}, S_{\mathrm{back}}\right)$."`
   * *Inaccuracy:* This formulation implicitly assumes the ball's total spin vector lies strictly in a vertical plane transverse to the trajectory (zero rifle/gyro spin component along the velocity vector, $\omega_{\parallel} = 0$). If a ball has non-zero rifle spin, $S \neq \sqrt{S_{\mathrm{side}}^2 + S_{\mathrm{back}}^2}$, making the direct decomposition $S_{\mathrm{back}} = S\cos\theta_{\mathrm{axis}}$ mathematically incomplete without explicitly defining the projection onto the transverse plane.

3. **Inconsistent Definition vs. Table Notes on Face-to-Path Sign**
   * *Exact phrase:* In Table: `"Face to path | Face angle − club path | Sign controls curvature"` vs. Keypoint: `"And note that TrackMan's reported face-to-path is already a hybrid --- face orientation evaluated at the contact point minus velocity direction evaluated at the geometric center"`
   * *Inaccuracy:* For off-center hits on curved faces (bulge), if Face Angle is taken at the contact point and Club Path at the GC, Face-to-Path computed this way deviates from the effective local impact normal relative to the local impact velocity vector (gear-effect dynamic loft/spin-loft offset), creating a mathematical mismatch between the scalar difference $(FA - CP)$ and the actual curvature-inducing torque.

### Part II: Timing and Concrete Definitional Inaccuracies

4. **Contradiction in Club Speed Timing Definition**
   * *Exact phrase:* Intro: `"club-delivery parameters at the time of maximum compression"` vs. Table: `"Linear speed of the GC just prior to first contact"`
   * *Inaccuracy:* Club speed is explicitly defined in TrackMan's published standards as the speed immediately prior to first impact/contact (to avoid deceleration from ball collision), directly contradicting the introductory assertion that *all* club delivery parameters are defined at "maximum compression".

5. **Dynamic Lie Definition Conflates Impact Timing with Pose**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"Dynamic lie | Shaft angle vs. horizontal at impact"`
   * *Inaccuracy:* In TrackMan standard definitions, Dynamic Lie is defined specifically as the angle of the shaft relative to the horizontal ground plane at the moment of **maximum compression**, not generic "impact" (which starts $\approx 200\text{--}400\,\mu\text{s}$ prior to maximum compression).

### Part III: Reference-Point, Sign, and Kinematic Inferences

6. **Inversion of Arc Curvature Kinematics on Path Differential**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"Two rotations drive it, both pushing the same way horizontally: the swing arc curves leftward through impact, so a point displaced forward along the arc has its velocity rotated further around it; and face closure about the shaft axis swings a point ahead of the rotation center leftward."`
   * *Inaccuracy:* For a right-handed golfer swinging on an in-to-out or circular swing arc centered behind the player, displacement along the shaft axis vs. forward along the face normal requires careful sign definition. The face center sits *ahead* of the CG in the direction of the face normal (pointing toward the ball/target), not necessarily tangential along the swing arc. Furthermore, if the swing arc center is inside the player's turn, the position vector $\vect{r} = \vect{p}_{\mathrm{face}} - \vect{p}_{\mathrm{CG}}$ crossed with swing arc angular velocity $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathrm{swing}}$ and closure rate $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{shaft}}$ can oppose or reinforce depending on whether the clubhead has passed low point and the orientation of the shaft axis relative to the instantaneous center of curvature.

### Part IV: Measurement vs. Model & Manufacturer Claims Needing Primary Verification

7. **Manufacturer-Specific 3° Discrepancy Attribution**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"TrackMan quantifies the resulting gap directly: for a driver, the CG path and the face-center path differ by approximately 3°, with the face-center path being the more out-to-in of the two..."`
   * *Status:* **Manufacturer claim needing primary verification.** The exact magnitude ($\sim 3^\circ$) depends heavily on individual swing dynamics (closure rate $\omega_z$, swing radius $R$, delivery speed $v$). Citing this as an intrinsic constant or definitive TrackMan quantification requires verification against TrackMan's primary technical whitepaper documentation.

8. **Over-Categorization of Radar Face Angle and Dynamic Loft as Purely "D-Plane Inversion"**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"$^g$ D-plane inversion from ball launch + path on radar-only units; optically assisted on OERT-class hardware"`
   * *Status:* **Inference / vendor-fact inaccuracy.** While early or single-radar units derived face orientation via D-plane equations (assuming centered contact and zero gear effect), dual-radar tracking units (TrackMan 4) track phase/frequency shifts from head motion features and micro-Doppler signals directly to extract 3D face orientation prior to/during strike, rather than relying exclusively on an inverted ball-launch model.

9. **Classification of Camera Systems Measuring Club Path as Requiring Face Fiducials**
   * *Exact phrase:* `"$^f$ Requires face fiducials (Foresight/Garmin R50) or overhead geometry (Uneekor/ProTee)."`
   * *Status:* **Incomplete / manufacturer claim.** Multiple photometric and stereoscopic camera systems (including modern markerless optical engines and overhead systems) measure clubhead velocity vectors and delivery kinematics without requiring physical fiducial markers on the club face.

10. **Attribution of Closure Rate Exclusivity**
    * *Exact phrase:* `"Foresight GCQuad-class only"`
    * *Status:* **Inaccurate exclusivity claim.** High-speed multi-camera systems (such as high-end optical systems, studio stereoscopic rigs, and modern overhead launch monitors) also measure or report 3D face closure rates; it is not exclusive to Foresight GCQuad hardware.
