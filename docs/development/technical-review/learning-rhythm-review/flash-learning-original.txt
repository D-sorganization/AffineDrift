### 1. Mathematical & Modeling Inaccuracies

#### A. Single-Rate State-Space Formulation & Washout Discrepancy
* **Model Equation in Text/Code:**
  $$x_{k+1} = a x_k + b e_k, \quad e_k = p_k - x_k$$
  where $a = \text{retention}$, $b = \text{learning rate}$.
* **Inconsistency in Fig. \ref{fig:ch9:visuomotor-adaptation}:**
  - In the schematic TikZ figure, during **Washout** ($p_k = 0$), the error curve is drawn going *up* to an error of $+0.8$ (`-- (8, 0.8)`). 
  - **Derivation / Reality:** Under the sign convention $e_k = p_k - x_k$, when $p_k = 0$ and $x_k > 0$ (learned compensation for a positive rotation $+30^\circ$), the initial aftereffect error is:
    $$e_{\text{washout}, 0} = 0 - x_{\text{end}} = -x_{\text{end}} < 0$$
    The sign of the aftereffect must be **opposite** to the initial perturbation error ($+p$). In the TikZ diagram, the error is shown as positive throughout baseline, adaptation, and washout, and increases towards the end of washout, contradicting both the simulation and empirical reaching kinematics.
* **Steady-State Asymptote Calculation:**
  - In the code/text, when $p_k = p = \text{const}$, the steady-state compensation $x_\infty$ is:
    $$x_\infty = a x_\infty + b (p - x_\infty) \implies x_\infty = \frac{b}{1 - a + b} p$$
  - With default parameters $a = 0.98, b = 0.05$:
    $$x_\infty = \frac{0.05}{1 - 0.98 + 0.05} p = \frac{0.05}{0.07} p \approx 0.714 p$$
    The model leaves an asymptotic steady-state error of $\sim 28.6\%$. The text refers to "gradual asymptote" without explaining why standard linear state-space models cannot reach $100\%$ adaptation unless $a = 1.0$ (which contradicts forgetting).

#### B. Exercise 2 Claim: "Dual-rate model explains savings"
* **Theoretical Inaccuracy:** A linear dual-rate state-space model (Smith et al., 2006: $x = x_f + x_s$) **cannot produce savings** after complete washout under standard initial conditions ($x_f = 0, x_s = 0$). 
* **Mathematical Proof/Counterexample:** If an intermediate washout period brings both states back to 0 ($x_f \approx 0, x_s \approx 0$), re-exposure to $+p$ produces the exact identical linear differential/difference trajectory as initial exposure. 
* **Empirical Reality vs. Model:** True savings requires nonlinear mechanisms, such as:
  1. Multi-rate models with contextual switching / hidden state inference (e.g., Berniker & Kording, 2008).
  2. Memory of errors / meta-learning of learning rate $\beta(e)$ (Herzfeld et al., 2014).
  3. Explicit strategy re-aiming vs. implicit slow cerebellar adaptation (Taylor, Krakauer, & Ivry, 2014).
* **Actionable Fix:** Reframe Exercise 2 to ask students to *demonstrate spontaneous recovery* (which the linear dual-rate model *does* explain) rather than savings after full washout, or ask them to show analytically why the standard dual-rate model fails to generate savings without state memory.

### 2. Conceptual & Theoretical Inaccuracies

#### A. Misattribution of "Consolidation and Structural Learning"
* **Text Claim:** *"Consolidation and structural learning (cortical): repetition of a movement biases future movements toward repeated patterns... explains habit formation."*
* **Correction:** 
  - Movement repetition biasing future movement direction is termed **use-dependent plasticity (UDP)** (e.g., Classen et al., 1998; Diedrichsen et al., 2010), not "structural learning".
  - **Structural learning** (Braun, Aertsen, Wolpert, & Mehring, 2009) specifically refers to identifying the low-dimensional manifold / parameterized structure of task variations (e.g., discovering that perturbations are all rotations of varying angles, rather than arbitrary vector fields).
  - Conflating UDP/habit formation with structural learning creates major conceptual confusion between low-level motor cortex history bias and higher-level task-manifold extraction.

#### B. Implicit Adaptation vs. Explicit Cognitive Re-aiming
* Visuomotor rotation (VMR) is presented purely as a cerebellar error-based process.
* **Empirical Literature Status:** Over the last decade, VMR is widely proven to decompose into:
  1. **Explicit re-aiming** (prefrontal/cortical, cognitive, fast, accounts for early savings).
  2. **Implicit adaptation** (cerebellar sensory prediction error via forward model update; saturates at $\sim 15^\circ$–$20^\circ$ regardless of rotation size).
* Neglecting explicit re-aiming makes the savings and interference discussion incomplete.

### 3. Missing Assumptions & Boundary Conditions

1. **Stationarity of Learning Parameters:** The model assumes constant $a$ and $b$. In reality, cerebellar sensitivity $b$ downregulates when environmental volatility/noise is high and increases when errors are consistently signed (Kalman filter / Bayesian integration).
2. **Proprioceptive Drift & Clamped Feedback:** The state-space equation assumes error is simply $p_k - x_k$. In error-clamp trials ($e_k$ clamped regardless of $x_k$), implicit adaptation proceeds automatically, proving adaptation is driven by sensory prediction error (SPE), not task error (TE).
3. **Timescales:** Cerebellar LTD operates on hundreds of milliseconds to seconds within trials; trial-by-trial retention dynamics reflect population decay and memory consolidation.

### 5. Concrete Golf Swing Applications & Reasoning

Integrating golf-specific motor control reasoning elevates this chapter from generic reaching abstractions to the core domain of the textbook:

1. **Dynamic vs. Visuomotor Adaptation in Clubfitting:**
   - *Dynamics (Force field adaptation / Shadmehr):* Changing club swingweight, shaft stiffness profile (EI curve), or total club mass perturbs internal dynamic inverse models (Coriolis/centrifugal compensation during the downswing).
   - *Kinematic/Visuomotor (Krakauer):* Adjusting clubface visual alignment at address (offset vs. non-offset hosel) or playing in crosswinds creates visuomotor mapping recalibration.
2. **Use-Dependent Plasticity (UDP) & Range Practice ("The Range Trap"):**
   - Grooving identical swings off a flat mat induces cortical UDP (M1 tuning shift). However, because UDP does not enhance structural learning or generalized forward model flexibility, golfers experience severe performance drops on the course when encountering uneven lies or varied shot shapes.
3. **Dual-Rate Dynamics in Swing Overhauls & Reversion Under Stress:**
   - When a golfer learns a new swing mechanic (e.g., shallowing the shaft in transition), the **fast process** adapts via explicit cognitive cues.
   - Under competitive arousal or high time-pressure (downswing $< 300\text{ ms}$, exceeding voluntary feedback reaction time $\sim 150\text{ ms}$), the explicit/fast system disengages, revealing the entrenched **slow cerebellar baseline / cortical habitual pattern** (the golfer reverts to their old "over-the-top" move).
4. **Error-Augmentation in Coaching:**
   - Rather than correcting an open clubface, temporarily fitting an exaggerated slice-inducing club (error augmentation) magnifies sensory prediction errors, accelerating cerebellar adaptation via climbing fiber LTD.
