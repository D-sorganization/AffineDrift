### 2. Equation-by-Equation & Derivation Audit

#### A. State-Space & Kalman Recursion
$$\begin{aligned}
x_{k+1} &= A x_k + B u_k + w_k, & y_k &= H x_k + v_k \\
\hat{x}^-_k &= A \hat{x}^+_{k-1} + B u_{k-1}, & \hat{y}^-_k &= H \hat{x}^-_k
\end{aligned}$$
- **Indices & Timing**: 
  - If $x_{k+1} = A x_k + B u_k + w_k$, then propagating conditional expectation from $k-1$ to $k$ yields $\mathbb{E}[x_k \mid y_{1:k-1}] = A \hat{x}^+_{k-1} + B u_{k-1} = \hat{x}^-_k$. This matches standard discrete-time convention.
  - The observation at $k$ is $y_k = H x_k + v_k$, and prior prediction is $\hat{y}^-_k = H \hat{x}^-_k$. **Correct.**
- **Innovation & Gain (\eqref{eq:ch5:prediction-error})**:
  $$\nu_k = y_k - H \hat{x}^-_k, \qquad S_k = H P^-_k H^{\mathsf T} + R, \qquad K_k = P^-_k H^{\mathsf T} S_k^{-1}$$
  - Signs, matrix transposes, and inverses are exact.

#### B. Example 1: Precision vs. Gain
- **Parameters**: Scalar $y = x + v$, $H = 1$, $R = 1$.
  - Case 1: $P^- = 4$, $R = 1$.
    $$S = 1 \cdot 4 \cdot 1 + 1 = 5, \quad K = 4/5, \quad P^+ = (1 - K H)P^- = (1 - 4/5)\cdot 4 = 4/5.$$
    Measurement precision is $R^{-1} = 1^{-1} = 1$. Posterior variance is $4/5$.
  - Case 2: $P^- = 1/4$, $R = 1$.
    $$S = 1/4 + 1 = 5/4, \quad K = \frac{1/4}{5/4} = 1/5, \quad P^+ = (1 - 1/5)(1/4) = 1/5.$$
  - Values $K = 4/5$, $P^+ = 4/5$ and $K = 1/5$ are mathematically exact. The conclusion that precision $R^{-1}$ does not alone set $K$ is algebraically sound.

#### C. Variational Free Energy
$$F[q; y] = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(z) - \log p(y, z)] = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p(z \mid y)) - \log p(y)$$
- **Derivation**:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(z) - \log p(y, z)] &= \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(z) - \log p(z \mid y) - \log p(y)] \\
  &= \int q(z) \log \frac{q(z)}{p(z \mid y)} \, dz - \log p(y) \int q(z) \, dz \\
  &= D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p(z \mid y)) - \log p(y).
  \end{aligned}$$
  - Signs: $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p) \ge 0$, so $F[q; y] \ge -\log p(y)$ (evidence lower bound is $-F$). The definition and sign identity are exact.

#### D. Model Predictive Control (\eqref{eq:ch5:active-inference})
$$\min_{u_{0:N-1}} \Phi(x_N) + \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \left[ \|h(x_j) - r_{k+j}\|_W^2 + \|u_j\|_U^2 \right]$$
- **Index & Time Horizon**:
  - The initial condition states $x_0 = \hat{x}_k$.
  - The step in the horizon is $j$, running $0 \dots N-1$. At $j$, the tracking target is correctly aligned to actual time $k+j$ via $r_{k+j}$.
  - The terminal cost $\Phi(x_N)$ penalizes state at prediction horizon step $N$ ($k+N$).
  - **Defect/Ambiguity**: $x_j$ reuses the symbol $x$ from the plant state equation $x_{k+1} = A x_k + \dots$. In MPC literature, this is typically denoted $x_{j|k}$ or $\hat{x}_{j|k}$ to distinguish the internal open-loop predicted trajectory along the prediction horizon from the true closed-loop state trajectory $x_k$. While standard shorthand in brief textbook summaries, explicitly noting $x_j$ as predicted horizon state avoids confusing $x_0$ with the initial plant state at time 0.

#### E. Example 2: Tracking Objective vs. Prediction Matching
- Problem: $x_1 = x_0 + u$, $x_0 = 0 \implies x_1 = u$. $y_1 = x_1 = u$, desired $r = 1$.
- Perfect predictor: $\hat{y}_1(u) = u$.
- Case 1: Objective $J_1(u) = (y_1 - \hat{y}_1)^2 + \lambda u^2 = (u - u)^2 + \lambda u^2 = \lambda u^2$.
  - For $\lambda > 0$, $\min_u \lambda u^2 \implies u^* = 0$.
- Case 2: Tracking objective $J_2(u) = (u - r)^2 + \lambda u^2 = (u - 1)^2 + \lambda u^2$.
  - Derivative: $2(u - 1) + 2\lambda u = 0 \implies u(1 + \lambda) = 1 \implies u^* = \frac{1}{1 + \lambda}$.
  - For $\lambda = 1$: $u^* = 1/2$.
- The counterexample is sound and formally isolates the difference between sensory prediction error and task error.
