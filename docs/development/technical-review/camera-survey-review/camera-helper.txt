### 1. Concrete Mathematical Errors and Ambiguities

1. **Center-Finding Bias Scale and Units (Section 2.1)**
   * **Definite Error / Dimensional Inconsistency:** The text states: *“fitting the projected conic outline rather than taking the blob centroid avoids a systematic offset of up to $RZ/f$ pixels.”*
   * *Analysis:* Under pinhole projection with focal length $f$ (in pixels or length units compatible with sensor coordinates), the perspective projection of a sphere of radius $R$ at depth $Z$ scales inversely with depth, roughly as $f \frac{R}{Z}$. The factor $RZ/f$ has dimension $[L]^2 / [L] = [L]$ (not pixels if $f$ is in pixels; or if $f$ is focal length in meters and pixel pitch is involved, it scales directly with $Z$, which is upside down). The displacement between the projection of the sphere center and the centroid of the projected elliptical silhouette along an off-axis angle $\alpha$ scales asymptotically with $\sim f \tan(\alpha) \cdot (R/Z)^2$, which **decreases** with depth $Z$ rather than increasing proportionally with $Z$.

2. **Rotational Angle and Spin Rate Formulation (Equation \ref{eq:rotangle})**
   * **Ambiguity / Under-Specification:**
     $$\hat{\vect{\omega}} = \mathrm{eig}_1(R), \qquad \theta = \arccos\!\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,R - 1}{2}\Bigr), \qquad \omega = \theta/\Delta t$$
     * $\mathrm{eig}_1(R)$ is undefined up to sign ($\pm \hat{\vect{\omega}}$). Without establishing orientation or an axial sign convention tied to the axis-angle representation (e.g., via the skew-symmetric matrix $\log(R) = \theta [\hat{\vect{\omega}}]_\times$ or the cross-product of matched vectors), $\hat{\vect{\omega}}$ has a two-fold sign ambiguity.
     * $\theta$ defined via standard $\arccos$ maps strictly to $[0, \pi]$. Direction of rotation (clockwise vs. counter-clockwise around the unit vector) is lost unless signed consistency with $\hat{\vect{\omega}}$ is enforced.

3. **DLT Formulation for Matched Points (Section 2.1)**
   * **Ambiguity:** The text notes solving *“the DLT system $\tilde{\vect{x}}\times(P\vect{X})=0$”*.
   * While standard Hartley & Zisserman notation for 3D point triangulation, for a sphere, $\tilde{\vect{x}}$ is an image point whose ray is tangent to the sphere silhouette, not the projection of the 3D center $\vect{X}$. Triangulating matched boundary points as if they were fixed physical surface points introduces triangulation bias, because the apparent silhouette contour generator on a moving sphere varies with viewpoint.

### 2. Conditional Statements Needing Assumptions & Identifiability Constraints

1. **Planar Face Pose from 4 Fiducials (Section 3.1)**
   * **Missing Assumptions:** The text asserts that *“four dots — placed on the vertical centerline, equidistant from the horizontal centerline — define the face plane and enable face angle, dynamic loft, lie...”*
   * *Collinearity / Planarity Degeneracy:* If four dots are placed strictly along the *vertical centerline*, they are collinear. A line of points cannot define a 6-DOF pose or a unique plane (it has an unconstrained degree of freedom around the line). If the author intended a cross or quadrilateral configuration (e.g., two on the vertical centerline, two on the horizontal line, or a 2D quad layout), the geometry must explicitly define non-collinear points with non-degenerate aspect ratio.
   * *Clubface Curvature (Bulge and Roll):* Drivers and woods have spherical/cylindrical bulge and roll. Defining the “face plane” using planar triangulation on a curved surface requires defining the reference tangent plane or specific fiducial offset corrections.

2. **Dimple-Pattern Registration on a Translating/Rotating Sphere (Section 2.2)**
   * **Missing Assumptions:**
     * *Decoupling translation and rotation:* Texture back-projection onto a unit sphere requires the 3D center $\vect{X}(t_k)$ to be determined *prior to* or *jointly with* $R$. Any residual error in the ball center estimate propagates into artificial surface distortion and false rotational shifts.
     * *Surface visibility / Orthographic vs. Perspective Back-Projection:* Dimples near the occluding limb undergo extreme perspective foreshortening and non-Lambertian specular shifts under IR strobes. Spherical cross-correlation requires masking out grazing-angle regions or explicit Lambertian photometric assumptions.
     * *Symmetry and Repeating Patterns:* Golf ball dimple patterns exhibit icosahedral, octahedral, or chiral regular tiling symmetries. Without distinct markings, logos, or seam identification, cross-correlation over small angular baselines is prone to local periodic minima, not just $2\pi$ aliasing.

3. **Single-Camera Multi-Exposure Identifiability in PiTrac (Section 5)**
   * **Ambiguity / Under-Specified Constraints:** The description states that in PiTrac, *“one camera watches the teed ball... the second captures the strobed flight images... 3D spin on all three axes is solved by searching candidate rotations that best register the dimple imagery between exposures.”*
   * *Out-of-Plane Pose Identifiability:* A single camera observing a strobed sequence provides only 2D projections of a rotating sphere. Estimating full 3D rotation ($SO(3)$) from monocular 2D images is ill-conditioned for out-of-plane spin components unless severe scale/perspective changes or spherical texture wrap are tracked with high SNR. Furthermore, if the ball translates between strobe pulses, the monocular scale/depth $Z$ is weakly constrained without relying solely on known ball diameter ($R = 21.34\,\text{mm}$).
