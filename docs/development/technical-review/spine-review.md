## Original Content and Edition Differences

The print chapter mislabels gross regional ranges as per-level ranges and suggests multiplying them by the number of levels. The web table has partly corrected magnitudes, but later bullets still claim 20–30 degrees of thoracic rotation per level and 40–45 degrees at each cervical level. Both confuse vertebra counts with articulations and place discs at all levels. The epigraph's anonymous attribution is unsupported. Both equate hip flexion with lumbar flexion, describe an anterior/right/superior left-handed frame while using right-handed rotation matrices, swap flexion and side-bending axes, and conflate moving-axis and fixed-axis composition. The print rotation expansion omits phi; the web expansion includes phi but the surrounding claims about axes and every matrix entry remain wrong.

Both editions infer tissue loading from aggregate X-factor, claim one biomechanically correct Euler sequence, and conflate muscle redundancy with coordinate ambiguity. Cervical motion cannot supply a relative rotation between pelvis and thorax when the neck is outside that kinematic path. A torso rigidly including the pelvis cannot simultaneously represent a pelvis–thorax relative rotation. Higher model detail is not a guaranteed accuracy or clinical-validity ladder.

The scalar spring/damping equations omit coupled inertia and rate terms. The torque expression called a drift field has torque units, omits state velocity and inverse inertia, and inconsistently treats active co-contraction as passive. The web correctly writes the series-compliance formula and then incorrectly says torque increases with more series segments at fixed total angle. Variable stiffness is said to concentrate existing energy without accounting for actuator work. Simultaneous acceleration is said necessarily to dissipate stored energy, which does not follow from a spring/damper balance.

The text adds normal and shear stress as scalars, gives universal disc tolerance/failure numbers normalized by body weight, declares a universally most dangerous transition, and supplies unsupported accuracy percentages and physiological prescriptions. Web qualifiers like illustrative do not cure unsupported quantitative thresholds or the claimed injury mechanism. All eight exercises need reconciliation: unit-correct mean traction versus tissue stress; finite rotation products instead of inflated angle sums; series compliance; signed gravity/elastic moments; a testing protocol instead of an injury-proof swing; pressure plus abdominal muscle forces; frame decomposition versus physical strategy; and coupled multibody dynamics.

## Derivations and Editorial Decisions

The replacement has one connected argument: segment motion constrains a resultant only when inertia and external loads are known; muscle recruitment and passive/contact assumptions partition that resultant; local geometry, material law and history determine tissue response; clinical outcomes need separate evidence. This supports the user's golf/humanoid-modeling objective without treating a segment-speed sequence, X-factor or net joint moment as a direct measure of tissue load. The figure follows that inference chain, replacing the original schematic without pretending to depict patient anatomy.

The right-handed frame is anterior/left/superior. For active column-vector rotations R=Rz(alpha)Ry(beta)Rx(gamma), gamma–beta–alpha is a fixed-axis x–y–z sequence. The reversed moving-axis interpretation is equivalent. Rdot gives body omega through E, whose columns are the transported elementary axes. Its determinant is cos(beta), so the chart loses rank at beta=±pi/2 while physical orientation remains regular. Conjugate generalized load is E-transpose times body moment from power equality. The distinction between chart coupling, physical compliance coupling and muscle redundancy is carried through the prose.

The dynamics use the full mass matrix, inertial velocity terms, passive potentials, active loads, damping and contact multipliers. Generalized coordinates are independent before contact reduction; quaternion formulations need qdot=Nv. The drift equation includes the velocity component and inverse mass matrix. Input means declared torque or excitation; zero excitation does not delete activation state. Contact must be solved consistently in the counterfactual. Changing the reference input changes the drift/input split, so co-contraction cannot be assigned to drift to manufacture a passive percentage.

The circular-beam example deliberately stops at stress components. Integrating sigma_zz gives -N, integrating y*sigma_zz gives Mx, and integrating -x*sigma_zz gives My. Torsional shear integrates to T about z. These signs depend on the positive-z face and declared right-handed axes. An annular material model needs more than a homogeneous-beam section or an isotropic equivalent stress. The examples therefore have no safety interpretation.

Series compliance holds only for coaxial, quasi-static linear springs without intermediate moments. More springs reduce the moment at fixed total angle but increase total deflection at fixed moment. The narrative now states both comparisons. For a tension-only ligament, integrate F=k(L-L0)^2 over the stretched branch to obtain U=k(L-L0)^3/3 and use Q=-F*dL/dq. The zero branch includes equality, preserving continuity and dimensioned definitions. Controlled spring energy contains both one-half kdot*delta² and -k*delta*theta0dot. Those terms explain how changing stiffness/equilibrium can change stored energy even with zero joint velocity.

Pressure forces are evaluated on a stated subsystem. The divergence theorem makes the uniform-pressure force on a closed surface vanish; the corresponding moment vanishes because the integral of r_i*n_j is proportional to the symmetric identity tensor. A selected surface need not have zero force. Neither statement determines spinal unloading without the abdominal wall and muscle equilibrium. The clinical studies are used only within their design/read boundaries, with no breathing prescription.

All nine article metadata records were retrieved from publisher-deposited Crossref entries and compared with the primary abstracts already read. For Adams/Hutton the Crossref date is March1983 with no pages, whereas PubMed gives the issue citation1982;6(4):249–253. The bibliography follows that explicit PubMed issue citation and records the discrepancy here. OpenStax uses its verified2022 second-edition metadata and a section-specific URL. No new clinical paper is represented as fully read merely because metadata was retrieved. Lim's author-university repository independently confirms the abstract and citation.

## Complete Exercise Solutions and Interpretation

1. **Joint Count.** Seven included bodies form six adjacent interfaces: T12–L1, L1–L2, L2–L3, L3–L4, L4–L5 and L5–S1. A fixed sacrum and six ideal three-rotation joints give18 configuration dimensions. Six independent relative coordinates at each interface give36. These are model choices; stiffness/contact constrain response and no implication of six equally free anatomical directions follows. A quaternion implementation may store more numbers than the configuration dimension.
2. **Tilted Spin.** Multiplying Ry(phi)-transpose by Omega*ez gives Omega*(-sin(phi),0,cos(phi)). At30degrees and4rad/s this is(-2,0,2sqrt(3))rad/s. Its squared norm is4+12=16. A nonzero local x component follows from basis projection and contains no information about the compliance matrix without a load–response experiment.
3. **Euler Kinematics and Power.** In R-transpose Rdot, the gamma contribution is ex, the beta contribution is Rx(-gamma)ey, and the alpha contribution is Rx(-gamma)Ry(-beta)ez. Those are the displayed E columns. At gamma=.3,beta=-.4,alpha=.7 with rates(.6,-.8,1.1), numerical differentiation of SciPy's extrinsic xyz product agrees with E*rates to2e-10. For body moment(2,-3,4), generalized power agrees to1e-9. At beta=pi/2 two columns become dependent; recovering unique Euler rates fails. R and physical omega remain meaningful.
4. **Finite Rotation Composition.** Rx(30deg)Ry(20deg)ez=(.3420201433,-.4698463104,.8137976813). Reversal gives(.2961981327,-.5,.8137976813). Their separation norm is about.05486; they are different orientations of the vector despite identical elementary angle magnitudes. Regional maxima can also refer to incompatible loads and postures. Neither noncommuting rotations nor incompatible maxima justify a scalar anatomical capacity sum.
5. **Load and Mean Traction.** N=8*75*9.81=5886N. A=1800mm² yields3.27N/mm²=3.27MPa. Reducing area20% increases the quotient25%, to4.0875MPa. Local stress needs geometry, load partition, moment/shear, contact/material laws and history. Clinical inference further needs participant/exposure/outcome evidence. Body-weight normalization is not a strength law.
6. **Stress Components.** Eigenvalues are -1.5±sqrt(3.25)MPa, approximately.3027756377 and-3.3027756377MPa. The scalar sum sigma+s=-2MPa is neither principal stress. This homogeneous two-dimensional illustration excludes annular anisotropy, hydration, spatial heterogeneity and damage history, so it is not a tissue failure criterion.
7. **Series Compliance.** Each stiffness converts to12*180/pi Nm/rad. For five springs, keq=2.4Nm/degree, torque48Nm, each angle4deg, total U=8pi/3≈8.37758041J. Ten at the same20deg give1.2Nm/degree,24Nm and4pi/3≈4.18879020J. Ten at48Nm give40deg and16pi/3≈16.75516082J. The equal-moment assumption requires negligible intersegmental acceleration and no intermediate applied moments; bridged muscle/ligament paths and facet engagement need a different network model.
8. **Ligament Geometry.** Stretch=.002m. F=80N; dF/dL=2k*.002=80000N/m; U=(2e7/3)*(.002)^3=.0533333333J. Q=-80*.010=-.8Nm (angle in radians). The sign resists increasing length. These teaching numbers are not fitted human tissue parameters. Both F and U vanish continuously at L=L0.
9. **Controlled Stiffness Work.** DeltaU=.5*(200-100)*.10²=.5J. Joint velocity and ordinary joint torque power are zero during the hold, but the parameter-changing mechanism supplies .5J through the kdot term. Equilibrium-position changes would add the separate -k*delta*theta0dot term. A joint mechanical balance does not determine metabolic cost.
10. **Signed Moments.** With angle from downward vertical, gravity is-10*9.81*.30*.5=-14.715Nm and elasticity is-20*pi/6=-10.471975512Nm. Total=-25.186975512Nm. At+1rad/s the total power is-25.186975512W: kinetic energy is being transferred into the two potentials in this isolated illustration. At-1rad/s the sign reverses and those potentials release energy. Additional applied loads/damping would change the full balance.
11. **Muscle Redundancy.** Initial torque=.05*(300-100)=10Nm and compression contribution=.8*(300+100)=320N. Adding150N to each leaves10Nm but increases compression to560N. Motion/net-moment measurements alone do not distinguish the two recruitment patterns under these idealized assumptions. This is a compressive muscle contribution, not the complete contact force.
12. **Pressure and Subsystems.** The planar surface contribution is5000Pa*.10m²=500N. Opposite faces of a uniform-pressure closed box cancel, and the divergence theorem generalizes that cancellation. Forces on only the diaphragm, pelvic floor or abdominal wall can be nonzero. The required wall tensions and muscles alter the spinal free-body balance, so subtracting500N from a previous compression estimate is not justified.
13. **Coupled Acceleration.** M-inverse=(1/3)[[2,-1],[-1,2]], giving(2/3,-1/3)rad/s². The second coordinate accelerates through inertial coupling even though its direct torque is zero. Under fixed contact, solve the multiplier and acceleration with J*qdd+Jdot*qdot=0; using the unconstrained inverse can violate the contact. No body trajectory over a finite interval is established by one instantaneous calculation.
14. **Peak Definitions.** The average history is(2,4,4)BW with peak4BW. Each individual peak is6BW, so mean individual peak is6BW. The discrepancy reflects timing, not conflicting measurements. Injury-phase claims additionally need concurrent loading, repeated exposure, population/outcome definitions and appropriate causal evidence. A chosen normalized force maximum alone supplies none of those.
15. **Testable Comparison.** Define the matched club-delivery outputs and tolerances; measure calibrated pelvis/thorax/limb poses, ground/club loads and timing, with EMG if used. Declare torque versus excitation inputs, activation/elastic initial state and contact modes. Fit relevant parameters on calibration data, evaluate independent trials and perform sensitivity over recruitment, stiffness, geometry and measurement uncertainty. Validate only against observable outputs actually measured; label internal contact/stress estimates conditional. If another plausible recruitment model removes the predicted reduction, report model dependence instead of claiming an established lower-load or injury-preventing technique.
