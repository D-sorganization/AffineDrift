## Findings and Derivations

The original repeatedly equated an affine decomposition with physical causal identification, passive drift with historical inevitability, and torque removal with releasing all muscular effort. These are different concepts. The replacement fixes the declared intervention: under u=alpha(x)+beta(x)w, the zero-w drift becomes f+G alpha. Existing activation, elastic state, prescribed supports and remaining controls must be specified. Pointwise evaluation, a stitched sequence of evaluations, one forward branch and branches from different actual states remain distinct. The exact same-state difference is G(x)u, while the forward separation obeys Delta_dot=f(x)-f(x0)+G(x)u. Mechanical branches have velocity separation B_a u h+O(h²) and position separation B_a u h²/2+O(h³); this short-time geometry explains why a small position gap is not proof of a negligible actuator effect.

Local Lipschitz regularity yields local uniqueness, not global completeness. Smooth xdot=x² has finite escape at 1/x_initial. For C^k, k>=1, the initial-state flow is C^k, whereas trajectories are C^(k+1) in time. Flow composition/inversion hold where defined; global groups need completeness. The STM is ordered propagation along a trajectory, not an exponential of an averaged Jacobian or a direct reading of its instantaneous eigenvalues. Hybrid contacts and constrained resets require separate treatment.

The mechanical convention is M vdot+C v+grad V+d=B u, with positive grad V on the left and applied gravity negative. ZVCF at zero input is -M^-1 grad V only under the declared natural model and zero velocity-dependent terms at rest. A control-preserved reset retains M^-1 B u; the original omitted B in this distinction. Full drift minus zero-velocity drift also contains damping and any other velocity dependence. Zero velocity is not equilibrium. For A vdot=-b, eliminate lambda from M vdot=B u-h+A^T lambda: Lambda=(AM^-1A^T)^-1, P=I-M^-1A^T Lambda A, a0c=-P M^-1h-M^-1A^T Lambda b and Bac=P M^-1B. Moving constraints require A v+phi_t=0, so zero velocity can be inadmissible. Fixed reduced coordinates can hide physical constraints; golf is not automatically unconstrained during the downswing.

A DCR must declare acceleration coordinates/weights, feasible input set, and whether the denominator is capacity, realized input or one component. A maximum capacity norm is not directional authority. Zero capacity is reported explicitly (infinite with nonzero drift, undefined when both vanish). The fixed-direction Christoffel bias is homogeneous quadratic, but its coefficient may vanish: an O(s²) upper bound is not a nonzero lower bound. The single pendulum has no quadratic angular bias at any speed while radial Cartesian acceleration grows. Units differ between angular omega² and Cartesian speed²/length. The orthogonal example f=(1000,0), G=(0,1), y=x2 has large drift norm and intact task authority. Directional support max ell^T Bac u and cancellation residual min||a0+Bac u|| answer better posed questions. For y=h(q), yddot=Dh vdot+D²h[v,v], which separates task kinematics from generalized acceleration.

For a prescribed nominal input, A=Df+sum u_j DG_j and delta x(tf)=integral Phi(tf,s)G(s)delta u(s) ds. Feedback adds G Dpi. A bounded input changed at one isolated time has zero ODE effect; a pulse has duration and an impulse is a distinct idealization. Input and output weights are needed before singular-value rankings; an L² Gramian does not directly impose peak torque bounds. A Lipschitz/absolute-input bound gives ||Delta x||<=epsilon(exp(Lh)-1)/L, with epsilon h at L=0. Large DCR supplies neither small epsilon nor a small propagator. For a transversal guard psi(x,t)=0, delta te=-psi_x delta x/(psi_t+psi_x F), delta xe=delta x+F delta te. Output/reset derivatives and changes in event sequence must be included. The free crossing q=vt, q=1 demonstrates nonzero fixed-time position sensitivity but zero position sensitivity at the event, with time derivative -1/v².

For fixed ideal supports, Edot=u^T B^T v-D. ZTCF energy is constant only with no other power and no dissipation; passive damping yields nonincrease, not strict negativity at rest. Actual-minus-zero E equals actual signed input work minus the difference of branch dissipation integrals (plus any boundary-power difference). Kinetic energy also requires -Delta V. Constant positive torque can do negative net work; work is not metabolic effort. Interface wrench power requires each body's interface velocity. Off-diagonal inertia cannot establish which body receives energy, and prescribed torso motion needs its enforcing reaction and power. Parameter sensitivity uses the whole field Sdot=Fx S+Fp with initial and policy derivatives, rather than differentiating only the same-state acceleration. Gravity or mass changes do not give trajectory superposition and do not destroy input affinity merely by altering coefficients.

Identification is relative to the model library, rank, conditioning and noise assumptions. With only u=pi(x), alternatives f_tilde=f+H pi and G_tilde=G-H fit the same observed dynamics but imply different zero-input branches. Exact state repetition is sufficient in some designs, not necessary for all finite-parameter regressions. Independent position noise produces forward-difference variance 2 sigma²/dt². Shared inverse-dynamics/kinematic errors and feedback correlation matter. The chapter distinguishes sparse nonlinear identification, linear ARX/state-space/DMD representations and reduction of an existing model. Validation of observed trajectories alone does not establish an unobserved intervention.

The contraction proof now uses a uniform Sym(Df)<=-alpha I bound on a convex forward-invariant domain and the exact integral mean-value identity, avoiding a wrongly discarded Taylor remainder. General metrics need Pdot+J^TP+PJ and appropriate uniform bounds/path conditions. xdot=-x³ on x>0 has pointwise negative derivative but algebraic decay. J=[-1,10;0,-1] is Hurwitz with Sym(J) maximum eigenvalue 4 and transient amplification. Hamiltonian phase-volume preservation rules out uniform full-state contraction in a uniformly equivalent metric on a regular persistent region, but not transient directional narrowing. Small-feedback robustness needs an equilibrium, derivative margin and retained domain; it does not guarantee reaching any chosen target.

The original minimal-intervention theorem was false. HJB minimization gives u*=-R^-1G^T grad(value)/2 under smooth unconstrained conditions, and constrained minimization/projected input otherwise. Large ||f|| does not force the value gradient small. In scalar LQR xdot=a x+u with integral(x²+u²), P=a+sqrt(a²+1) gives K=20.0498756 for a=10 and K=.0498756 for a=-10. Unstable drift needs strong feedback; favorable stable drift reduces effort only in this defined problem. The unconstrained example is explicitly not an assertion of bounded-input feasibility: for a>0, x>umax/a cannot return to zero under |u|<=umax. Even stable xdot=-x+u needs u=1 to hold x=1.

## Exercise Solutions and Review Decisions

1. Duffing: zero-input E=v²/2+z⁴/4=.25. With u=-2z, E+z²=1.25 is conserved, so |z|<=1 and the chosen capacity3 exceeds the realized maximum2. Zero/input branch accelerations initially -1/-3. Capacity ratio |z|³/3 differs from realized ratio |z|²/2 except at zeros where the literal ratio is undefined. Zero net signed work can coexist with different phase/frequency and nonzero control.
2. Zero-input ZVCF=-1 and control-preserved ZVCF=-3 initially; the full feedback acceleration is -3. There is no gravitational term in this nondimensional oscillator. The constraint q-t=0 requires v=1, so a v=0 reset is inadmissible without changing or impulsively re-establishing the constraint.
3. At fixed q,w, a_cor(s w)=s² a_cor(w). With nonzero coefficient and fixed positive finite capacity, rho/s² tends to ||a_cor(w)||W/cW when other terms are lower order. For delta=0 the two-link sine coefficient vanishes even at high speed; for a single pendulum angular C is identically zero. A component coefficient can vanish when the other component does not.
4. Kinetic-energy balance subtracts potential change and dissipation. Actual-minus-zero total energy subtracts the difference of dissipation. For an ideal one-dimensional free mass initially at rest, apply +a for h then -a for h: final velocity and net kinetic work return to zero, but position changes by a h². Thus a nonzero zero-net-work input changes the path. Include the boundary force enforcing any prescribed motion.
5. Regressors [sin q,v,u]. If u=k sin q, columns1 and3 are dependent; only theta1+k theta3 is identified. Inputs that vary independently of those columns can restore rank, but noisy differentiated data create errors in regressors and targets. Holdout policy/state coverage, singular values and a stated noise model are required before interpreting the fit as a causal torque decomposition.
6. Hanging conservative linearization has Hamiltonian oscillatory eigenpairs rather than dissipative convergence. In canonical variables trace(J)=0 and det(Phi)=1. For the nonnormal J=[-1,10;0,-1], P=diag(1,100) gives J^TP+PJ=[[-2,10],[10,-200]], with negative trace and determinant300>0, hence negative-definite. It contracts in that weighted metric while allowing Euclidean transient growth. Damped linearization can be Hurwitz; its Lyapunov metric must be solved/checked rather than assumed to equal M.
7. Pulse response is the integral of Phi G over its nonzero duration. Finite differences in amplitude converge to that sensitivity while duration remains declared. Angle event conditioning adds delta te and changes state/output derivatives; require nonzero guard velocity and the same event branch. A scalar actuator has only signed directions; multi-input rankings require selected weights and admissibility. A single-point change is measure zero; an impulse changes momentum under its impulse law.
8. No numerical winner is determined by the original incomplete three-link problem. A complete solution must supply geometry/mass, forces, state/trajectory and supports; compute body energy derivatives and interface wrench powers; and account for the prescribed torso's enforcing reaction. M off-diagonal entries encode kinetic coupling in chosen coordinates, not a unique energy-transfer quantity. This exercise deliberately teaches how to close the modeling problem rather than inventing a proximal-to-distal energy percentage.
