## Conceptual Corrections

Distinguish inertial coupling, muscle force transmission, holonomic constraints and physiological state evolution. Derive Rq=-D(length)^T, Rv=N^T Rq and skeletal power=-tension dot path-length rate. A compliant biarticular muscle does not eliminate a kinematic freedom. Opposite joint-coordinate powers do not by themselves identify physical segment energy transport or zero muscle work/metabolism. Generalized speed coordinates avoid quaternion dimension confusion. Ground/hand boundary wrenches, independent passive loads and muscle loads are kept distinct.

Stiffness is a specified derivative: restoring K=-dQ/dq equals R KMT R^T minus the tension-weighted derivatives of moment-arm columns in the declared quasistatic model. It is not a universal short-range fiber stiffness or all of impedance. Muscle/tendon series reduction is stated only for aligned nonpennate linear springs. Exercises supply inertias, moment arms and force slopes sufficient to reproduce the numerical examples.

Inverse dynamics subtracts known external loads from inertial/gravity demand; the minus sign is correct. Floating-base residuals indicate inconsistency, not internal actuation. Feasible nonnegative force allocations are an image of a force box/cone, and an underdetermined linear system can still be infeasible or have a unique boundary solution. The activation objective explicitly declares its force approximation and passive-force accounting. CMC first forms acceleration targets, then allocates controls and integrates states; tracking is not neural-command identification.
