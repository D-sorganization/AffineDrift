### 1. Mass Distribution Comparison: $M(q)$ vs. `_club_kinetic_energy`

A fundamental physical and mathematical inconsistency exists between the distal link inertia models in `mass_matrix` ($M$) and `_club_kinetic_energy`:

* **`mass_matrix` ($M$) Model:**
  * Uses an effective point mass $m_e = \_m2eff(\text{params}) = m_2 + m_{\text{Club}}$ placed entirely at the **tip** of the distal rod (distance $L_2$ from the wrist pivot).
  * Explicitly docstring-certified: `"Clubhead is modeled as a point mass at the tip of the shaft."`
  * Distal moment of inertia about the wrist joint is:
    $$I_{\text{wrist}, M} = m_e L_2^2 = (m_2 + m_{\text{Club}}) L_2^2$$
  * Distal kinetic energy contribution when proximal arm is stationary ($\dot{\theta}_1 = 0$, $\dot{\phi} \neq 0$):
    $$T_{\text{distal}, M} = \frac{1}{2} M_{22} \dot{\phi}^2 = \frac{1}{2} (m_2 + m_{\text{Club}}) L_2^2 \dot{\phi}^2$$

* **`_club_kinetic_energy` Model:**
  * Treats the shaft as a **uniform distributed rod** of mass $m_2$ and length $L_2$ (center of mass at $\frac{1}{2}L_2$, rotational inertia about COM $I_{\text{COM}} = \frac{1}{12} m_2 L_2^2$), plus a point clubhead of mass $m_{\text{Club}}$ at the tip ($L_2$).
  * Distal moment of inertia about the wrist joint by parallel-axis theorem:
    $$I_{\text{wrist}, \text{club}} = \left(\frac{1}{12} m_2 L_2^2 + m_2 \left(\frac{L_2}{2}\right)^2\right) + m_{\text{Club}} L_2^2 = \frac{1}{3} m_2 L_2^2 + m_{\text{Club}} L_2^2$$
  * Distal kinetic energy contribution when $\dot{\theta}_1 = 0$:
    $$T_{\text{distal}, \text{club}} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{1}{3} m_2 + m_{\text{Club}}\right) L_2^2 \dot{\phi}^2$$

* **Discrepancy:**
  The coefficient on $m_2 L_2^2$ differs by a factor of 3 ($1.0$ in $M$ vs. $\frac{1}{3}$ in `_club_kinetic_energy`):
  $$I_{\text{wrist}, M} - I_{\text{wrist}, \text{club}} = \frac{2}{3} m_2 L_2^2$$

### 2. Minimal Numeric Counterexample Isolating Wrist Angular Velocity

Let the shoulder be stationary and evaluate pure wrist rotation with strictly positive parameters:

* **Parameters:**
  * $m_1 = 1.0\text{ kg}$, $L_1 = 1.0\text{ m}$
  * $m_2 = 3.0\text{ kg}$, $m_{\text{Club}} = 1.0\text{ kg} \implies m_e = 4.0\text{ kg}$
  * $L_2 = 1.0\text{ m}$
* **Kinematic State:**
  * $\theta_1 = 0\text{ rad}$, $\phi = 0\text{ rad}$
  * Proximal velocity $\dot{\theta}_1 = 0\text{ rad/s}$
  * Wrist angular velocity $\dot{\phi} = 2.0\text{ rad/s} \implies \text{club\_rate} = 2.0\text{ rad/s}$

#### Computation via `mass_matrix` ($M$):
$$M_{22} = m_e L_2^2 = (3.0 + 1.0)(1.0)^2 = 4.0\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$$
$$T_{\text{distal}, M} = \frac{1}{2} \dot{q}^T M \dot{q} = \frac{1}{2} M_{22} \dot{\phi}^2 = \frac{1}{2} (4.0)(2.0)^2 = 8.0\text{ J}$$

#### Computation via `_club_kinetic_energy`:
* Wrist velocity: $v_w = [0, 0]$
* Shaft COM velocity: $v_{\text{shaft, COM}} = 0 + 0.5 (1.0)(2.0) [1, 0] = [1.0, 0]\text{ m/s}$
* Shaft inertia about COM: $I_{\text{shaft}} = \frac{1}{12}(3.0)(1.0)^2 = 0.25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$
* Shaft kinetic energy:
  $$E_{\text{shaft}} = \frac{1}{2}(3.0)(1.0)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(0.25)(2.0)^2 = 1.5 + 0.5 = 2.0\text{ J}$$
  *(Equivalently $\frac{1}{2} I_{\text{wrist}} \omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} (\frac{1}{3} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 1^2)(2^2) = 2.0\text{ J}$)*
* Tip velocity: $v_{\text{tip}} = 0 + (1.0)(2.0)[1, 0] = [2.0, 0]\text{ m/s}$
* Head kinetic energy:
  $$E_{\text{head}} = \frac{1}{2}(1.0)(2.0)^2 = 2.0\text{ J}$$
* Total distal kinetic energy:
  $$T_{\text{distal}, \text{club}} = 2.0 + 2.0 = 4.0\text{ J}$$

#### Numerical Residual:
$$T_{\text{distal}, M} - T_{\text{distal}, \text{club}} = 8.0\text{ J} - 4.0\text{ J} = 4.0\text{ J} \neq 0$$
The kinetic energy reported to downstream signals via `_club_kinetic_energy` under-reports the Lagrangian inertia matrix $M$'s energy by a factor of 2 in this configuration.

### 3. Scope and Limits of the Audit

* **Pinned Python Fallback:**
  This audit inspects the pure Python fallback formulations of `mass_matrix`, `coriolis_vector`, `gravity_vector`, and `_club_kinetic_energy`.
* **Not Backend-Wide Certification:**
  `mass_matrix`, `coriolis_vector`, and `gravity_vector` branch on optional native acceleration:
  ```python
  native_mass_matrix = _native_backend.double_mass_matrix(phi, params)
  if native_mass_matrix is not None:
      return native_mass_matrix
  ```
  If the C/C++ or compiled native backend implements a distributed rod instead of the point-mass $m_e$ assumption (or vice versa), the system will manifest backend-dependent divergence across native and Python runtimes.

### 4. Inspection of Window / Work Closure

In `summarize_transfer`:
```python
total_power, drift_power, control_power, along_path_force = _force_power_terms(
    velocity, total_force, drift_force, control_force
)
total_work = float(np.trapezoid(total_power, time))
drift_work = float(np.trapezoid(drift_power, time))
control_work = float(np.trapezoid(control_power, time))
...
work_closure_residual_j = total_work - drift_work - control_work
```

1. **Exact Linearity vs. Numerical Closure:**
   * In `double_pendulum_transfer_signals`, control force is constructed by direct definition as:
     $$\mathbf{F}_{\text{control}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{total}} - \mathbf{F}_{\text{drift}}$$
   * By linearity of the inner product $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{v}$ and the linearity of numerical trapezoidal integration:
     $$\int (\mathbf{F}_{\text{total}} \cdot \mathbf{v})\, dt - \int (\mathbf{F}_{\text{drift}} \cdot \mathbf{v})\, dt - \int ((\mathbf{F}_{\text{total}} - \mathbf{F}_{\text{drift}}) \cdot \mathbf{v})\, dt \equiv 0$$
   * Therefore, `work_closure_residual_j` is identically zero to machine epsilon. It validates numerical linearity of force decomposition, **not** physical energy conservation.

2. **Energy–Work Accounting Mismatch:**
   * `summarize_transfer` computes:
     $$\Delta E_{\text{distal}} = \text{distal\_energy\_gain\_j} = \text{energy}[-1] - \text{energy}[0]$$
     where `energy` is derived directly from `_club_kinetic_energy`.
   * However, the equations of motion and joint forces ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{total}}$, $\mathbf{F}_{\text{drift}}$ via `net_joint_forces` and `zero_torque_joint_forces_double`) are derived from the Lagrangian with point mass $m_e$ ($M_{22} = m_e L_2^2$).
   * Because the work terms $\int \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{v}\, dt + \int \tau_{\text{wrist}} \omega\, dt$ are driven by the $m_e$ point-mass dynamics, while $\Delta E_{\text{distal}}$ is calculated using the distributed-rod formula:
     $$\text{Total External Work on Distal Link} \neq \Delta E_{\text{distal}}$$
   * The boundary work and distal kinetic energy will systematically fail to close whenever $m_2 > 0$ and distal angular acceleration occurs across the window.
