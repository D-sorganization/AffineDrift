### 1. High Priority: Inaccurate Control-Affine and Input Structure Claims

* **Conflation of Bounded Input Sets / State-Dependent Input Gain with Non-Affine Dynamics**
  * **Location**: Application Appendix, *Limitations*, Item 1:
    > *"All applications assume control-affine structure holds: The entire methodology assumes that inputs enter the dynamics linearly ($\dot{x} = f(x) + G(x)u$). If the system exhibits strong input saturation (e.g., muscles have hard limits on torque that vary with velocity), input nonlinearities, or state-dependent input gain modulation, the affine decomposition becomes approximate or invalid."*
  * **Technical Inaccuracy**:
    1. **State-dependent input gain ($G(x)$)**: By explicit definition of control-affine dynamics $\dot{x} = f(x) + G(x)u$, the input matrix $G(x)$ is *inherently state-dependent* (e.g., coordinate transformation matrices, configuration-dependent moment arms, or velocity-dependent gain scalings). State-dependence in $G(x)$ does *not* violate or invalidate the affine decomposition.
    2. **Input saturation / bounded control sets ($u \in \mathcal{U}(x)$)**: Muscle force-velocity torque limits define a state-dependent constraint set $\mathcal{U}(x) = [u_{\min}(x), u_{\max}(x)]$ on the admissible control magnitude. Restricting $u$ to a bounded or state-dependent set does *not* make the differential equation nonlinear in $u$.
    3. The decomposition $\dot{x} = f(x) + G(x)u$ (or $F = F_{\text{drift}} + F_{\text{input}}$) remains strictly exact as long as the vector field depends affinely on the chosen input variable $u$. Only terms with genuine nonlinear dependence on the declared input variable (e.g., quadratic control drag $u^2$, nonlinear actuator compliance, or input-coupled hysteresis) break affine linearity.
  * **Suggested Correction**: Clarify that state-dependent actuator gains $G(x)$ and bounded admissible control envelopes $u \in \mathcal{U}(x)$ are fully compatible with control-affine structure. Reframe the limitation to address genuine non-affine mechanics (e.g., activation dynamics where control enters nonlinearly, or bilinear/product terms between controls).

### 2. Medium Priority: Pointwise Decomposition vs. Forward-Counterfactual Trajectories

* **Imprecise Definition of Drift Force via Forward Simulation ($ZTCF$)**
  * **Location**: Application Appendix, *Interpreting Net Model Input*:
    > *"Given measured or simulated total force $F_{\text{total}}$, and drift force $F_{\mathrm{drift}}$ from ZTCF-based computation, input forces are obtained via $F_{\mathrm{input}} = F_{\text{total}} - F_{\mathrm{drift}}$."*
  * **Technical Inaccuracy**:
    * If $F_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is computed via a forward-integrated ZTCF trajectory ($u(t) \equiv 0$ integrated over time), the resulting state $x_{\mathrm{ZTCF}}(t)$ diverges from the actual trajectory $x(t)$. Subtracting $F_{\mathrm{drift}}(x_{\mathrm{ZTCF}}(t))$ from $F_{\text{total}}(x(t))$ mixes states across counterfactual branches, which is invalid.
    * The instantaneous decomposition $F_{\text{total}}(x, u) = F_{\mathrm{drift}}(x) + F_{\mathrm{input}}(x, u)$ requires evaluating both terms at the **exact same instantaneous state** $x(t)$.
  * **Suggested Correction**: Distinguish the **same-state instantaneous decomposition** (evaluating $f(x)$ and $G(x)u$ at the current state $x(t)$) from the **forward-counterfactual trajectory** (integrating $\dot{x} = f(x)$ over $t \in [t_0, t_1]$ with $u \equiv 0$). Make clear that residual extraction requires evaluating $F_{\mathrm{drift}}$ at the instantaneous state of the measured swing.

* **Falsification Criterion: Distal Acceleration Independence**
  * **Location**: Workbench Article, *What Would Count Against the Mechanism?*:
    > *"- a distal acceleration that remains unchanged when the declared proximal motion and applied torques are removed;"*
  * **Technical Inaccuracy**:
    * In coupled multibody dynamics, removing a proximal generalized drive does not strictly mandate a non-zero shift in instantaneous distal acceleration for all states. At specific singular poses, zero-velocity configurations, or points where kinetic coupling crosses zero ($M_{12}(q) = 0$ or specific force cancellation), distal acceleration can be momentarily invariant to the removed term.
  * **Suggested Correction**: Frame the falsifier over a finite interval or non-singular trajectory segment rather than implying a pointwise acceleration variation must occur across arbitrary configurations.

### 3. Medium Priority: Mechanics Identifiability & Force Couple Attribution

* **Two-Hand Couple Attribution and Zero-Moment Arm Limits**
  * **Location**: Workbench Article, *Guided Experiments* and *What Would Count Against the Mechanism?*:
    > *"Two-Hand Force and Equivalent-Couple Mechanism: Hand separation and reaction directions... a two-hand couple that remains when the hands are coincident or the moment arm is set to zero"*
  * **Technical Boundary**:
    * Coincident hands ($d \to 0$) mathematically eliminate the macroscopic force-couple moment arm between the two grip centers ($\mathbf{r}_{\text{sep}} \times \mathbf{F}$). However, each physical hand can apply an intrinsic contact traction moment (torsion/twist) through distributed contact shear stress across the cylindrical grip interface.
  * **Suggested Correction**: Specify that setting hand separation distance to zero eliminates the **couple formed by the pair of discrete point forces**, but does not preclude local traction moments exerted by an individual hand unless point-contact assumptions are declared.
