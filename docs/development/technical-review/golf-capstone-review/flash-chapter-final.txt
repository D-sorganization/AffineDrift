#### 2. Incorrect Kinematic Velocity Derivation in Numerical Example
* **Location:** Section 2 (*Geometry Before Attribution*), paragraph 3:
  > *"For the default lengths $(0.28,0.46,1.10)$\,m and relative rates $(5,5,5)$\,rad/s, the straight configuration $q=(0,0,0)$ gives $22.5$\,m/s. At $q=(0,\pi,\pi)$ the signed transverse contributions are $1.4-4.6+16.5=13.3$\,m/s."*

* **Derivation & Defect:**
  Let relative rates be $\dot q = (\dot q_1, \dot q_2, \dot q_3) = (5, 5, 5)\,\text{rad/s}$.
  According to the chapter's definition in the preceding paragraph:
  $$\omega_i = \sum_{j=1}^i \dot q_j$$
  Therefore:
  $$\omega_1 = \dot q_1 = 5\,\text{rad/s}$$
  $$\omega_2 = \dot q_1 + \dot q_2 = 5 + 5 = 10\,\text{rad/s}$$
  $$\omega_3 = \dot q_1 + \dot q_2 + \dot q_3 = 5 + 5 + 5 = 15\,\text{rad/s}$$

  The tip velocity formula defined in the text is:
  $$\bm v = \sum_{i=1}^3 \ell_i \omega_i \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta_i \\ \cos\theta_i \end{bmatrix}$$

  With $\ell = (0.28, 0.46, 1.10)\,\text{m}$:
  * For $q = (0, 0, 0)$: $\theta_1 = 0$, $\theta_2 = 0$, $\theta_3 = 0$.
    All vectors point in the $+y$ direction:
    $$v_y = \ell_1 \omega_1 + \ell_2 \omega_2 + \ell_3 \omega_3$$
    $$v_y = (0.28)(5) + (0.46)(10) + (1.10)(15) = 1.40 + 4.60 + 16.50 = 22.50\,\text{m/s}$$
    *(This part is correct).*

  * For $q = (0, \pi, \pi)$:
    $$\theta_1 = q_1 = 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} -\sin 0 \\ \cos 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    $$\theta_2 = q_1 + q_2 = 0 + \pi = \pi \implies \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\pi \\ \cos\pi \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    $$\theta_3 = q_1 + q_2 + q_3 = 0 + \pi + \pi = 2\pi \equiv 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} -\sin 2\pi \\ \cos 2\pi \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

    Therefore, the signed transverse (vertical) contributions are:
    $$v_y = \ell_1\omega_1(+1) + \ell_2\omega_2(-1) + \ell_3\omega_3(+1) = 1.40 - 4.60 + 16.50 = 13.30\,\text{m/s}$$
    *(The signed contributions and true speed $13.3\,\text{m/s}$ are correct).*

  * **The Error:** The sentence immediately following states:
    > *"The configuration-blind value would overestimate this particular folded example by about $69\%$."*
    
    Let us compute the percentage overestimate:
    - True velocity magnitude at folded state: $s_{\text{true}} = 13.3\,\text{m/s}$.
    - Configuration-blind sum (scalar sum of speed magnitudes / straight sum): $s_{\text{blind}} = 22.5\,\text{m/s}$.
    - The overestimate is:
      $$\frac{s_{\text{blind}} - s_{\text{true}}}{s_{\text{true}}} = \frac{22.5 - 13.3}{13.3} = \frac{9.2}{13.3} \approx 0.691729 \rightarrow 69.2\% \approx 69\%$$
      *(Note: If calculated as $(22.5 - 13.3)/13.3 \approx 69\%$, this is exact.)*

    However, check the wording:
    > *"A scalar sum of angular rates times lever-arm lengths discards cancellation."*
    
    If an author calculates "scalar sum of angular rates times lever-arm lengths" literally as:
    $\sum_i \ell_i \dot q_i = (0.28)(5) + (0.46)(5) + (1.10)(5) = 1.4 + 2.3 + 5.5 = 9.2\,\text{m/s}$, that would be conflating relative joint rates with absolute segment angular velocities. Because $22.5\,\text{m/s}$ is obtained by $\sum \ell_i \omega_i$, state explicitly: *"A scalar sum of link lengths times absolute segment angular speeds $\sum \ell_i \norm{\omega_i}$ discards cancellation ($22.5$\,m/s vs. $13.3$\,m/s, an overestimate of $69\%$)"* to avoid ambiguity between relative joint rates $\dot q_i$ and absolute rates $\omega_i$.

#### 3. Prescribed Path Endpoint Velocity and Acceleration Inconsistency with Caption
* **Location:** Section 5 (*A Reproducible Synthetic Example*):
  $$q(\sigma) = \begin{bmatrix} -\pi/2 + \pi\sigma^2 \\ (\pi/3)\sin(\pi\sigma) \\ -(\pi/2)\cos(\pi\sigma/2) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \sigma = \frac{t}{T}, \quad \sigma \in [0, 1].$$
* **Derivation:**
  Differentiating with respect to $t$:
  $$\dot q(t) = \frac{1}{T}\frac{\dd q}{\dd\sigma} = \frac{1}{T}\begin{bmatrix} 2\pi\sigma \\ (\pi^2/3)\cos(\pi\sigma) \\ (\pi^2/4)\sin(\pi\sigma/2) \end{bmatrix}$$
  At $t=0$ ($\sigma=0$):
  $$\dot q(0) = \frac{1}{T}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \pi^2/3 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \approx \frac{1}{T}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 3.2899 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \ne \bm 0$$
  At $t=T$ ($\sigma=1$):
  $$\dot q(T) = \frac{1}{T}\begin{bmatrix} 2\pi \\ -\pi^2/3 \\ \pi^2/4 \end{bmatrix} \ne \bm 0$$
  The text correctly notes: *"Its nonzero endpoint rates are allowed; this example is not a rest-to-rest trajectory."*
  However, notice $q(0)$:
  $$q(0) = \begin{bmatrix} -\pi/2 \\ 0 \\ -\pi/2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  Here $\theta_1(0) = -\pi/2$, $\theta_2(0) = -\pi/2$, $\theta_3(0) = -\pi$.
  This represents link 1 pointing straight down, link 2 aligned with link 1 (straight down), and link 3 folded back up. This is a non-standard release pose for a golf backswing, which is fine for a synthetic numerical test, but in Section 3 and Exercise 3 the text refers to:
  > *"At a straight vertical configuration, the ideal model has $g=0$ and $C\dot q=0$..."*

  **Physics/Sign audit on $g(q)$:**
  The text states:
  > *"World height increases upward and gravity acts downward."*
  
  For potential energy with world vertical axis $\bm e_y$ pointing up, the height of link CoM is proportional to $\sin\theta_i$.
  Thus potential energy $V = \sum m_i g y_i$.
  Generalized gravity torque $g_j(q) = \frac{\partial V}{\partial q_j}$.
  Since $y_i$ depends on $\sin\theta_k$, $\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial q_j} \propto \cos\theta_k$.
  - At a **straight horizontal** configuration ($\theta_i = 0$), $\cos\theta_i = 1 \ne 0 \implies g(q) \ne 0$.
  - At a **straight vertical downward** configuration ($\theta_i = -\pi/2$), $\cos(-\pi/2) = 0 \implies g(q) = 0$.
  - At a **straight vertical upward** configuration ($\theta_i = \pi/2$), $\cos(\pi/2) = 0 \implies g(q) = 0$.
  
  The text states: *"At a straight vertical configuration, the ideal model has $g=0$ and $C\dot q=0$: derivatives of its relative-angle cosine terms vanish."*
  This is mechanically and mathematically sound.

#### 4. Derivative of Tip Speed Formula Discontinuity / Non-smoothness
* **Location:** Section 3 (*What Adds at the Same State*):
  $$\dot s_v = \frac{\bm v^{\mathsf T}\bm a_{p,d}}{s_v} + \frac{\bm v^{\mathsf T}\bm a_{p,u}}{s_v}$$
* **Defect:**
  The text states:
  > *"This formula is undefined at zero speed; acceleration vectors remain meaningful there."*
  
  While the text notes it is undefined at zero speed, it fails to state that even for $s_v > 0$, $\bm a_{p,d}$ includes the centripetal acceleration $\dot J_p \dot q = -\sum \ell_i \omega_i^2 \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_i \\ \sin\theta_i \end{bmatrix}$.
  Notice that:
  $$\bm v = \sum \ell_i \omega_i \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta_i \\ \cos\theta_i \end{bmatrix}$$
  For a rigid body or single link rotating at $\omega$, $\bm v \perp (\dot J_p \dot q)$ identically!
  For a multi-link chain, $\bm v^{\mathsf T}(\dot J_p \dot q) \ne 0$ in general due to cross-terms when links are not collinear, but when the chain is straight ($\theta_1 = \theta_2 = \theta_3 = \theta$):
  $$\bm v^{\mathsf T}(\dot J_p \dot q) = \left( \sum \ell_i \omega_i \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta \\ \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \right)^{\mathsf T} \left( -\sum \ell_k \omega_k^2 \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \end{bmatrix} \right) \equiv 0$$
  This identity is a valuable property for students verifying the projection: in any straight configuration, the centripetal term $\dot J_p \dot q$ is strictly orthogonal to $\bm v$ and drops completely out of $\dot s_v$. Adding this clarifies why centripetal acceleration contributes zero to instantaneous speed rate of change in straight configurations.
