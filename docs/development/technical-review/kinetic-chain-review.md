## Findings

- **P1 — Sequencing Is Not a Power-Flow Proof:** a universal whip/segment sequence, peak-velocity/peak-acceleration equivalence and quarter-period delay are asserted without a derivation or coherent measurement definition. Negative proximal power does not identify distal receipt; accelerations, segment angular velocities, joint rates and energy are conflated. Torso, shoulders and wrists overlap or are joints rather than disjoint rigid bodies. An arbitrary constraint Lagrangian derivative and an inverse-mass expression with acceleration units are called constraint torques. Establish Newton–Euler segment balances, joint wrench power, relative-joint actuator power, constraint cancellation and whole-system energy before interpreting timing.
- **P1 — Release and Muscle Mechanics:** clubhead/hand speed is equated with muscle shortening and an unsupported Hill-curve conclusion that wrists cannot drive the club. The club work balance omits force power at the moving grip. A constrained-club model loses its moving-pivot forcing on release, then claims continued centrifugal/Coriolis acceleration while its equation contains only drag. Late-release integrals use inconsistent endpoints and yield an unsupported 15–30% advantage. Replace with a coherent moving-grip pendulum, feasible constraint torque, declared release intervention and counterexamples to universal timing rules. Preserve distal active torque, activation/tendon dynamics and three-dimensional measurement limits.
- **P1 — Ground Wrench and Angular Momentum:** vertical force at a lateral foot is called a yaw torque; vertical and horizontal moments about different axes are added to predict hip angular acceleration. The computed 2,150deg/s is then said to explain 300–400deg/s. CoP alone is claimed to determine horizontal force direction; residual free moment is absent. A force peak before impact is equated with zero force at impact. Whole-system angular momentum and hip motion are conflated. Derive r cross F with declared axes, free moment and appropriate fixed-origin/COM angular-momentum balance; distinguish stationary-ground work from its external momentum channel.
- **P1 — Coupled Modes, X-Factor and Empirical Attribution:** a coupled spring model is assigned one independent natural frequency per segment, ignores its rigid mode, and uses individual frequencies to infer universal peak order. Absolute dampers are described as joint damping; maximum rotations are assumed equal and simultaneous, and nonzero separation is treated as zero preload. X-factor is called an angle between rotation axes, and stretch is imposed rather than tested. The claimed Cheetham sample, correlations, cohort ranges, energy distributions and timing values need primary-source verification. Adding 'illustrative' does not repair an algebraically false model or an unsupported empirical attribution.
- **P1 — Energy, Efficiency and Biological Inference:** total input is said to be conserved despite ongoing muscle work, gravity/elastic storage are dropped without scale analysis, segment maxima are mixed with simultaneous energy percentages, and unmeasured eccentric action is inferred from deceleration. A mechanical work ratio is confused with metabolic efficiency and unsupported 40–60% values; remaining energy is assigned to heat/collisions without a balance. Define signed/positive/net work, initial energy, remaining body/club/elastic/gravitational energy and losses, and state what inverse dynamics or EMG can identify.
- **P2 — Drift, Evidence and Instruction:** configuration derivative is incorrectly used as the full control-dependent drift; mass-matrix coupling is described as an inverse-created constraint force. Activation is equated with concentric action and deactivation with eccentric action. DCR is correctly distinguished from latency once, but pressure feedback later claims to calibrate it directly. Slow-motion similarity, faster-hip interpretation, maximal X-factor, passive wrist and effective training advice are unsupported. Rebuild the golf argument around model/port-frozen counterfactuals, measured outputs, finite-horizon sensitivity and testable individualized hypotheses. Rewrite all six exercises to be solvable and consistent with the corrected mechanics.
