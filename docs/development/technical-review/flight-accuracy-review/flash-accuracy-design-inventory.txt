## 2. Inconsistent Measurement Definitions & Metric Ambiguities

### A. Clubhead Speed Reference Point
* **Inconsistency:** Ch. 1 notes a multi-mph systematic spread arising from "reference-point differences (geometric center vs. fastest return)." Ch. 2 notes that a CW Doppler module sees a composite smear of head and shaft velocities, proposing an empirical fixed percentile of the velocity distribution to avoid "toe-speed inflation."
* **Impact:** Without a unified spatial coordinate definition on the clubhead (e.g., center of gravity, geometric face center, or impact point), "club speed" is not comparable across radar percentile estimators and optical tracker tracking points.

### B. Face Angle Definition & Provenance
* **Inconsistency:** Ch. 1 states radar calculates face angle via D-plane inversion amplifying launch-direction bias by $\sim 1.15\times$. Ch. 2 states the same inversion amplifies launch-direction bias by $1.32\times$.
* **Ambiguity:** Neither text specifies whether face angle is defined dynamic-at-maximum-compression, pre-impact normal, or projected into the horizontal plane.

### C. Club Path & Attack Angle: Direct vs. Derived
* **Inconsistency:** Ch. 2 Section 2 calls club path and attack angle from angle radar "legitimate direct measurements." However, Ch. 2 Section 3 (Tier 4) states that a screw-theoretic imaging radar estimator "moves path and attack angle from *derived* to *measured* in \cref{tab:hierarchy}."
* **Adjudication Note:** Path and attack angle in angle-radar are spatial trajectory derivatives/inversions of an extended moving body, blurring the boundary between direct measurement and model derivation.

## 3. Disentangling Metric Categories: Repeatability, Agreement, and Accuracy

1. **Repeatability (Precision / Reliability):**
   * *TrackMan 4 ICC Study (2024):* Reports high repeatability for club/ball speed ($\text{ICC} \ge 0.97$), but low repeatability for spin rate ($\text{ICC } 0.02\text{--}0.60$). ICC measures consistency within/between sessions, not closeness to truth.
   * *Robot Testing (Golf Laboratories):* Measures dispersion (standard deviation $\approx 82\text{ rpm}$ vs. $\approx 175\text{ rpm}$) under mechanically repeatable impacts. This measures precision/repeatability, not absolute calibration accuracy.
2. **Inter-Device Agreement:**
   * *Leach et al. (2017) & Mevo+ vs. TrackMan 4 (2025):* Evaluates concordance between concurrent instruments. Systematic offsets (e.g., $-0.4\text{ mph}$ median difference) quantify relative agreement. Agreement between two units does not establish ground truth if both share underlying model bias.
3. **Accuracy (Traceable Ground Truth):**
   * Strictly limited to validation against independent, calibrated external reference systems (e.g., the 5,400 fps optical reference in Leach et al., achieving benchmark pass criteria of only $54\%$ and $29\%$ for clubhead velocity).

## 4. Unsupported Universal Claims

* **"Ball speed is the strongest channel in any radar design: the Doppler line is unambiguous, and $\pm0.5\%$ class accuracy from a commodity module matches commercial practice."**
  * *Critique:* Universalizes ideal point-target Doppler returns. Ignores ball deformation dynamics at impact, separation from clubface Doppler smear, and multipath reflections in enclosed/cluttered indoor environments.
* **"[An EKF smoother with back-extrapolation] is the single highest-leverage software upgrade for angle quality, and it costs nothing in hardware."**
  * *Critique:* Unsubstantiated assertion of optimality ("single highest-leverage"); assumes phase-difference data meets EKF Gaussian noise assumptions during early flight.
* **"Alignment calibration dominates their accuracy, and no amount of signal processing recovers a misaimed sensor."**
  * *Critique:* Absolute claim ("no amount of signal processing") ignores multi-sensor cross-calibration or algorithmic boresight estimation from launch-velocity vector distributions.
* **"The honest disclosure did not cost them anything..."**
  * *Critique:* Unsubstantiated commercial counterfactual assertion regarding market share, product revenue, and consumer behavior.

## 6. Coupling to Downstream Models

1. **Flight Models:**
   * Radar-truncated flight requires switching from measurement to estimation. Ball spin and launch vector errors propagate non-linearly into trajectory solvers; an unvalidated $\Delta C_D = 0.01$ shift causes $\sim 8\text{ yards}$ carry variance.
2. **Inverse-Impact Models (D-Plane):**
   * Commercial radar units reconstruct face angle via inverse D-plane equations using measured path and ball launch angles. This inversion assumes center-face strikes, has no awareness of gear effect, and amplifies launch-direction measurement error by $1.15\times\text{--}1.32\times$.
3. **Observation Models:**
   * Radar relies on an aspect-angle cosine projection model from unit position to impact/launch vector.
   * Optical fiducial tracking relies on calibrated 2D-to-3D pose estimation, constrained by visibility, shutter synchronization, and marker-to-face geometric calibration.
