### 1. Mathematical and Statistical Rigor

* **Equation (1) vs. Sentence Context ($s$ vs. $\sigma$):**
  * **Location:** Section 2, immediately following Eq.~\eqref{eq:changeuncertainty}:
    > "Under an independent, equal-precision, approximately normal error model, a two-sided 95\% noise threshold is about $1.96\sqrt{2}\,s$, not $2s$."
  * **Issue:** Equation~\eqref{eq:changeuncertainty} introduces $s_1, s_2$ and error covariance $c_{12}$ as empirical sample standard deviations / covariances (or known error standard deviations). If $s$ is an estimated sample standard deviation with finite degrees of freedom, the multiplier is drawn from a Student's $t$-distribution ($t_{0.975, \nu}\sqrt{2}\,s$), not the asymptotic normal critical value $1.96$. Furthermore, the common rule of thumb "$\approx 2s$" or "$2.77s$" ($1.96\sqrt{2} \approx 2.77$) typically applies when testing a single reading against another single reading with error variance $s^2$, whereas the text earlier referred to "two session estimates".
  * **Concrete Correction:** Explicitly state the asymptotic/known-variance assumption:
    > "Assuming independent, equal-precision, approximately normal errors with known or well-estimated standard deviation $\sigma$ ($s_1 = s_2 = \sigma$, $c_{12} = 0$), a two-sided 95\% noise threshold for the difference between two single estimates is $1.96\sqrt{2}\,\sigma \approx 2.77\sigma$ (or using the critical $t$-value for small samples), rather than the unscaled $2\sigma$ or $2s$ sometimes informally cited."

* **Equation (2) Notation and True Values:**
  * **Location:** Section 3, Eq.~\eqref{eq:paireddevicevariance}:
    $$\operatorname{Var}(e_A - e_B) = \operatorname{Var}(e_A) + \operatorname{Var}(e_B) - 2\operatorname{Cov}(e_A, e_B)$$
  * **Issue:** The text states: *"If two devices measure the same scalar measurand with errors $e_A$ and $e_B$..."* If the true scalar measurand is $X$, device readings are $Y_A = X + e_A$ and $Y_B = X + e_B$, so $Y_A - Y_B = e_A - e_B$. This identity holds identically. However, the text immediately says:
    > "This reasoning presumes matched reference points, timing and definitions; otherwise the physical measurand difference contributes too."
  * **Ambiguity:** If the physical measurands differ ($X_A \neq X_B$), then the observed difference is $(X_A - X_B) + (e_A - e_B)$, and its variance includes $\operatorname{Var}(X_A - X_B)$ plus cross-covariances with errors.
  * **Concrete Correction:** Make the decomposition explicit:
    > "If two devices target measurands $X_A$ and $X_B$ with errors $e_A$ and $e_B$, the observed difference is $(X_A - X_B) + (e_A - e_B)$. Only when the target measurand is identical ($X_A = X_B$) does the variance reduce strictly to the error variance:
    > $$\operatorname{Var}(Y_A - Y_B) = \operatorname{Var}(e_A) + \operatorname{Var}(e_B) - 2\operatorname{Cov}(e_A, e_B).$$
    > Discrepancies in timing or impact definition introduce true physical differences $\operatorname{Var}(X_A - X_B)$, confounding the error evaluation."

* **Interpretation of Retained Percentages in Bliss & Langdown (Section 3):**
  * **Location:** Section 3, paragraph 1:
    > "Of 118 driver, 118 seven-iron and 174 pitching-wedge attempts, 58, 80 and 69 were retained after exclusions for missing fields, estimated spin or severe mishits: approximately 49\%, 68\% and 40\%."
  * **Arithmetic Check:**
    * Driver: $58 / 118 = 49.15\% \approx 49\%$
    * 7-iron: $80 / 118 = 67.80\% \approx 68\%$
    * Pitching wedge: $69 / 174 = 39.66\% \approx 40\%$
  * **Comment:** Arithmetic is correct.

### 2. Ambiguities and Terminology

* **ICC Definitions (Consistency vs. Absolute Agreement & Single vs. Average):**
  * **Location:** Section 3, paragraph 1 & 2:
    > "Table 2 reports a seven-iron ball-speed consistency ICC of 0.996 alongside a mean TrackMan-minus-Mevo+ difference of $-3.03$\,mph; its absolute-agreement ICC is 0.90 with a wide interval from $-0.02$ to 0.97. The protocol used mean-rating ICCs and reported both consistency and absolute agreement."
  * **Ambiguity:** In the Shrout & Fleiss / McGraw & Wong taxonomy, "mean-rating" is denoted ICC$(k)$ (e.g., ICC(2,$k$) or ICC(3,$k$)), while single-measurement is ICC(1). In paragraph 2, the text states:
    > "An average-measures ICC targets an average of ratings, rather than the reliability of one instrument reading; its value should not be quoted as a single-reading accuracy specification."
  * **Concrete Correction:** Explicitly name the model convention (e.g., "two-way mixed-effects average-measures ICC, $\text{ICC}(3,k)$ or $\text{ICC}(2,k)$") so the reader understands why consistency ICC can remain near 1.0 despite systematic bias (consistency discards between-device additive bias $\sigma_{\text{rater}}^2$, whereas absolute agreement incorporates it).

* **Degrees Symbol Notation ($\degs$):**
  * **Location:** Section 1, paragraph 2 & 3: `$0.01\degs$`, `$0.0\degs$`, `$0.1\degs$`.
  * **Issue:** While standard in some custom macro packages, verify if `\degs` is defined in the project's preamble (commonly `^\circ` or `\degree` from `gensymb`/`siunitx`). If non-standard, this may trigger compilation failure.
