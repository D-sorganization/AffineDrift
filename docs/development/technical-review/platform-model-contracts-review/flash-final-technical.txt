2. **Sign of COM Kinematics in the URDF Frame vs. Equation of Motion**
   * **Quote:**
     > *"<origin xyz=\"0 0 -0.5\" rpy=\"0 0 0\"/>"*
     > *"<axis xyz=\"0 1 0\"/>"*
     > *"At angle $q$ and rate $v$, the expected COM position and scalar dynamic terms are*
     > \begin{equation}
     > p_C=(-\tfrac12\sin q,\ 0,\ -\tfrac12\cos q)
     > \end{equation}"*
   * **Error:** 
     Under the right-hand rule about the axis $\hat{y} = (0, 1, 0)$, a positive rotation by angle $q$ transforms a vector initially pointing along $-\hat{z} = (0, 0, -0.5)$ into:
     $$R_y(q) \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -0.5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos q & 0 & \sin q \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -\sin q & 0 & \cos q \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -0.5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{-0.5 \sin q} \\ 0 \\ \mathbf{-0.5 \cos q} \end{pmatrix}.$$
     This matches $p_C = (-\frac{1}{2}\sin q, 0, -\frac{1}{2}\cos q)$.
     Now let's check the potential energy and gravitational torque sign:
     With gravity $g = (0, 0, -g) = (0, 0, -9.81)$, the potential energy is:
     $$V(q) = - m \mathbf{g} \cdot \mathbf{p}_C = - (1.0) (-g) (-0.5 \cos q) = -0.5 m g \cos q = 0.5 m g (1 - \cos q) + \text{const}.$$
     The generalized gravitational force $Q_g = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q} = -0.5 m g \sin q = -4.905 \sin q$.
     In the equation of motion written as:
     $$M(q)\dot v + c(q,v) = \tau,$$
     where gravity is placed on the left side: $M \dot v + g_q = \tau_{\rm pass}$, we have:
     $$M \ddot q - Q_g = \tau_{\rm pass} \implies M \ddot q + 0.5 m g \sin q = \tau_{\rm pass} \implies g_q = 0.5 m g \sin q = 4.905 \sin q.$$
     This matches.
     
     Wait! Now check Chapter 1:
     * **Quote:**
       > \begin{equation}
       > \dot E = -b \dot q^2
       > \end{equation}
       > \begin{equation}
       > I_P\ddot q+b\dot q+mg(L/2)\sin q=0,
       > \end{equation}
       > \begin{equation}
       > E=\tfrac12 I_P\dot q^2+mg(L/2)(1-\cos q),
       > \end{equation}
       This sign is consistent with $\dot E = \dot q (I_P \ddot q + mg(L/2)\sin q) = \dot q (-b \dot q) = -b \dot q^2$.

3. **Trapezoidal Quadrature Scaling and Endpoint Consistency**
   * **Quote:**
     > *"For $t=(0,1,3)$ and scalar normalized errors $(0,1,3)$, the trapezoidal integral of squared error is $10.5$, giving $\sqrt{3.5}$. The unweighted node RMS is $\sqrt{10/3}$; neither equals the exact continuous RMS $\sqrt{3}$ for $e(t)=t$ on this coarse grid."*
   * **Verification:**
     Let $t_0=0, t_1=1, t_2=3$, with errors $e_0=0, e_1=1, e_2=3$.
     Squared errors: $y_0 = 0^2 = 0$, $y_1 = 1^2 = 1$, $y_2 = 3^2 = 9$.
     Trapezoidal rule over intervals $[0, 1]$ and $[1, 3]$:
     - Interval 1 ($h_1 = 1 - 0 = 1$): $\frac{0 + 1}{2} \times 1 = 0.5$.
     - Interval 2 ($h_2 = 3 - 1 = 2$): $\frac{1 + 9}{2} \times 2 = 10.0$.
     Total integral $= 0.5 + 10.0 = 10.5$.
     Duration $T = 3 - 0 = 3$.
     Mean square $= 10.5 / 3 = 3.5$.
     RMS $= \sqrt{3.5}$.
     Unweighted node RMS: $\sqrt{(0^2 + 1^2 + 3^2) / 3} = \sqrt{10/3}$.
     Continuous function $e(t) = t$: $\frac{1}{3}\int_0^3 t^2 dt = \frac{1}{3} [\frac{t^3}{3}]_0^3 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 9 = 3 \implies \text{RMS} = \sqrt{3}$.
     This calculation is mathematically exact and correctly illustrates grid-spacing bias.

### Priority 2: Model vs. Numerical Validation Distinctions

4. **Conflating Solver Accuracy / Agreement with Model Validity**
   * **Quote:**
     > *"Two simulations' difference is an agreement metric; calling it accuracy requires an independently justified reference, such as an analytic limit or a documented convergence study."*
     > *"Repository tests parse the actual listing and, when MuJoCo is installed, load it to check mass, coordinates, COM, inertia, gravity and damping. That single-engine test does not certify import by Drake or Pinocchio, or a time-integrated comparison."*
   * **Analysis:**
     The text carefully and explicitly distinguishes:
     - **Model verification/equivalence:** Confirming that the parsed physical properties ($M, I_P, p_C, g_q, \tau_{\rm pass}$) match the intended physical parameters before integration.
     - **Numerical validation/solver accuracy:** Measuring integration truncation error, step refinement, and energy-work residual ($r_E(t) = E(t) - E(0) + \int b \dot{q}^2 dt$) against an independent reference.
     - **Cross-engine agreement:** Cross-engine comparison without an analytic ground truth only assesses whether two engines make the same assumptions, not physical ground-truth correctness.

### Priority 3: Exact Specification Checks

5. **URDF Cylinder Inertia vs. Analytic Solid Cylinder Formulas**
   * **Quote (Chapter 1):**
     > \begin{equation}
     > I_C=\frac{m(L^2+3r^2)}{12},\qquad
     > I_P=I_C+m(L/2)^2=m\left(\frac{L^2}{3}+\frac{r^2}{4}\right).
     > \end{equation}
   * **Quote (Chapter 3 URDF):**
     > *`mass value="1.0"`*
     > *`length="1.0" radius="0.02"`*
     > *`ixx="0.0834333333333333"`*
     > *`iyy="0.0834333333333333"`*
     > *`izz="0.0002"`*
   * **Check:**
     With cylinder axis aligned along $z$:
     - Transverse moments:
       $$I_{xx} = I_{yy} = \frac{m(L^2 + 3r^2)}{12} = \frac{1.0 \times (1.0 + 3 \times 0.0004)}{12} = \frac{1.0012}{12} = 0.08343333333333333\dots\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2.$$
     - Axial moment:
       $$I_{zz} = \frac{1}{2} m r^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.0 \times 0.02^2 = 0.0002\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2.$$
     - Off-diagonal terms: $0$.
     The URDF tensor values and analytic formula match to full double precision.
