## 1. Chapter: *Physics Engine Comparison*

### A. Benchmark Inference & Energy vs. Phase/Drift Errors
1. **Conflation of Energy Drift with Integration/Trajectory Accuracy:**
   - **Error:** Caption to Figure \ref{fig:benchmark-drift} states: *"Smaller drift indicates better numerical integration accuracy."*
   - **Scientific Defect:** Symplectic integrators (e.g., Velocity Verlet, semi-implicit Euler) do not conserve exact energy; they conserve a shadow/modified Hamiltonian. Consequently, energy oscillates within an $O(\Delta t^p)$ bounded band without secular drift, even while phase/trajectory error accumulates linearly or quadratically over time ($O(t\,\Delta t^p)$). In contrast, dissipative or higher-order explicit integrators (e.g., RK4) can exhibit secular energy decay or growth while yielding lower point-wise phase error over short horizons. Energy conservation alone is not a monotonic proxy for trajectory/integration accuracy.
   - **Correction:** Clarify that energy conservation verifies Hamiltonian preservation (absence of spurious numerical damping or divergence), whereas phase-space trajectory error ($q(t), \dot{q}(t)$) or phase drift measures integration accuracy.

2. **Damping Work Balance Formulation:**
   - **Error:** Section *Benchmark Interpretation* states: *"Systems with damping dissipate energy; drift measures additional numerical errors relative to expected dissipation."*
   - **Scientific Defect:** The code listing computes `energy_drift: float` as a simple relative change without tracking non-conservative generalized forces ($Q_{\text{diss}}$) or integration of dissipative power $\int \dot{q}^T D \dot{q} \, dt$. For damped systems, energy drift is physically undefined unless evaluated against the total energy balance equation:
     $$\Delta E_{\text{drift}}(t) = |(T(t) + V(t)) - (T(0) + V(0)) - W_{\text{ext}}(t) - W_{\text{diss}}(t)|$$
   - **Correction:** Explicitly define the energy-work balance residual incorporating $\int \tau_{\text{damper}}\dot{q}\,dt$. For unforced, undamped benchmarks, restrict `energy_drift` to $\frac{|E(t) - E(0)|}{E(0)}$.

3. **Benchmarking Protocol Deficiencies:**
   - **Error:** In `compare_engines`, `dt` and `t_end` are passed as uniform parameters, while solver types, internal substeps, integrator orders (e.g., semi-implicit vs. Runge-Kutta 4 vs. implicit Euler), and constraint solver tolerances are omitted.
   - **Correction:** Parameterize the `PhysicsEngine` protocol with integration scheme identifiers and constraint solver tolerance/iterations to prevent comparing variable-order or disparate constraint methods under identical nominal step size $\Delta t$.

### B. Contact Model & Integrator Categorization (Table \ref{tab:contact-comparison})
1. **MuJoCo:**
   - **Contract/Schema Defect:** Listed as *"Soft contact with implicit integration"*.
   - **Primary Verification:** MuJoCo models contacts using convex optimization (quadratic programming / cone complementarity problems) with dual velocity-stepping formulations (standard semi-implicit / Euler, with optional RK4 or implicit integrators for smooth dynamics). The contact model itself is a regularized complementary contact model via parameters $\text{solref}$ and $\text{solimp}$, not exclusively an "implicit integration soft contact".
   - **Correction:** Describe as *"Regularized complementarity / convex optimization contact (solref/solimp) with velocity-stepping"*.

2. **Drake:**
   - **Contract/Schema Defect:** Listed as *"Rigid with hydroelastic contact"*.
   - **Primary Verification:** Drake supports both compliant (hydroelastic contact model, representing volumetric pressure fields) and discrete rigid approximation (time-stepping with convex optimization/LCP). Hydroelastic contact specifically models volumetric pressure fields over compliant meshes, rather than standard rigid point contact.
   - **Correction:** Describe as *"Point contact (rigid/time-stepping) and hydroelastic contact (volumetric compliant pressure field)"*.

3. **Pinocchio:**
   - **Contract/Schema Defect:** Listed as *"No built-in contact handling"*.
   - **Primary Verification:** Pinocchio includes native algorithms for constrained dynamics (proximal point algorithms for contact dynamics, `pinocchio::computeConstraintDynamics`, impulse dynamics, and constrained forward dynamics with friction cones).
   - **Correction:** Update description to *"Analytical rigid contact dynamics and constraint solvers (proximal formulation); lacks native volumetric collision geometry/proximity detection"*.

4. **OpenSim:**
   - **Contract/Schema Defect:** Listed as *"Hunt-Crossley damping model"*.
   - **Primary Verification:** OpenSim relies on Simbody as its underlying multi-body engine; Simbody implements Hunt-Crossley and elastic foundation contact models.
   - **Correction:** Specify *"Simbody-based Hunt-Crossley compliant contact and elastic foundation models"*.

## 2. Chapter: *Building a Multi-Body Model*

### A. URDF Schema, Kinematic Tree, and Spec Violations

1. **Disconnected Kinematic Tree (Orphan Link):**
   - **Schema Violation:** `build_golf_swing_model` defines 7 links (`pelvis`, `trunk`, `upper_arm`, `forearm`, `hand`, `club_shaft`, `club_head`). However, only 6 joints are defined:
     - `pelvis_rotation`: `world` $\to$ `pelvis`
     - `trunk_flexion`: `pelvis` $\to$ `trunk`
     - `shoulder`: `trunk` $\to$ `upper_arm`
     - `elbow`: `upper_arm` $\to$ `forearm`
     - `wrist`: `forearm` $\to$ `hand`
     - `grip`: `hand` $\to$ `club_shaft`
   - **Impact:** `club_head` has no parent joint or link connection. In standard URDF parsing (ROS `urdfdom`, Pinocchio, Drake), a model must be a connected directed tree rooted at a single base. An unattached link causes parser validation failure (`Link 'club_head' is not connected to tree`).
   - **Correction:** Add a joint attaching `club_head` to `club_shaft` (e.g., `Joint("club_hosel", "club_shaft", "club_head", "fixed")`).

2. **Invalid URDF Joint Type (`ball`):**
   - **Schema Violation:** `Joint("shoulder", "trunk", "upper_arm", "ball")`.
   - **Primary URDF Specification:** The official ROS URDF specification supports only: `revolute`, `continuous`, `prismatic`, `fixed`, `floating`, and `planar`. **`ball` is not a valid URDF joint type.** (Spherical/ball joints are valid in MJCF `<joint type="ball"/>` and SDF, but not in URDF).
   - **Impact:** Fails URDF validation schema in `urdfdom`, Drake (`AddModelsFromUrl`), and Pinocchio (`pinocchio::urdf::buildModel`).
   - **Correction:** For URDF export, either decompose the spherical joint into three orthogonal 1-DOF `revolute` joints separated by zero-mass/zero-inertia intermediate dummy links, or restrict spherical joints to native MJCF generation.

3. **Missing URDF Required Elements (`<origin>`, `<inertia>` tensors, `<limit>`):**
   - **Schema Violation:** In `to_urdf()`:
     - `<inertial>` contains only `<mass value="..."/>`. It omits `<origin>` and the required inertia matrix tensor element:
       ```xml
       <inertia ixx="..." ixy="..." ixz="..." iyy="..." iyz="..." izz="..."/>
       ```
       Without `<inertia>`, standard URDF parsers reject the link or fail to assemble spatial inertia.
     - For `revolute` joints, `<limit effort="..." lower="..." upper="..." velocity="..."/>` is **mandatory** under URDF specification. Omitting `<limit>` causes parser rejection.
     - No `<origin xyz="..." rpy="..."/>` transforms are emitted for joints, placing all link frame origins coincident at $(0, 0, 0)$.
     - Joint `<axis xyz="..."/>` is omitted in `to_urdf()`.
   - **Correction:** Expand `to_urdf()` to serialize full spatial transforms (`<origin>`), the 3x3 inertia matrix via `<inertia>`, `<axis>`, and `<limit>` attributes for non-fixed joints.

4. **Inconsistent Generalized Coordinate Counts ($q$ vs. $v$):**
   - **Kinematic Defect:**
     ```python
     dof_map = {"revolute": 1, "ball": 3, "fixed": 0}
     return sum(dof_map.get(j.joint_type, 1) for j in self.joints)
     ```
     For spherical/ball joints, the coordinate representation differs between configuration space $q \in \mathbb{R}^4$ (unit quaternion / $SO(3)$) and tangent space / velocity vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (angular velocity vector $\boldsymbol{\omega}$). A single `n_dof` scalar collapses the distinction between $n_q$ (positions) and $n_v$ (velocities/degrees of freedom).
   - **Correction:** Separate into `n_q` (configuration dimensions) and `n_v` (velocity/degree-of-freedom dimensions).

5. **Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) Misattribution:**
   - **Text Error:** *"URDF models use the Denavit-Hartenberg convention or explicit spatial transforms \cite{dh1955}."*
   - **Primary Verification:** URDF uses explicit 6-DOF spatial transforms (`<origin xyz="..." rpy="..."/>`) with modified fixed joint-to-link reference frames. URDF does not use DH parameter syntax ($\theta, d, a, \alpha$).
   - **Correction:** Revise text to state that URDF utilizes explicit rigid Cartesian spatial transforms ($SE(3)$ transformations parameterized by translation vectors and Euler/RPY rotations), not Denavit-Hartenberg parameterizations.

## 3. Anthropometric & Biomechanical Provenance

1. **Unsupported and Implausible Anthropometric Parameterization:**
   - **Text Claim:** Segment parameters are derived from standard anthropometric literature for an 80 kg male.
   - **Scientific Defect:**
     - Sum of defined masses:
       $$m_{\text{total}} = 11.2 + 25.5 + 2.16 + 1.30 + 0.49 + 0.10 + 0.20 = 40.95\,\text{kg}$$
       This accounts for only ~51% of an 80 kg individual. The lower extremities (thighs, shanks, feet, which constitute ~32-35% of body mass) and head/neck (~7-8%) are omitted without mass re-apportionment or base-frame grounding justification.
     - Trunk mass of $25.5\,\text{kg}$ without head/arms represents ~31.8% body mass, which differs from established biomechanical reference tables (e.g., de Leva 1996 adjustments to Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov: trunk is ~43.5% including thorax, abdomen, and pelvis; pelvis alone is ~14.2% $\approx 11.36\,\text{kg}$, but upper arm is ~2.71% $\approx 2.17\,\text{kg}$).
     - `com_position` is provided as a scalar float along an unspecified 1D axis rather than a 3D vector $\mathbf{r}_{\text{CoM}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ relative to the link coordinate origin.
     - Default inertia is initialized to the identity matrix $\mathbf{I}_{3 \times 3} = \mathbf{E}$, which is physically ungrounded (violates triangle inequalities of moments of inertia for thin cylindrical or anatomical segments: $I_{xx} + I_{yy} \ge I_{zz}$).
   - **Correction:**
     - Explicitly cite the specific regression model (e.g., *de Leva (1996)* or *Winter (2009)* anthropometric tables).
     - State that the 7-segment model represents an upper-body and club kinematic chain with rigid/fixed lower-body assumptions, or adjust the total mass distribution accordingly.
     - Represent center of mass as a 3D spatial coordinate vector $[x, y, z]^T$.
     - Compute realistic principal moments of inertia assuming geometric primitives (e.g., solid cylinder or ellipsoid) scaled by segment mass and length.
