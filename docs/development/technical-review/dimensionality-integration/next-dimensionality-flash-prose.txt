In biomechanical modeling, a system is described by $n$ independent configuration coordinates $q$, yielding $2n$ mechanical state coordinates $(q, v)$ when paired with generalized velocities. The system is actuated by $m$ input commands $u$, while task-level goals are evaluated through $d$ outputs $y = h(q)$. Any additional muscle activation dynamics, tendon elasticity, or internal controller states further expand the total state dimension beyond $2n$. When the task differential $Dh(q)$ maintains a locally constant rank $r$, generalized velocities that instantaneously preserve the task output reside in the null space $\ker Dh(q)$, which has dimension $n - r$. Under these constant-rank conditions, configurations achieving the exact same instantaneous task output form a smooth local level manifold.

Crucially, kinematic redundancy does not ensure mechanical feasibility. Having instantaneous degrees of freedom that leave a kinematic task unchanged guarantees neither physiological muscle forces nor a dynamically reachable, stable trajectory within tissue limits. Furthermore, introducing a reduced actuator basis $u = S a$ with $\operatorname{rank}(S) = k < m$ restricts independent control inputs to $k$ actuation channels, yet the underlying mechanical state dimension remains strictly $2n$. In a golf swing, defining the impact target typically requires prescribing both the clubhead pose and its instantaneous velocity, making the task output dependent on the complete mechanical state $(q, v)$ rather than configuration alone. Consequently, joint coordinates omitted from a terminal task penalty still govern critical physical constraints, including dynamic torque limits, posture, balance, and club-body collisions.

As an illustration, consider an upper-limb model with $n = 7$ joint coordinates tracking a $d = 6$ spatial pose target for the clubhead. At generic configurations where the kinematic Jacobian exhibits full row rank $r = 6$, the null space $\ker Dh(q)$ has dimension $7 - 6 = 1$, yielding a single instantaneous null direction for joint motion that leaves the club pose undisturbed. However, at singular configurations where the Jacobian drops rank ($r < 6$), this regular null-space structure changes, restricting task-space mobility while expanding unconstrained internal velocity directions.
