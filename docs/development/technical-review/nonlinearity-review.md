## Confirmed Findings

- **P1 — Inconsistent Mechanical Equations:** a rigid physical pendulum about a fixed pivot is incorrectly assigned angle-dependent inertia. The 2R inertia matrix lacks the standard distal-inertia and coupling terms and is not reconciled with its Coriolis matrix. The Coriolis section adds a gravitational Jacobian matrix to a torque vector, uses inconsistent Christoffel indexing and an incorrect example. Gravity potential/sign conventions are inconsistent. The final drift vector uses an identity instead of velocity and subtracts a matrix from a force vector. Derive one complete, energy-consistent 2R model from declared geometry, kinetic and potential energies and verify its equations independently.
- **P1 — Affinity, Force Addition and Counterfactuals:** the initial correct fixed-state acceleration superposition statement is contradicted throughout the synthesis and examples. State-dependent gains are sometimes claimed to break affinity; co-contraction is said to violate force addition; zero torque, zero excitation, passive tissue and equilibrium are conflated. A nonlinear-input zero-input comparison is called invalid, although the exact difference remains definable even when it is not G(x)u. Separate physical force sums, fixed-state increments, smooth/mode-specific affinity, admissible inputs and divergent finite trajectories. A state extension is not a guarantee that every nonlinear input dependence disappears.
- **P1 — Contact and Friction:** static-friction branches overlap, omit other applied loads and lack a consistent sign convention. A kinetic Stribeck law is used at rest to claim zero damping and stiction; its exponent lacks a defined speed magnitude and its stated high-speed behavior lacks a viscous term. Load-dependent normal force can make Coulomb friction input-dependent after contact elimination. Rigid contact is confused with perfectly inelastic impact and tangential versus normal velocity, and contact changes are asserted to change the ambient state dimension automatically. Derive a stick/slip example, contact feasibility and a fixed-active-set constraint solve, distinguishing impulses and compliant dynamics.
- **P1 — Transmission and Limits:** backlash has a self-referential memoryless formula and is categorically input-nonlinear even with motor/load states retained. Two simultaneous torques and their identical sum are claimed physically different. Deadzone uses a discontinuous threshold without distinguishing the usual continuous law. Saturation, joint stops, speed bounds and power constraints are conflated with loss of affinity; realized torque and commanded input ports must be separated. Back-EMF/current/voltage relationships need a coherent motor model. Include preload, engagement, actuator states and feasible input sets explicitly.
- **P1 — Muscle Mechanics and Interpretation:** force-length is misattributed and an activation-linear gain is called non-affine; the normalized Hill curvature parameter is inverted and assigned an unsupported universal value. Isometric force is called maximum despite eccentric force, and a shortening hyperbola is extended outside its domain. Excitation, activation and muscle force are interchanged; zero excitation does not instantly remove active force. Tendon force equality omits pennation and the model's quasistatic/massless assumption. Co-contraction and stretch-shortening claims need bounded models, history states, force/work distinctions and evidence. Punch speed does not identify muscle-fiber speed or collision force.
- **P2 — Elasticity, Fluids and Thermomechanics:** axial spinning is confused with radial centrifugal tension; stiffness needs tangent/secant and stable-domain distinctions. Constant complex stiffness is treated as a generic acceleration-dependent time-domain damper without a real causal realization. Kelvin–Voigt need not introduce an independent state, and memory models may require distributed states. The Magnus equation has incorrect force dimensions for a dimensionless lift coefficient; relative airspeed, spin parameter and coefficient regime are missing. Vortex shedding is not necessarily an independent periodic disturbance. Temperature/wear changes and input-driven heating can alter affinity in the augmented model. Do not infer constitutive terms independently from one zero-input trial.
- **P2 — Control and Explanations:** linearization omits the operating input and residual when the nominal point is not an equilibrium. Full-state G is generally rectangular for second-order mechanics; feedback linearization needs relative degree, decoupling rank, internal dynamics and feasible inputs. Impedance control is not an automatic uncertainty guarantee. Golf gravity is not universally destabilizing, ball impact is not backlash, and the club remains hand-constrained outside ball contact. The steering-radius analogy reverses the kinematic bicycle result and confuses curvature with lateral acceleration. Replace unsupported numerical steering/play claims and repair actual internal links.
