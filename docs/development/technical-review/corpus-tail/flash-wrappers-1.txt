### 1. `articles/Launch_Monitor_Technology_Review/sections/01-introduction.tex`

#### Issue 1.1: Unsupported / Overstated Technical Generalization
* **Classification:** Proven Inconsistency / Misleading Scope Statement
* **Location:** Section 2 (*The Two Sensing Families*), paragraph 2:
  > `"Their weakness is the mirror image of radar's: everything downrange of the capture volume is simulated, and club data requires either fiducial stickers on the face or an overhead viewing geometry."`
* **Audit Finding:**
  The claim that club data requires *either* fiducial stickers *or* an overhead viewing geometry is technically inaccurate and contradicts the market examples cited in the same chapter. Several ground-level photometric systems (e.g., Foresight GCQuad, GC3, Bushnell Launch Pro) can measure club delivery parameters without overhead mounting, and modern systems (including camera/radar fusions and stickerless photometric systems like Uneekor EYE MINI or machine-learning-assisted edge detectors) do not strictly require face fiducials.
* **Suggested Correction:**
  Change to:
  > `"Their weakness is the mirror image of radar's: everything downrange of the capture volume is simulated, and direct optical measurement of club-face presentation typically requires high-contrast fiducial markers, dedicated multi-angle optics, or specialized viewing geometries."`

#### Issue 1.2: Overstated Claim of Measurement Fidelity
* **Classification:** Unsupported Scientific Claim
* **Location:** Section 2 (*The Two Sensing Families*), paragraph 2:
  > `"They measure launch conditions --- including 3D spin from dimple-pattern rotation --- essentially perfectly for simulator purposes, then \emph{model} the flight."`
* **Audit Finding:**
  The phrase `"essentially perfectly"` is unscientific, unsupported by error bounds, and directly contradicts the text's own neutrality note and Chapter 8/accuracy references (where optical tracking errors, lighting/exposure noise, spin decay, and shutter sync uncertainties are evaluated).
* **Suggested Correction:**
  Change to:
  > `"They measure initial launch conditions --- including 3D spin vector estimation from dimple-pattern tracking --- with high precision over the calibrated capture window, then \emph{model} downrange flight."`

#### Issue 1.3: Reference Timing Inconsistency for Club Delivery
* **Classification:** Inconsistent Model Assumption / Proven Error
* **Location:** Section 3 (*Reading Guide*), Keypoint box:
  > `"All club-delivery values are referenced to the moment of \emph{maximum ball compression}; all ball-launch values to the moment of \emph{separation from the face}."`
* **Audit Finding:**
  While ball launch is standardly referenced at separation, standard impact mechanics (e.g., Cochran & Stobbs, TrackMan definitions, USGA/R&A protocols) and commercial photogrammetry reference incoming delivery parameters (such as face angle, dynamic loft, and club path) to the moment of *initial contact* or *immediately prior to impact*, because severe deceleration, face deflection, and twisting occur between first contact and maximum ball compression (~200–250 $\mu$s later). Defining club-delivery values strictly at maximum compression conflicts with TrackMan's stated conventions cited in the same section.
* **Suggested Correction:**
  Change to:
  > `"Unless otherwise specified by a vendor's reported convention, club-delivery values are referenced to the moment of \emph{initial ball contact} (or immediately prior to impact deceleration), whereas ball-launch kinematics are referenced to the moment of \emph{separation from the face}."`
