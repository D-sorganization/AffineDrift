## 1. Concrete Contradictory Evidence Wording & Mathematical Scope Errors

### A. Mathematical Scope & Falsifier Formulation in `ad-dcr-reachability.qmd`
1. **Falsifier 1 ("The declared constant-additive-drift system changes reachable-interval width when only constant drift changes"):**
   - **Scope / Mathematical Tension:** For linear time-invariant control-affine systems $\dot{x} = A x + B u + d$ with compact convex control sets $U$, when $A = 0$ (pure integrator/constant additive drift $\dot{x} = B u + d$), the reachable set at horizon $T$ is $R(T) = x_0 + d T + \int_0^T B U \, dt$. The *set* translates by $d T$, but the set diameter/width $\mu(R(T))$ is independent of $d$. However, if $A \neq 0$ or state-dependent bounds/constraints exist (or in coordinates where drift couples nonlinearly with control direction over $T$), reachable volume/width *can* couple with drift. Calling this property an analytical falsifier without explicitly specifying the zero-matrix $A=0$ restriction creates a scope ambiguity where a general constant-drift system could appear to falsify the counterexample merely due to nonzero drift dynamics.
2. **Technical Claim vs. Plain-Language Tension on "Imbalance":**
   - The Plain-Language Summary states: *"A large DCR may flag a modeled acceleration imbalance worth investigating."*
   - In contrast, the Bounded Technical Claim and critique records (`crit-dimensional-inconsistency-dcr`, `crit-normative-ambiguity-drift`) emphasize that without common units, projection metrics, and coordinate-free normalization, the scalar ratio of drift to control cannot intrinsically quantify a physical "acceleration imbalance" without coordinate-dependent distortion. Framing DCR as flagging an "acceleration imbalance" in the plain summary re-introduces physical intuition that the technical claim explicitly disclaims.
