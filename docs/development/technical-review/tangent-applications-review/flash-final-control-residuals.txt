### Audit Findings & Verification Summary

1. **Cost factor $1/2$ and Value Function Hessian**:
   - The cost has the explicit factor $\frac{1}{2}$ on both terminal and running costs:
     $$J = \frac12\xi(t_f)^TQ_f\xi(t_f) + \frac12\int_{t_0}^{t_f} (\xi^TQ\xi + \nu^TR\nu)\,dt.$$
   - The text states: $V(\xi,t)=\frac12\xi^TS(t)\xi \implies D_\xi^2 V = S$. This is mathematically correct.
   - The note on omitting the factor of $1/2$ giving $2S$ and preserving the feedback gain $K = R^{-1}B^T S$ is exact.

2. **Riccati Identity & Closed-Loop Lyapunov Identity**:
   - Continuous Riccati differential equation:
     $$-\dot S = A^T S + S A - S B R^{-1} B^T S + Q.$$
   - With $A_{\mathrm{cl}} = A - BK = A - B R^{-1} B^T S$, we calculate:
     $$A_{\mathrm{cl}}^T S + S A_{\mathrm{cl}} = (A^T - S B R^{-1} B^T)S + S(A - B R^{-1} B^T S) = A^T S + S A - 2 S B R^{-1} B^T S.$$
     Substituting $-\dot S$:
     $$\dot S + A_{\mathrm{cl}}^T S + S A_{\mathrm{cl}} = -(A^T S + S A - S B R^{-1} B^T S + Q) + (A^T S + S A - 2 S B R^{-1} B^T S) = -Q - S B R^{-1} B^T S.$$
     Since $K = R^{-1}B^T S$, $K^T R K = S B R^{-1} R R^{-1} B^T S = S B R^{-1} B^T S$.
     Thus $\dot S + A_{\mathrm{cl}}^T S + S A_{\mathrm{cl}} = -Q - K^T R K$.
   - The identity in the text is mathematically exact.

3. **Discrete DDP vs. iLQR Hessian Contractions**:
   - For $x_{k+1} = F_k(x_k, u_k)$ and $\mathcal Q_k(x, u) = \ell_k(x, u) + V_{k+1}(F_k(x, u))$:
     Let $v = D_x V_{k+1}$ (row vector or 1D component gradient $v_i$). By the chain rule:
     $$D_u \mathcal Q_k = D_u \ell_k + v D_u F_k = \ell_u + v B_k.$$
     Differentiating with respect to $u$:
     $$\mathcal Q_{uu} = \ell_{uu} + B_k^T (D_x^2 V_{k+1}) B_k + \sum_i v_i D_{uu}^2 F_{k,i}.$$
     Similarly, $\mathcal Q_{ux} = \ell_{ux} + B_k^T (D_x^2 V_{k+1}) A_k + \sum_i v_i D_{ux}^2 F_{k,i}$.
   - The statement that full DDP retains $\sum_i v_i D^2 F_{k,i}$ while iLQR drops these second-order dynamics contractions is exact and standard.

4. **Feedforward Gain Signs and One-Step Example**:
   - Quadratic expansion: $\Delta \mathcal Q \approx \mathcal Q_u^T \delta u + \mathcal Q_x^T \delta x + \frac12 \delta u^T \mathcal Q_{uu} \delta u + \delta u^T \mathcal Q_{ux} \delta x + \frac12 \delta x^T \mathcal Q_{xx} \delta x$.
   - Minimizing with respect to $\delta u$:
     $$\mathcal Q_u + \mathcal Q_{uu}\delta u + \mathcal Q_{ux}\delta x = 0 \implies \delta u = -\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_u - \mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_{ux}\delta x.$$
   - Setting $k_k = -\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_u$ and $L_k = -\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_{ux}$ matches the definition and update $u_k = \bar u_k + \alpha k_k + L_k(x_k - \bar x_k)$.
   - In the example: $x_1 = x_0 + u$, $x_0 = 0$, $J = \frac12(x_1 - 1)^2 + \frac12 u^2 = \frac12(u - 1)^2 + \frac12 u^2 = u^2 - u + \frac12$.
     - At nominal $u=0$:
       $$\mathcal Q(u) = u^2 - u + \tfrac12 \implies \mathcal Q_u = -1, \quad \mathcal Q_{uu} = 2.$$
     - $k = -\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_u = - \frac{-1}{2} = 1/2$.
     - For step $u = 0 + 1/2 = 1/2$: $J(0) = 1/2$, $J(1/2) = \frac12(-1/2)^2 + \frac12(1/2)^2 = 1/8 + 1/8 = 1/4$.
   - Cost drops from $1/2$ to $1/4$. All signs and numbers are exact.

5. **Full Taylor Mixed Remainder**:
   - In Part IV, the integral remainder $R_2 = \int_0^1 (1-s) D^2f(\bar z + s\Delta z)[\Delta z, \Delta z]\,ds$ correctly captures the symmetric bilinear form on $\Delta z = (\Delta x, \Delta u)$.
   - The example $f(x,u) = xu$ at $(0,0)$ gives $Df(0,0) = 0$, $D^2f[\Delta z, \Delta z] = 2\Delta x \Delta u$, and $\int_0^1 (1-s) 2 \Delta x \Delta u\,ds = \Delta x \Delta u$.
   - The text emphasizes that mixed terms cannot be dropped, and notes that norms/bounds containing sums of squares validly bound bilinear/cross terms without false rejection.

6. **Finite-Horizon Error Bound and Perturbation Ratio**:
   - **Error bound**:
     With $\dot r = A(t)r + R_2(t)$, $r(t_0) = 0$,
     $$r(t) = \int_{t_0}^t \Phi(t,s) R_2(s)\,ds.$$
     With $\|\Phi(t,s)\| \le K e^{L(t-s)}$, $L \ge 0$, and $\|R_2(s)\| \le \frac12 H \|\Delta z(s)\|^2 \le \frac12 H C_0^2 \epsilon^2$:
     $$\|r(t)\| \le \int_{t_0}^t K e^{L(t-s)} \frac12 H C_0^2 \epsilon^2\,ds \le \frac12 K H C_0^2 \epsilon^2 \int_{t_0}^t e^{L(t-s)}\,ds.$$
     Over an interval of length $T$, since $e^{L(t-s)} \le e^{LT}$ for $0 \le t - s \le T$, bounding the integrand by $e^{LT}$ gives $\int_{t_0}^t e^{L(t-s)}\,ds \le T e^{LT}$.
     Hence $\|r(t)\| \le \frac12 K H C_0^2 T e^{LT}\epsilon^2$. The bound is rigorous.
   - **Perturbation Ratio**:
     For $\dot x = x^2, x(0) = \epsilon$:
     $$x(t) = \frac{\epsilon}{1 - \epsilon t}.$$
     Linearization at $\bar x = 0$ is $\dot{\hat\xi} = 0, \hat\xi(0) = \epsilon \implies \hat\xi(t) = \epsilon$.
     Residual:
     $$r(\epsilon, t) = x(t) - \hat\xi(t) = \frac{\epsilon}{1 - \epsilon t} - \epsilon = \frac{\epsilon - \epsilon(1 - \epsilon t)}{1 - \epsilon t} = \frac{\epsilon^2 t}{1 - \epsilon t}.$$
     Ratio of $r(2\epsilon, t)$ to $r(\epsilon, t)$:
     $$\frac{r(2\epsilon, t)}{r(\epsilon, t)} = \frac{\frac{(2\epsilon)^2 t}{1 - 2\epsilon t}}{\frac{\epsilon^2 t}{1 - \epsilon t}} = 4 \frac{1 - \epsilon t}{1 - 2\epsilon t}.$$
     This is exact.

### Specific Defect Found in Part III

#### 1. Inconsistent Expansion Indexing for Value Function / Successor State
* **Location**: Part III, under section **"Why a Feedforward Update Is Essential"** and **"iLQR and DDP Use Different Dynamics Derivatives"**.
* **Issue**:
  Under "Why a Feedforward Update Is Essential":
  $$\mathcal Q_k(x,u)=\ell_k(x,u)+V_{k+1}(F_k(x,u))$$
  Then in the next section:
  > "Let $A_k=D_xF_k$, $B_k=D_uF_k$, and let $v=D_xV_{k+1}$ and $P=D_x^2V_{k+1}$ be evaluated at the nominal successor."

  Notice that in the expansion:
  $$\mathcal Q_x^T\delta x+\mathcal Q_u^T\delta u$$
  and
  $$k_k=-\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_u,\qquad L_k=-\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_{ux}$$
  $\mathcal Q_u$ and $\mathcal Q_x$ are written without transposes in the gain definitions ($k_k = -\mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1}\mathcal Q_u$, which assumes $\mathcal Q_u$ is a column vector of dimension $m\times 1$), whereas the Taylor expansion writes $\mathcal Q_x^T \delta x + \mathcal Q_u^T \delta u$, treating $\mathcal Q_x, \mathcal Q_u$ as column vectors.
  However, in:
  $$D_u \mathcal Q_k$$
  the text defines:
  > "and let $v=D_xV_{k+1}$ and $P=D_x^2V_{k+1}$ be evaluated at the nominal successor. The second-order chain rule gives, for example,
  > $$
  > \begin{aligned}
  > \mathcal Q_{uu}
  >  &=\ell_{uu}+B_k^TPB_k+\sum_i v_iD_{uu}^2F_{k,i},\\
  > \mathcal Q_{ux}
  >  &=\ell_{ux}+B_k^TPA_k+\sum_i v_iD_{ux}^2F_{k,i}.
  > \end{aligned}
  > $$"

  If $v = D_x V_{k+1}$ is evaluated at the nominal successor $\bar x_{k+1} = F_k(\bar x_k, \bar u_k)$, $v_i$ are the components of the gradient. In standard vector calculus notation where $v = \nabla_x V_{k+1}$ (column vector), $v_i$ denotes $[\nabla_x V_{k+1}]_i$. If $v = D_x V_{k+1}$ is a Jacobian row vector ($1 \times n$), $v_i$ is its $i$-th entry. Either way, $\sum_i v_i D^2 F_{k,i}$ is mathematically standard and unambiguous.

#### 2. Minor Dimension Inconsistency in Gradient Term $\mathcal Q_u$ vs. $k_k$
* **Location**: Part III, "Why a Feedforward Update Is Essential":
  $$\text{linear terms }\mathcal Q_x^T\delta x+\mathcal Q_u^T\delta u$$
  Here $\mathcal Q_u$ is defined as a column vector ($m \times 1$) so that $\mathcal Q_u^T \delta u$ is a scalar.
  In the example:
  > "At nominal $u=0$, $\mathcal Q_u=-1$, $\mathcal Q_{uu}=2$, hence $k=1/2$."
  Here $\mathcal Q_u$ is scalar $-1$, and $k = - \mathcal Q_{uu}^{-1} \mathcal Q_u = - (2)^{-1} (-1) = 1/2$. This matches completely.
