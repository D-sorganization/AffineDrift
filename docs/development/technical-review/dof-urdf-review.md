## Decisions and Derivations

The chapter now follows anatomy → admissible motion → club task → applied force → executable model → experimental interpretation. The central insight is that these maps answer different questions. An internal configuration change can preserve present club pose while changing future acceleration authority; an internal grip-force change can preserve the club's resultant wrench while changing arm loads. Neither observation alone identifies a neural strategy, energy saving or injury reduction. Both become useful hypotheses when their predicted observables, intervention and comparison event are specified.

### Mobility and Closure

The fixed-pelvis upper-body example has nine physical bodies, eight compound joints, four three-DOF and four two-DOF joints. Their freedoms sum to20. With the pelvis as ground, the nominal spatial count is6(9−1)−28=20; independence of the constraint rows is stated. Adding a separate ground and fixed pelvis joint adds six body coordinates and six constraints, leaving the count unchanged. XML intermediate frames are representation choices, not additional anatomical segments.

Add the club once. A rigid lead-hand attachment adds zero mobility; a full-rank trail-hand pose closure removes six, giving14. Alternatively, an independent club gives26, with two independent rank-six rigid grips giving26−12=14. Two compliant grips impose force laws and leave26 configuration freedoms; they do not create two club bodies. Legs add12 to this selected fixed-pelvis model, a floating base adds6, and one free club adds6:44 before stacked constraints. Four independent rank-six foot/grip closures would leave20 locally. This last count is explicitly conditional on rank and the contact mode. The original introductory alternative also totals32, not35, but its pelvis/spine/arm allocation differs; equal totals do not make models identical. Quaternion coordinate count, velocity dimension, Euler chart singularities and friction/contact feasibility are treated separately.

### Task Geometry and Dynamic Redundancy

For admissible velocities v=Zη, task velocity is JZη. Its regular local redundancy is d−rank(JZ). A seven-DOF open arm has one local null direction for a regular six-dimensional pose task and four for a rank-three position task. The closed14-DOF upper body has eight for a regular pose task. These are not universal measured golfer counts. Local level-set dimensions require constant rank; joint limits, singularities and disconnected branches can invalidate a global manifold picture. An instantaneous null step preserves the task only to first order unless integrated along the changing distribution.

The elbow swivel is derived from intersecting spheres. With shoulder/wrist distance D and segment lengths a,b, the circle center along the shoulder–wrist line is c=(a²−b²+D²)/(2D), with radius sqrt(a²−c²). Elbow flexion satisfies cosβ=(D²−a²−b²)/(2ab), so it remains constant around this ideal circle. For a=.30,b=.25,D=.40m, c=.234375m, radius=.1872654783m and cosβ=.05. The published radius is correctly rounded to.18727m. A draft's.18700 and then.18726 were corrected against this calculation. A wrist-orientation requirement calls for coordinated rotations; isolated elbow flexion is not the swivel.

For a full-row-rank wide J, the Euclidean pseudoinverse is Jᵀ(JJᵀ)⁻¹. Exact task realization requires membership in the range; SVD, damping and unit/metric choices are distinguished. Minimum Euclidean joint speed is not automatically minimum kinetic energy.

With unconstrained torque-driven dynamics M v̇+h=Bu, the full-state input map is G=[0;M⁻¹B]. It is injective when B=I, even if the club task is redundant. Differentiating ẏ=Jv yields ÿ=JM⁻¹Bu−JM⁻¹h+J̇v. At fixed state the torque-null condition is JM⁻¹Bδu=0. For M=diag(2,1), J=[1,1], the vector(1,−1) is velocity-null but produces task acceleration−1/2 when used as torque. This elementary counterexample prevents a velocity/force category error from being hidden inside a human model.

Define Λ=(JM⁻¹Jᵀ)⁻¹, J̄=M⁻¹JᵀΛ, N=I−J̄J. Then JN=0, while the torque projector is Nᵀ and JM⁻¹Nᵀ=0. The example gives J̄=(1/3,2/3)ᵀ. The chapter proves the identity and states that J̄ ẏ minimizes instantaneous kinetic energy subject to the task velocity. A task force still must account for drift and desired acceleration. A posture-gradient term makes only its own contribution to the posture cost nonpositive; the concurrent task term can increase that cost. Preserving a prescribed instantaneous club speed cannot increase that same speed at the same instant. Future speed, bounds, trajectory and impact-event effects require forward analysis.

For stationary ideal constraints M v̇+h=Bu+Aᵀλ, A v̇+Ȧv=0, eliminate λ using K=AM⁻¹Aᵀ and W=M⁻¹−M⁻¹AᵀK⁻¹AM⁻¹. Then v̇=W(Bu−h)−M⁻¹AᵀK⁻¹Ȧv and the task input map is JWB. Redundant constraints require a suitable reduced/generalized formulation. Underactuation can prevent arbitrary projected generalized forces from being realizable. Ideal stationary reaction power sums to zero, but interbody power exchange, compliant storage and prescribed moving constraints require their own accounting. Removing specified torque commands retains the passive, activation and elastic state assigned by the declared intervention.

### Anthropometry, Sensitivity and Identification

De Leva1996 adjusts living-subject gamma-scan segment estimates, not a new cadaver-measurement dataset. Table4 selected male mass fractions are2.71% upper arm,1.62% forearm,.61% hand,14.16% thigh,4.33% shank,1.37% foot and43.46% whole trunk. Each limb fraction refers to one segment. Whole trunk cannot be summed again with its subdivisions, nor can a generic thorax value justify an independently assigned lumbar partition. The male forearm longitudinal radius of gyration is.121L, hence I=.014641mL²; failing to square the radius coefficient is a substantial error. Segment endpoint conventions affect normalized coefficients. The executable model deliberately uses derived cylinder properties rather than presenting population means as individual anatomy.

With mass factor sm and geometrically similar length factor sL, inertia scales as sm sL². Fixed density makes sm=sL³ and inertia scale as sL⁵. Repartitioned segments require recomputed mass, COM and inertia. Sensitivity differentiates a declared model and input history: Ṡ=fxS+fp. Feedback adds fuπx and fuπp terms. Reoptimizing the controller answers a different question from replaying fixed controls. For an impact event H(x(t*,p),p,t*)=0, t*p=−(HxS+Hp)/(Hxf+Ht), assuming a nonzero denominator; terminal output derivatives include the event-time shift. Grazing, switching contacts and resets need additional analysis.

The hypothetical derivative−.05(m/s)/kg with ±.2kg perturbation gives ∓.01m/s, about.0222% of45m/s. It is not an observed golf sensitivity or confidence interval. Correlated uncertainties propagate as sΣsᵀ locally. A convenient±20% sweep does not certify universal robustness, and finite-time amplification is not proof of chaos. Club kinematics, full-body motion, external wrenches, net generalized torque, muscle allocation and fitted control cost have different identifiability limits. Positive rescaling of an objective preserves its minimizers; fitting a trajectory does not uniquely recover a brain objective. Held-out conditions and controlled perturbations can distinguish explanations more effectively.
