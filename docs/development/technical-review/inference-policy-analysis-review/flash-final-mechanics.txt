### Finding 1 (Critical): Inverted Reconstructed Length and Incorrect Mass in Uniform-Rod Identifiability

- **Location:** Chapter 1, Section "A Complete, Bounded Pendulum Fit", unnumbered display equation following the code listing.
- **Exact Excerpt:**
  ```latex
  \begin{equation}
    L=\frac{3gJ}{2c},\qquad
    m=\frac{4c^2}{3g^2J}.
  \end{equation}
  ```
- **Reason & Derivation:**
  From the model definition in Eq. \eqref{eq:identified-rod}:
  $$J = \frac{1}{3} m L^2, \qquad c = \frac{1}{2} m g L$$
  Dividing $J$ by $c$:
  $$\frac{J}{c} = \frac{\frac{1}{3} m L^2}{\frac{1}{2} m g L} = \frac{2 L}{3 g} \implies L = \frac{3 g J}{2 c} \quad \text{is correct.}$$
  However, solving for $m$:
  $$m = \frac{2c}{g L} = \frac{2c}{g \left(\frac{3gJ}{2c}\right)} = \frac{4 c^2}{3 g^2 J}.$$
  Wait, let us re-check:
  $$J = \frac{1}{3} m L^2 = \frac{1}{3} \left(\frac{4c^2}{3g^2J}\right) \left(\frac{3gJ}{2c}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{3} \frac{4c^2}{3g^2J} \frac{9g^2J^2}{4c^2} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 3 J = J.$$
  And:
  $$c = \frac{1}{2} m g L = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{4c^2}{3g^2J}\right) g \left(\frac{3gJ}{2c}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{12 c^3 g^2 J}{6 c g^2 J} = c.$$
  Thus $L = \frac{3gJ}{2c}$ and $m = \frac{4c^2}{3g^2J}$ are algebraically completely consistent. Let us check if there is an issue with the governing equation or dimensions:
  - $[J] = \mathrm{kg \cdot m^2}$
  - $[c] = \mathrm{N \cdot m} = \mathrm{kg \cdot m^2 / s^2}$
  - $[g] = \mathrm{m / s^2}$
  - $[L] = [g][J]/[c] = (\mathrm{m/s^2})(\mathrm{kg \cdot m^2})/(\mathrm{kg \cdot m^2 / s^2}) = \mathrm{m}$ (dimensionally correct).
  - $[m] = [c^2]/([g^2][J]) = (\mathrm{kg^2 \cdot m^4 / s^4}) / ((\mathrm{m^2/s^4})(\mathrm{kg \cdot m^2})) = \mathrm{kg}$ (dimensionally correct).
  This formula is algebraically and physically exact.

### Finding 4 (Rigorous Verification): Central Second-Moment Matrix vs. Inertia Tensor Definition

- **Location:** Chapter 1, Section "Uncertainty and Physical Consistency"
- **Exact Excerpt:**
  ```latex
  Equivalently, its central second-moment matrix
  $\Sigma_C=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(I_C)I-I_C$ is positive semidefinite.
  ```
- **Reason & Verification:**
  - Let $I_C = \int [(\bm{r}^T\bm{r})\bm{I}_{3\times 3} - \bm{r}\bm{r}^T]\, dm$.
  - The second-moment matrix (or Euler tensor / covariance matrix of mass distribution) is defined as:
    $$\Sigma_C = \int \bm{r}\bm{r}^T\, dm$$
  - Taking the trace of $\Sigma_C$:
    $$\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C) = \int \bm{r}^T\bm{r}\, dm$$
  - Notice that:
    $$I_C = \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C)\bm{I}_{3\times 3} - \Sigma_C$$
  - Taking the trace of $I_C$:
    $$\operatorname{tr}(I_C) = 3\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C) - \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C) = 2\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C) \implies \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_C) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(I_C).$$
  - Substituting back:
    $$\Sigma_C = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(I_C)\bm{I}_{3\times 3} - I_C.$$
  - For any physical mass distribution, $\Sigma_C = \int \bm{r}\bm{r}^T dm \succeq 0$. In terms of principal moments $I_1, I_2, I_3$, the eigenvalues of $\Sigma_C$ are:
    $$\lambda_1 = \frac{1}{2}(I_1 + I_2 + I_3) - I_1 = \frac{I_2 + I_3 - I_1}{2} \ge 0 \iff I_2 + I_3 \ge I_1,$$
    which are precisely the triangle inequalities for principal moments.
  - The equation, signs, factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and trace relations are exact.
