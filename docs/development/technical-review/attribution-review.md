# Physics Energy and Hip Citation Review

Epic #4009; corpus #4021; Physics batch #4054; issue #4239. This is a targeted paired correction of the entire energy-budget section in ch14_complete_swing and the hip-motion interpretation in ch22_anatomy_joint_modeling. Neither complete chapter is claimed audited.

Nesbit and Serrano's primary paper defines club work divided by reconstructed total body joint work, with four amateur cases and ratios 20.2–26.8%. The source does not measure metabolism or establish elite metabolic superiority. The paired energy section now derives a declared mechanical balance, distinguishes signed/positive/absorbed actuator work, retains initial and final storage, accounts for moving boundaries and residual actuators, and treats collision separately. Ideal stationary ground contact transmits momentum without implying dissipation. Club energy left after impact is not heat. A 100 + 500 − 80 − 20 = 500 J budget independently illustrates why different denominators give different ratios, without pretending those ratios are the source's club-work measure. Drift is a specified zero-input evolution, not a new energy source or a metabolic measurement.

Cheetham's original study computes X-factor from pelvis and T3 turn near transition. It cannot substantiate the cited lead-hip 35–50-degree requirement. Both anatomy passages now distinguish global pelvis turn from femur-relative-to-pelvis motion and require declared frames, rotation sequence, sign, reference posture and compatible assessment conditions. The surrounding chapter's anatomical ranges and detailed tissue claims await full review; their generic values are explicitly not individual fixed limits.

Primary evidence was revisited at the author/publisher PDFs: https://www.jssm.org/volume04/iss4/cap/jssm-04-520.pdf (Methods work definition, Results Tables 2–3, discussion) and https://www.philcheetham.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Stretching-the-X-Factor-Paper.pdf (sensor placement and X-factor definition). No new source figure is reproduced. This follow-up does not expand the prior source-access claims to uninspected figures or full biomechanical validation.

Adjacent readings also identify unsupported phase-specific DCR numbers, constraint-work and eccentric-action generalizations, and follow-through neural claims in ch14, plus overly categorical anatomical idealizations in ch22. These are explicitly pending the full chapter reviews. This targeted issue does not certify those neighboring passages.
