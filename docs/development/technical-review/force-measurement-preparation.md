## Primary-Source Follow-up on September 22

- Bincalar et al. (2025), DOI 10.3390/s25051283: obtained the author-repository PDF at https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/499548/1/sensors-25-01283.pdf (local bincalar-2025.pdf/.txt). Read the introduction, model framing, Section 5.1, equations 35–36, Table 1, discussion and conclusion. The three percentages are simulation results for an 8-by-8, 48 cm square mat against a 512-by-512 reference. The metric combines coordinate-relative x/y percentage errors and then averages; it is origin-dependent, not a millimetre error or percentage of mat width. The 3.93% footprint method uses the uniform layout; it is not an extra sequential improvement on the 5.47% optimized layout. Physical validation is future work. The paper's introductory identification of COP with projected COM is itself unsafe; do not copy it. Keep a brief bounded description, not a generic commercial-device error claim.
- Davidson et al., PMID 40704489, DOI 10.1080/00140139.2025.2536545: primary abstract, online 2025, issue publication 2026. Thirty-nine participants, occupational lifting/packaging/palletising/overhead tasks, three-compartment insoles with an estimator and drift correction; COP RMSE 1.7–3.4 cm applies to these tasks/device. Not golf or all wireless insoles.
- Cudejko et al. (2023), PMID 37696840, DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-41622-3: primary abstract. ML shortening has an explicit exception for jumps. AP similarity and lower vertical forces are protocol-specific; reliable repeated readings do not imply interchangeability with plates.
- Leary et al. (2012), PMID 22797001, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31826791bf: primary abstract. The article mislabels force at 150 ms (r=.46/.47, p=.07/.06) as rate of force development. Actual reported 0–150 ms RFD correlations are r=.38/.36, p=.11/.12, n=12. Neither nonsignificance nor authors' positive interpretation establishes a general absence/presence of a causal training effect. This is an isometric mid-thigh pull test, not swing GRF slope.
- Johansen et al. (2023), PMID 37585706, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2023.2245998: primary abstract, 21 national-level male golfers. Positive associations include leg-press RFD, strength and power. It does not justify saying RFD generally fails or replacing one observational association with causal proof. Abstract does not supply .89; omit that exact coefficient unless full paper verifies it. The existing linked record is 2023, not a combined 2023/2026 citation.
- Jones et al. (2024; online 2021), PMID 34455916, DOI 10.1080/14763141.2021.1965649: primary abstract, 104 amateurs, driver/5-iron, within-golfer variability lower than between-golfer variability. Also reports lower variability with skill in some components. Does not close the argument that every personal signature is optimal.
- Jones et al. (2023), PMID 37149899, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2023.2210852: separate primary abstract, 104 amateurs. Cluster optima depend on criterion; continuous PCA supports a multidimensional continuum. Lower handicap/higher speed tended toward front-foot patterns and faster early-downswing transfer. Must include this counterevidence to treating Ball's two styles as natural fixed categories; association still is not an intervention prescription.
- Han et al. (2019), PMID 31042142, DOI 10.1080/14763141.2019.1586983: primary abstract, 63 highly skilled male golfers, three club conditions. Distinguishes GRF, pivoting and foot-contact moments. Lead-foot force components and trail-foot forward/backward force also correlate with speed. Thus the article's exclusive torsional skill signal is overstated; do not equate pivoting moment with per-foot free moment without consulting full definitions. No basis for 'largest published sample'.

All other retained quantitative study and manufacturer claims still require their own primary-source verification. Abstract access is recorded as such; do not turn this preparation into a completed scientific review.

- Worsfold et al. (2008), full primary PDF obtained at https://www.jssm.org/volume07/iss3/cap/jssm-07-408.pdf; read methods and Table 1/results. The 18.2 versus 14.2 Nm values are maximum-to-minimum torque range, not peak free moment or angular impulse. Driver-only front-foot range is 38.9–43.5 Nm; not every club/table cell. Model Kistler 9851, natural turf with 35 mm surface offset, 1000 Hz verified. The article's jssm XML links currently redirect to a generic journal page; use the working PDF. The guessed 2007 v6n4-4 PDF is a different paper and must NOT support golf claims.
- Karatsidis et al. (2017), full primary XML from Europe PMC PMC5298648 saved locally. The listed six correlations/errors are Table 11 NORMAL WALKING results for 11 healthy subjects, not golf and not a universal ranking. GRMs are about the ground projection of the ankle, not COP free moments. The smooth-transition curves were fit using all subjects/trials of the same dataset (Section 2.4/Figure 3), so not a held-out golf prediction benchmark. Exact rRMSE definition points to Ren (2008); do not casually label it percent body weight.
- Li et al. (2026), DOI 10.3390/biomimetics11030159, full XML from Europe PMC PMC13023547 saved locally. Methods: 48 right-handed professional golfers, ten driver swings each, subject-level ten-fold validation; 60 Hz IMUs and GRF reference low-pass filtered to 6 Hz. Reported R-squared .94 is for the evaluated filtered indoor protocol. Does not validate torque, impact transients, untested populations or outdoor transfer. Bidirectional recurrent architecture alone does not establish real-time causal forecasting. The paper's time normalization does not itself create independent trials; retain the subject-level split as the relevant safeguard.
