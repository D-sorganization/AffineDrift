1 Abstract

High-speed golf swings present an extreme example of a drift-dominated nonlinear dynamic system, where segmental angular velocities exceed the neuromechanical bandwidth of voluntary control. To formalize this, we define the Drift–Control Ratio (DCR):

[ (t) = ]

for a nonlinear control-affine system (=f(x)+g(x)u) applied to a multibody model of the golfer–club system. We derive closed-form expressions for the drift and control contributions of a 3-link shoulder–forearm–club model and examine controllability using Lie brackets. We show analytically and numerically that DCR grows by two orders of magnitude from takeaway to impact. This growth places the downswing firmly in a drift-dominated regime, restricting effective control inputs to early parts of the swing. Finally, we analyze the stability of impact dynamics and demonstrate how DCR directly predicts clubface closure variability, offering a quantitative rationale for early structural work in golf coaching.

2 1. Introduction

The modern golf swing is an exceptionally fast whole-body movement. Elite players routinely generate:

  • Clubhead speeds: ( 4050; )
  • Wrist angular velocities: ( > 2000^/ )
  • Torso rotation velocities: ( 700900^/ )

Human reaction time (150–250 ms) and electromechanical delay (30–50 ms) are far too slow to influence such rapid movements, implying the downswing is open-loop and pre-structured.

This paper provides the first integrated:

  • multibody derivation of drift and control dynamics,
  • nonlinear controllability analysis, and
  • impact stability characterization

framed around a single measure: the Drift–Control Ratio (DCR).

8 7. Numerical Illustration: Drift vs Control Magnitude

import numpy as np import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

t = np.linspace(0, 1, 500)

drift = 0.2 + 2.5 * np.exp(-((t - 0.80)2) / 0.002) control = 0.6 - 0.3 * np.exp(-((t - 0.10)2) / 0.01)
