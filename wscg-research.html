WSCG2024 Research

Evaluation of the Effect of Momentum on Interaction Forces in a Linked System

Research Overview

This research was presented at the WSCG2024/ISEA conference, exploring the effect of momentum on interaction forces in linked systems. The work investigates how passive momentum contributions affect hand-club interaction forces during dynamic motion, providing insights into the mechanical behavior of coupled multibody systems.

Key Findings

The research demonstrates that passive momentum contributes substantially to both normal and axial hand forces during dynamic motion. Key findings include:

  • Passive momentum can reverse direction prior to the reversal of total force
  • The lead hand experiences larger passive axial force contributions than the trail hand late in motion
  • Passive momentum contributions can exceed total forces temporarily, indicating competing active and passive effects
  • These findings validate the theoretical prediction that drift forces do not vanish when torque inputs are removed

Methodology

The research utilized a forward-dynamics implementation in MATLAB/Simulink, featuring a planar two-hand, multi-link system with a flexible beam shaft. The model incorporated "kill switches" at every joint to compute Zero Torque Counterfactual (ZTCF) trajectories, allowing isolation of passive momentum contributions.
