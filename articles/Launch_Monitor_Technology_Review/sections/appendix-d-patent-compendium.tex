\chapter{Patent Portfolio Compendium}
\label{app:patents}

This appendix organizes selected launch-monitor patent publications by
technical topic and named organization. It includes US grants, published
US applications and explicitly identified foreign or PCT documents.
The links provide a route to the disclosures; they do not establish an
exhaustive portfolio search or a commercial product's implementation.
TrackMan's marking notice lists 47 US numbers at the October 1, 2026
check~\cite{trackmanpatents}; Uneekor's product-specific marking list
expressly notes that other products and applications may exist
outside its table~\cite{uneekorpatents}.

The priority column records the indexed priority year of each row's
leading document. It is distinct from that application's filing year
and the document's publication year, and it does not assign a common
priority to every related number in the notes. A claim's entitlement to
priority and a patent's term require separate analysis. Descriptions
identify technical subject matter rather than the complete limitations
of every claim. Neither a matching title nor a shared inventor proves
common ownership, a continuation relationship or use in a named product.

Two attribution corrections surfaced by this sweep are worth flagging
prominently: (i) the widely cited ``radar + image data 3D tracking''
family US10596416 / US11697046 / US12128275 belongs to
\textbf{Topgolf Sweden AB (Toptracer)}, not TrackMan; and (ii) Full
Swing's launch-monitor publication US2020/0147470A1 corresponds to
the grant \patent{US11311789B2}. The Garmin and USGA entries below
separate the disclosed mechanism from unsupported product and
company-portfolio attributions.

\section{Others and Foundational Radar Art}

\textbf{United States Golf Association:} \patent{US6186002B1}
(indexed priority 1998) is assigned in the publication record to the
USGA, not Acushnet. It describes fitting lift and drag coefficients
from launches with specified initial conditions and timed ballistic
light-screen crossings, then relating those coefficients to Reynolds
number and spin ratio. It is a useful experimental-modeling reference,
not evidence that arbitrary launch-monitor outputs uniquely identify
the aerodynamic coefficients.

\textbf{AccuSport:} \patent{US7209576B2} (indexed priority 2002;
original assignee AccuSport International Inc.) describes golf-ball
image processing, including marked-surface spin estimation and image
calibration. Its existence corrects the earlier claim that no
attributable AccuSport patent was found.

\textbf{SkyTrak / SkyHawke:} \patent{US12515116B2} (2023, swing-tag +
launch-monitor fusion display) is one relevant disclosure. This catalog
does not establish the complete SkyHawke portfolio or the patent marking
of every SkyTrak model. No verified entries for \textbf{Zelocity,
GolfTek, Ernest Sports or ProTee United} are supplied here; that search
limitation establishes neither the absence of patents nor the technical
ancestry of their products.
\textbf{Voice Caddie (Ucomm):} \patent{US10338212B2} (2014, portable
Doppler swing/ball analyzer). \textbf{Sports Sensors (Dilz):}
\patent{US6079269A} (1997 Swing Speed Radar),
\patent{US6898971B2}, \patent{US8007367B2} (club speed + tempo) ---
all expired, free art. \textbf{Weibel Scientific} (TrackMan's
engineering origin): \patent{EP1735637B1} (2004 multi-antenna CW
Doppler tracking), MFCW ranging family (2014). \textbf{Applied
Concepts (Stalker):} \patent{US10935657B2} (2019, baseball spin via
Doppler micro-modulation) and the autocorrelation continuation ---
directly relevant prior art for radar spin outside golf.

\begin{implication}
The technical value of this catalog is comparative. Radar spin
estimation, optical surface matching, trajectory inversion and
multisensor fusion use different observations and assumptions even
when their outputs have the same name. Read each disclosure for the
measured signal, calibration, ambiguity resolution and validation
conditions, then distinguish that evidence from an implemented
product's performance. A grant is not a benchmark result.

The dates and topic groupings do not establish freedom to operate.
Expiration of a particular patent does not clear every implementation
of a broad technique or every related claim. The USPTO distinguishes
the right to exclude others from permission to practice an invention;
rights are territorial, and term depends on the applicable filing
basis and other conditions.\footnote{USPTO, \emph{Patent Essentials},
\url{https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/essentials}.}
A design-specific review must identify the relevant jurisdiction,
claims and current records. The research map in \cref{sec:fto} should
be read with those limits, rather than as a clearance decision.
\end{implication}
