\chapter{Patent Portfolio Compendium}
\label{app:patents}

This appendix organizes selected launch-monitor patent publications by technical topic and named organization.
It includes US grants, published US applications and explicitly identified foreign or PCT documents.
The links provide a route to the disclosures; they do not establish an exhaustive portfolio search or a commercial product's implementation.
TrackMan's marking notice lists 47 US numbers at the October 1, 2026 check~\cite{trackmanpatents}; Uneekor's product-specific marking list expressly notes that other products and applications may exist outside its table~\cite{uneekorpatents}.

The priority column records the indexed priority year of each row's leading document.
It is distinct from that application's filing year and the document's publication year, and it does not assign a common priority to every related number in the notes.
A claim's entitlement to priority and a patent's term require separate analysis.
Descriptions identify technical subject matter rather than the complete limitations of every claim.
Neither a matching title nor a shared inventor proves common ownership, a continuation relationship or use in a named product.
\emph{Also} denotes another technical reference, not identical claim scope or verified family membership.
Publication of an application is a document type, not an assertion that prosecution remains pending.

Two attribution corrections surfaced by this sweep are worth flagging prominently: (i) the widely cited ``radar + image data 3D tracking'' publications US10596416 / US11697046 / US12128275 identify \textbf{Topgolf Sweden AB (Toptracer)}, not TrackMan; and (ii) Full Swing's launch-monitor publication US2020/0147470A1 corresponds to the grant \patent{US11311789B2}.
The Garmin and USGA entries below separate the disclosed mechanism from unsupported product and company-portfolio attributions.

\section{Others and Foundational Radar Art}

\textbf{United States Golf Association:} \patent{US6186002B1} (indexed priority 1998) is assigned in the publication record to the USGA, not Acushnet.
It describes fitting lift and drag coefficients from launches with specified initial conditions and timed ballistic light-screen crossings, then relating those coefficients to Reynolds number and spin ratio.
It is a useful experimental-modeling reference, not evidence that arbitrary launch-monitor outputs uniquely identify the aerodynamic coefficients.

\textbf{AccuSport:} \patent{US7209576B2} (indexed priority 2002; original assignee AccuSport International Inc.) describes golf-ball image processing, including marked-surface spin estimation and image calibration.

\textbf{SkyTrak / SkyHawke:} \patent{US12515116B2} (2023, swing-tag + launch-monitor fusion display) is one relevant disclosure.
This catalog does not establish the complete SkyHawke portfolio or the patent marking of every SkyTrak model.
No verified entries for \textbf{Zelocity, GolfTek, Ernest Sports or ProTee United} are supplied here; that search limitation establishes neither the absence of patents nor the technical ancestry of their products.
\textbf{Voice Caddie (Ucomm):} \patent{US10338212B2} (2014, portable Doppler swing/ball analyzer).
\textbf{Sports Sensors (Dilz):} \patent{US6079269A} (1997) describes a short-range sports-object speed sensor, including a ball-catching application; it should not be labeled the Swing Speed Radar product patent.
\patent{US6898971B2} (2000) addresses short-range speed measurement, including clubhead speed; \patent{US8007367B2} (2005) explicitly addresses swing duration and clubhead speed.
These are different measurement tasks.

\textbf{Weibel Scientific:} \patent{EP1735637B1} (2004) describes simultaneous CW and FMCW tracking with three receivers, phase-based angles and maintained track records.
Calling it only a CW-speed method omits a material part of the arrangement.
\textbf{Applied Concepts:} \patent{US10935657B2} (2019) estimates projectile spin from radar spectral shoulder frequencies adjacent to a peak.
Its relevance extends beyond golf; the disclosure is not evidence of a particular product's implemented accuracy.

\begin{implication}
The catalog compares observations and assumptions behind similarly named outputs.
Radar periodicity, image rotation and trajectory fitting need different calibration and ambiguity tests; a grant establishes no accuracy benchmark.
For each implementation, retain the measured signals and model dependencies when reporting derived or estimated quantities.
The rights boundaries in \cref{sec:fto} apply throughout this appendix.
\end{implication}
