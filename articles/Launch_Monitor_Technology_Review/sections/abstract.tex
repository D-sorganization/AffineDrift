\noindent
This review surveys the measurement technology behind commercial golf launch
monitors --- Doppler-radar systems (TrackMan, FlightScope, Garmin~R10, Full
Swing KIT), photometric camera systems (Foresight GCQuad/GC3, Uneekor,
SkyTrak, ProTee~VX, Garmin~R50), and hybrid radar+camera architectures
that combine these sensing families --- with emphasis on \emph{how each system
measures or infers club-delivery parameters} (club speed, club path, attack
angle, face angle, dynamic loft, impact location) \emph{and ball-launch
parameters} (ball speed, launch angles, spin rate, spin axis). Primary
sources include selected patent disclosures (TrackMan/Tuxen radar spin and
spin-axis methods, FlightScope/EDH radar-spin methods,
Wintriss/Foresight photometric methods, and Acushnet optical
launch-monitor designs), regulatory (FCC) filings, manufacturer technical
documentation, and the peer-reviewed validation literature. The review
develops the underlying physics --- the D-plane impact model, oblique-impact
spin generation, gear effect, and golf-ball aerodynamics --- as the common
mathematical framework for interpreting launch-monitor outputs, and closes with a technical map of patent disclosures and design guidance for implementers building or evaluating a system.
The central organizing principle is the provenance of each reported quantity: the observations, calibration and model assumptions that support it.
Combining radar and optical observations can constrain different parts of the inference problem, but does not automatically make every output directly measured or independently validated.
Early optical disclosures illustrate surface-image rotation and club-marker geometry; their age does not establish implementation accuracy or design-specific rights clearance.

This document is written to be vendor- and project-neutral. Where a
specific product is named it is as evidence --- a published definition, a
measured tolerance, a patent claim --- not as an endorsement or a design
target.
