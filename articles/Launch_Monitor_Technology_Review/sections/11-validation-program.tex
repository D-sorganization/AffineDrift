\chapter[Governed Analytics and Validation Program]{Governed Analytics\\and Validation Program}
\label{ch:validation-program}

The distinction between a useful analytics platform and a scientific
vendor comparison is the evidence design. A large collection of launch
records can support exploratory model checking without supporting the
stronger claim that one device disagrees with another.
At the revisions pinned below, the repositories associated with this review use a two-release program with a
versioned data contract, explicit use eligibility, and fail-closed
statistics. The program is tracked by the public
\href{https://github.com/D-sorganization/Tools/issues/4583}{Tools program
epic}. UpstreamDrift is the canonical generic contract and evidence-envelope
authority. Tools owns the current specialized grouped estimators: Python is
the desktop calculation authority and React is a contract-tested browser twin.
Those grouped operations are not yet UpstreamDrift API operations, so that
boundary is published rather than obscured by a cross-service parity claim.

\section{Qualified Corpus Snapshot}

The controlled authority snapshot contains 261,666 rows from 27 traceable
sources. Size alone is misleading. Only 13,855 rows contain finite,
non-imputed values for all five primary launch inputs (ball speed, launch
angle, launch direction, spin rate, and spin axis). Before the per-source
minimum-size gate, 13,647 rows also contain carry, 13,532 lateral outcome,
9,850 descent angle, and 9,283 flight time. The strict model cohort contains
13,843 rows from 15 source families with complete launch inputs and at least one supported observed outcome.
Each outcome comparison then uses its own eligible subset; 13,843 is not the sample size for every metric.
Eligibility is evaluated by source, metric, provenance,
and intended use; a blank field is represented as \emph{unavailable}, never
silently filled merely to increase sample size.

The largest source is a 236,590-row ShotLink-derived table. It has no
complete five-input launch rows and no observed carry values. Its rights and
transformation lineage also require separate approval. It is therefore
quarantined: the source is \textbf{not eligible for vendor-model training},
public redistribution, or vendor-performance claims. It may be retained
internally for provenance review and for analyses that are independently
shown to match its permitted use. This rule prevents a large but unsuitable
table from dominating the apparent evidence base.

At the present qualification gate, no vendor-labelled surrogate is currently eligible
for production training. The corpus contains 11,699 TrackMan-labelled
rows and 9,298 strict launch-input rows, but it lacks a trusted repeating
player or session grouping. Foresight has four rows (two strict); FlightScope
has 2,794 rows but no strict five-input rows. The historical TrackMan surrogate
used unique shot identifiers as partitions, which cannot protect against
player, session, or source leakage; it is retained for audit with status
\path{retired_non_group_safe} and cannot be loaded, queried, or retrained.
A future model that passes the group-safe gate would be described as
\emph{vendor-comparable on its qualified source}, not as a reproduction or
certification of proprietary vendor software.

\section{Release A: Qualified Analytics Platform}

Release A delivers the professional analysis surface on data that passes the
declared gate. Its scope includes unit-aware scatter and distribution views,
model residuals, arbitrary variable relationships, within-player and
between-player covariance, session trends, uncertainty, exportable plots,
and reproducible project files. Player-level analysis requires an explicit
player identity column. Identity is never inferred from session, club, row
order, filename, or directory layout; missing and whitespace-only identities
are excluded and reported.

UpstreamDrift's versioned contract returns estimates, uncertainty, warnings,
source lineage, backing-row references, and machine-readable unavailable
states for its registered generic analyses. Tools supplies PyQt and React/Vite
views with parity in variable choices, units, formulas, backing-data export,
persistence, and explanatory text. The specialized grouped estimators for
covariation and longitudinal synthesis are currently released within Tools,
with Python as the desktop authority and TypeScript as a tested browser twin;
they are not yet UpstreamDrift API operations. This bounded duplication is a
tracked architectural seam, not evidence that two independent implementations
are automatically equivalent.

Exploratory all-pairs scans report multiplicity warnings; a warning does not control a false-positive rate.
Within-player
analysis separates raw pooled association, player-mean-centred association,
between-player association, and per-player estimates; these quantities answer
different questions and must not be substituted for one another. Correlation
and principal-component loadings are descriptive, not causal. Predictive
work uses grouped splits whenever a trusted player or session identity exists
and otherwise states that leakage-resistant grouping is unavailable.
The split must match the intended generalization: holding out sessions from known players does not test prediction for previously unseen players or sources.

Strokes gained is similarly gated. A result labelled \emph{strokes gained}
requires a cited baseline table, a declared lie and distance state, and a
reproducible mapping from shot outcome to expected strokes. A distance-only
or target-error score without that backing is labelled a \emph{performance
proxy}; it must not borrow the strokes-gained name.

The canonical scoring contract requires exact lie, context, target, and distance
states before and after the shot. Missing states, incomplete retained rows, and
queries outside baseline support fail closed. No expected-strokes baseline is bundled
with the public application. A baseline's declared source URL, licence, version,
and digest preserve provenance; that declaration does not independently endorse
its methodology, legal suitability, or relevance to a particular player
population. The distance/target performance proxy remains a separate yard-based
dispersion measure whose machine-readable flag is
\path{is_strokes_gained=false}.

\section{Qualified Model-Comparison Results}

The Release A campaign evaluated seven open flight-model configurations on the
13,843-row strict cohort, producing 96,901 predictions with no execution
failures. Its primary estimands are source-stratified error, bias, association,
and residual relationships. Pooled metrics are retained only as descriptive
summaries because source composition, device, venue, ball, and acquisition
conditions are confounded.

For the Blackmore TrackMan-labelled carry cohort ($n=8{,}860$), the best tested
configuration was the Waterloo/Penner implementation: carry RMSE
$12.256$\,m, mean error $-4.093$\,m, and strong linear association with the
reported outcome. These are agreement statistics against the source's reported
carry, not an absolute-accuracy result and not evidence that the proprietary
TrackMan equations have been identified. For context, the best tested carry
configuration on one Garmin R10 source ($n=1{,}541$) had RMSE $7.150$\,m and
bias $-4.722$\,m, while one Garmin R50 source ($n=255$) had RMSE
$10.238$\,m and bias $+6.554$\,m. The sign change is one reason a pooled vendor
ranking would be scientifically unsafe.
Here ``best tested'' means the lowest carry RMSE among the seven configurations on that source's evaluation rows.
Selecting that minimum is not a new held-out evaluation, and comparisons between these unpaired sources cannot isolate a vendor effect.

\section{Release B: Paired-Device Scientific Validation}

Release B's scientific claims require a protocol fixed before confirmatory data collection, followed by execution and analysis under that protocol.
The pinned bundle is a \emph{structural template}, not a completed preregistration.
The ball specification, numeric speed bands, device/reference set, placement, and calibration procedures still require a documented freeze before the pilot.
Pilot-informed changes must be distinguished from confirmatory decisions, with the final analysis plan registered before inspecting confirmatory differences.
The software bundle and its synthetic fixture establish an executable workflow, not completion of those scientific decisions.

The broader intended program covers speed, loft, spin, launch direction, impact location, representative mishits, and indoor/outdoor conditions.
Record device model and firmware, ball and club, environmental state, alignment, player or robot identity, shot identity, and measured-versus-estimated status.
Firmware changes form new strata rather than being pooled invisibly.
The current template covers only three robot-center, outdoor club/speed cells: driver, 7-iron, and wedge.
Even a completed study in those cells would not establish indoor performance, mishit robustness, or generalization to human players.

\subsection{Pairing and Reference Uncertainty}

Each paired comparison must concern the same physical shot and the same \emph{measurand}, the quantity intended to be measured.
For example, horizontal carry to first ground contact in a declared frame differs from a model's carry normalized to standard weather, even if both outputs are labelled carry.
A traceable reference requires an uncertainty statement, not merely a passed-calibration flag; calibration and repeatability have specific metrological meanings~\cite{jcgmvim}.
Alignment, time registration, environmental corrections, and the distinction between measured and estimated outputs remain part of the comparison.
A common trigger timestamp associates records; it does not demonstrate zero acquisition lag or negligible synchronization uncertainty.

An additive model shows what pairing can and cannot remove.
For shot $s$, let $X_s$ be the shared physical quantity, $Y_{ds}$ the output of device $d$, and $R_s$ the reference output.
Within a declared condition, write constant biases as $b_d,b_R$ and zero-mean random errors as $e_{ds},e_{Rs}$:
\begin{align}
Y_{ds}&=X_s+b_d+e_{ds}, & R_s&=X_s+b_R+e_{Rs},\\
D_{ds}:=Y_{ds}-R_s&=b_d-b_R+e_{ds}-e_{Rs}.
\label{eq:paired-device-difference}
\end{align}
The common $X_s$ cancels, but reference bias and uncertainty do not.
Suppressing the shot index for the random variables across shots within this condition,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(D_d)=\operatorname{Var}(e_d)+\operatorname{Var}(e_R)
-2\operatorname{Cov}(e_d,e_R).
\label{eq:paired-error-variance}
\end{equation}
Shared calibration or environmental corrections can therefore change apparent agreement through error covariance.
As a constructed counterexample, two devices with the same \SI{5}{\meter} offset agree perfectly while both differ from the physical quantity by \SI{5}{\meter}.
This is a mathematical example, not a measured vendor error.
Across golf shots, dispersion of $Y_{ds}$ also includes variation in $X_s$ and any covariance between the physical outcome and measurement error.
It cannot automatically be attributed to golfer inconsistency, sensor noise, or a motor-control mechanism.

\subsection{Agreement, Repeatability, and Repeated Shots}

For independent paired differences from an approximately normal distribution with constant mean and variance, descriptive 95\% limits of agreement are
\begin{equation}
\overline D\ \pm\ 1.96s_D,
\label{eq:paired-agreement-limits}
\end{equation}
where $\overline D$ and $s_D$ are the sample mean and sample standard deviation of the differences.
These limits concern individual paired differences, not a confidence interval for mean bias; each estimated limit also has sampling uncertainty.
Repeated observations of changing quantities and replicates of a constant quantity require different variance decompositions~\cite{blandaltman2007}.
Condition-dependent bias or spread calls for a prespecified conditional model or justified transformation and examination of its residual assumptions.
Correlation alone cannot establish agreement, and a nonsignificant mean difference cannot establish acceptable error: practical bounds and precision requirements must be specified for the intended use.

Within-device repeatability concerns repeated measurements under specified conditions~\cite{jcgmvim}.
For two independent normal errors from one instrument with within-device standard deviation $\sigma_w$, measured at the same underlying quantity, the standard deviation of their difference is $\sqrt{2}\sigma_w$.
The corresponding 95\% absolute-difference threshold is $1.96\sqrt{2}\sigma_w$.
A device--reference difference standard deviation does not separately identify either instrument's $\sigma_w$.
Repeated robot shots can themselves have different physical outcomes, so their raw dispersion is not automatically instrument repeatability.

The pinned descriptive analyzer computes $1.96s_D$ under the field name \path{repeatability_coefficient}; here it is an \emph{agreement half-width}, not an identified within-device repeatability coefficient.
The analyzer's bootstrap resamples individual pairs to estimate uncertainty in mean bias; it does not implement the proposed hierarchical analysis or confidence intervals for both agreement limits.
Its pooled device summaries must not be mistaken for the completed condition-specific confirmatory analysis.

To illustrate the effect of clustering, suppose difference $j$ in block $b$ follows $D_{bj}=\mu+U_b+E_{bj}$.
Let $\mu$ be the population mean difference, independent zero-mean block effects $U_b$ have variance $\tau^2$, and mutually independent zero-mean residuals $E_{bj}$ have variance $\sigma^2$, also independent of all block effects.
For $B$ independent blocks of $m$ shots each,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(\overline D)=\frac{\tau^2}{B}+\frac{\sigma^2}{Bm}.
\label{eq:paired-block-mean}
\end{equation}
Treating all $Bm$ shots as independent would instead give $(\tau^2+\sigma^2)/(Bm)$, understating uncertainty when $m>1$ and $\tau^2>0$.
This illustrative model is not a fit to the campaign.
Unequal blocks, player effects, serial dependence, and comparisons sharing a reference require the model and resampling scheme appropriate to that design, preserving the shot pairing and relevant clusters.

\subsection{Sample Planning and the Completion Gate}

The template's planning arithmetic uses one two-sided paired-mean test with independent, identically distributed normal differences:
\begin{equation}
n\ \approx\ \left[\frac{(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})\sigma_D}{\delta}\right]^2.
\label{eq:paired-sample-planning}
\end{equation}
Here $z_p$ is the standard-normal $p$ quantile, $\alpha$ the test's type-I error probability, $1-\beta$ its power, $\sigma_D$ the assumed difference standard deviation, and $\delta$ the mean difference to detect.
With $\alpha=0.05$, power $0.80$, $\sigma_D=\SI{3}{\meter}$ and $\delta=\SI{1}{\meter}$, this approximation gives $70.6399$, rounded up to 71 complete independent pairs.
Allowing an expected 15\% unusable fraction gives $\lceil71/0.85\rceil=84$ \emph{attempts}; that expected-yield calculation does not guarantee a quota.
It neither establishes power for the protocol's multiplicity-adjusted family of endpoints and comparisons nor specifies precision for agreement limits.
Clustering and the intended interaction estimates also require separate planning.

The existing software gate nevertheless requires \textbf{84 accepted pairs per cell}, or 252 across the three cells; that operational threshold is retained.
The checked-in schedule has exactly 252 slots, so it supplies no attrition reserve for achieving 252 accepted pairs.
Before collection, specify and version an attempt budget, stopping rule, and replacement policy consistent with that gate.
Retain every failed attempt and its reason, rather than overwriting a failure or silently removing a device that struggles with difficult shots.
Report missingness by device and condition alongside agreement among retained pairs; complete-pair results alone need not represent performance over all attempted shots.

The software's ``pair'' identifier actually denotes one same-shot observation set containing at least two distinct commercial devices and exactly one reference.
Each device--reference contrast forms a statistical pair; contrasts within the same set share the reference, and do not create additional independent shots toward the quota.
The validator checks the recorded calibration status, unique device rows, hashed serial identifiers, and a common trigger timestamp; these schema checks do not establish physical independence or reference accuracy.
The versioned protocol template, capture-row JSON Schema, validator, deterministic schedule, collection ledger, command-line procedure, and synthetic fixture are present.
The generated status record reports \path{confirmatory_ready=false}, 252 \path{not_collected} exclusions, and zero analyzed pairs.
The schedule and ledger are bound by SHA-256:

No paired observations are represented as collected in this snapshot.
The remaining protocol decisions and physical acquisition are hold points; neither software readiness nor a synthetic fixture is device-validation evidence.

Neural-network vendor emulators remain internal research artifacts. Their
training manifests must pin the eligible sources, feature and target units,
splits, random seeds, software version, and evaluation cohort. They are
released only with held-out and out-of-distribution performance, uncertainty
or applicability bounds, and a model card that forbids device-emulation or
certification claims. Until the paired-device gate is satisfied, Release B
comparisons remain unavailable rather than being approximated from unrelated
shots.

\section{Reproducibility and Claim Discipline}

Release manifests bind software, interface, paper, and data revisions to their checksums.
Normalization and exclusions have their own lineage; exports retain the request and backing rows needed to reproduce a displayed calculation independently of interface state, subject to the source's access restrictions.

Unpaired public records support source-specific model comparisons.
Same-shot observations with characterized reference uncertainty support the proposed device comparisons.
Neither design alone identifies proprietary algorithms or uniquely separates sensor and aerodynamic-model errors: those inferences require additional assumptions and identifiable experiments.
Reproducible calculations make these limits inspectable; they do not remove them.
