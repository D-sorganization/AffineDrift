\subsection{The TrackMan Newsletter Archive}

Historical newsletters are indexed below through a third-party mirror.
Issues \#7--\#9 were retrieved for the present annotation review; this is
not a claim that the collection is complete or that official and archival
copies are unavailable. The mirror directory is
\url{https://www.gregsmithgolfcoach.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/}:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \texttt{newsletter1.pdf} (Nov 2007) through \texttt{newsletter9.pdf}
  (Jan 2013), sequentially numbered.
\item \texttt{TrackMan\_Newsletter\_2014.pdf} is issue \#10 (Jan 2014); the
  filename differs from the numbered examples. This does not establish an
  end date for the series.
\item \textbf{\#7 (Oct 2010), ``Ten Fundamentals''} presents historical
  vendor rules of thumb: driver launch weights of 85\% toward the face in
  both planes, 75\% for irons, and nominal spin decay of 4\% per second.
  These are not universal material or aerodynamic constants. Its scalar
  loft-minus-attack definition requires a common-plane interpretation;
  spatial spin loft is the angle between the face normal and velocity.
\item \textbf{\#9 (Jan 2013), pp.\ 3 and 5} specifies a maximum-compression
  reporting reference with averaging qualifications. It lists TrackMan
  III/IIIe \emph{absolute} specifications, described by the manufacturer as
  95\% confidence: speed $\pm1.5$~mph, attack $\pm1.0\degs$, path
  $\pm1.0\degs$, loft $\pm0.8\degs$, face $\pm0.6\degs$. Its separate
  repeatability column is not interchangeable with absolute error.
  Conditions include a centre of gravity within 10\,mm of the geometric
  centre for attack/path, impact entirely on the face for loft/face, and
  successful measurement of the required club, ball-direction and spin data.
  A reporting reference does not mean every output is an instantaneous
  direct measurement; see also the dated club-data definitions above.
\item \textbf{\#8 (Jun 2011), p.\ 4} gives a 12.7\,mm heel offset and a
  $2\degs$ face-angle change toward closed. Its universal-driver wording
  should not be adopted as a geometric law: the change depends on local face curvature.
  For an ideal circular horizontal section, $\Delta\theta=\arcsin(x/R)$
  for lateral offset $x$ and radius $R$, relative to the central normal.
  At $x=12.7$\,mm, radii of 250 and 500\,mm give about $2.91\degs$ and
  $1.46\degs$, respectively. These are geometric examples, not measured
  driver specifications; the sign depends on the heel/toe convention.
\end{itemize}

\begin{warning}
Those accuracy figures are manufacturer specifications. Leach et al.\ tested
TrackMan Pro IIIe and GC2+HMT against an optical reference in an indoor,
eight-golfer, 240-shot study
(Leach et al.\ 2017,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2017.08.009}). In Table~6,
45\% of TrackMan path differences and 66\% of face-angle differences fall
within $\pm1\degs$ of the \emph{median difference}, not of zero error.
Such a band describes dispersion after centering; a systematic bias can
remain. It is not a direct pass/fail test of the vendor's absolute limits.

Data availability is another quantity: TrackMan returned club velocity on
98\% of shots, but the other four club parameters on 62\% overall and
19\% for the utility wedge. The authors also describe installation
compromises, possible radar interference, markers, and differences in
reference point or impact timing. Reference calibration alone does not
make all compared outputs the same measurand. Report bias, dispersion,
uncertainty and return rate separately, and retain these device/setup limits
when applying the result to a golfer's swing or to a newer monitor.
\end{warning}
