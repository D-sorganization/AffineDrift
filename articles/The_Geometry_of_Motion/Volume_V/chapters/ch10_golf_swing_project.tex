\chapter{Capstone: Golf Swing Analysis}
\label{ch:golf-capstone}

\begin{gomdraftnotice}
This chapter provides a runnable, tested three-link example and a roadmap for
larger investigations. Its synthetic motion is prescribed, not measured or
optimized. The extensions on muscles, feedback, uncertainty and impact require
additional implementation and evidence.
\end{gomdraftnotice}

The scientific question is how geometry, inertia and actuation together produce
an endpoint motion. A rapidly moving clubhead does not by itself reveal which
joint supplied energy, which muscle was active, or what would happen after
removing an input. Those questions require different calculations. This capstone
connects them through one explicit mechanical model, then identifies the extra
information each extension needs.

\section{Model and Question}

The implemented system has three rigid links and three revolute coordinates.
The base is fixed. The first angle is measured counterclockwise from the
horizontal; the other two are relative joint angles. World height increases
upward and gravity acts downward. Angles, rates and accelerations use radians,
rad/s and rad/s$^2$; lengths, masses and generalized torques use metres,
kilograms and N\,m. The chain can illustrate a shoulder--elbow--wrist linkage,
but it has no moving torso, two-arm closed loop, muscles, shaft flexibility,
ground contact or ball collision.

Each link is a uniform rod with centre of mass at half its length and central
inertia $I_i=m_i\ell_i^2/12$. The parallel-axis contributions are included when
the model assembles its mass matrix. The default values are illustrative
parameters, not subject-specific measurements. A head-heavy club requires
measured or justified centre-of-mass and inertia parameters; changing its mass
alone does not implement that distribution. The model therefore supports
conditional mechanical deductions, not a prescription for human technique.

\section{Geometry Before Attribution}

Let $\theta_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}q_j$ and
$\omega_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\dot q_j$. The tip position and velocity are
\begin{align}
 \bm p(q)&=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i
   \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_i\\\sin\theta_i\end{bmatrix},\\
 \bm v(q,\dot q)&=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\omega_i
   \begin{bmatrix}-\sin\theta_i\\\cos\theta_i\end{bmatrix}
   =J_p(q)\dot q.
\end{align}
Here $J_p$ is the $2\times3$ Jacobian of the tip position. It is not the
six-component spatial-twist Jacobian; the distinction matters when comparing
robotics conventions \cite{LynchParkJacobianReview}.
Speed is $s_v=\norm{\bm v}$, taken \emph{after} adding the velocity vectors.
Their directions depend on configuration. The scalar sum $\sum_i\ell_i|\omega_i|$ discards directional cancellation.

For the default lengths $(0.28,0.46,1.10)$\,m and relative rates
$(5,5,5)$\,rad/s, the straight configuration $q=(0,0,0)$ gives
$22.5$\,m/s. At $q=(0,\pi,\pi)$ the signed transverse contributions are
$1.4-4.6+16.5=13.3$\,m/s. The configuration-blind value would overestimate
this particular folded example by about $69\%$. This is a reproducible
geometric counterexample, not a population estimate.

\section{What Adds at the Same State}

For this unconstrained rigid model, write
\begin{equation}
 M(q)\ddot q+b(q,\dot q)=\tau,\qquad
 b=C(q,\dot q)\dot q+g(q),\qquad M(q)\succ0.
\end{equation}
Forward dynamics evaluates acceleration from the state and input; inverse
dynamics evaluates the input required by a specified acceleration
\cite{LynchParkDynamicsReview}. At a \emph{common} state define
\begin{equation}
 a_d=-M^{-1}b,\qquad a_u=M^{-1}\tau,\qquad \ddot q=a_d+a_u.
 \label{eq:capstone-joint-split}
\end{equation}
These are signed joint-acceleration vectors. The inverse is notation: the code
solves linear systems. The equality is exact in the mathematical model, subject
to numerical error in its implementation. It does not establish that arbitrary
supplied position, velocity and torque arrays form a dynamically consistent
trajectory.

\begin{warningbox}
The magnitudes $\norm{a_d}$ and $\norm{a_u}$ generally do not add to
$\norm{\ddot q}$. If $\tau=b\ne0$, then $a_u=-a_d$ and the net joint
acceleration is zero, even though both component norms are positive. Reporting
those norms as complementary percentages loses the cancellation. The norms also
depend on the chosen joint coordinates and weighting. They are neither a speed
partition nor a measure of muscular effort.
\end{warningbox}

The tip acceleration contains an additional geometric term:
\begin{align}
 \bm a_p&=J_p\ddot q+\dot J_p\dot q=\bm a_{p,d}+\bm a_{p,u},\\
 \bm a_{p,d}&=J_pa_d+\dot J_p\dot q,\qquad
 \bm a_{p,u}=J_pa_u,\\
 \dot J_p\dot q&=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ell_i\omega_i^2
   \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_i\\\sin\theta_i\end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}
These vectors have units m/s$^2$. When $s_v>0$, its instantaneous rate is
\begin{equation}
 \dot s_v=\frac{\bm v^{\mathsf T}\bm a_{p,d}}{s_v}
          +\frac{\bm v^{\mathsf T}\bm a_{p,u}}{s_v}.
\end{equation}
The two signed tangential terms can oppose one another. This formula is
undefined at zero speed; acceleration vectors remain meaningful there.
The current listing reports joint components, not these Cartesian projections.

At a straight vertical configuration, the ideal model has $g=0$ and
$C\dot q=0$: derivatives of its relative-angle cosine terms vanish.
Nevertheless, $\dot J_p\dot q$ can be nonzero. A rotating straight chain can
have zero joint drift acceleration at that instant and a substantial centripetal
tip acceleration. Straightness alone is insufficient even for zero joint drift:
a horizontal chain generally has nonzero gravity torque.

\section{A Counterfactual Is a New Trajectory}

With $x=(q,\dot q)$, write $\dot x=f(x)+G(x)\tau$. A zero-torque trajectory
released at $t_r$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 \dot x_0=f(x_0),\qquad x_0(t_r)=x_u(t_r).
\end{equation}
For a driven trajectory $x_u$, their difference satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}(x_u-x_0)
 =f(x_u)-f(x_0)+G(x_u)\tau.
\end{equation}
The drift difference vanishes at release but generally not afterward. Thus
integrating $M^{-1}\tau$ along the driven path does not by itself recover the
difference in subsequent joint velocities. Different configurations also change
the tip Jacobian. Control-affine input dependence does not make entire
trajectories, or their speed magnitudes, superposable.

A useful comparison declares the release state, retained passive forces,
intervention and endpoint: a common elapsed time and each trajectory's contact
event are different experiments. Turning off excitation in a muscle model is
also different from setting generalized torque to zero; activation and tendon
states can retain history. Here the unforced system retains gravity and inertia
and contains no friction or elastic elements. Energy conservation and timestep
refinement provide checks on its numerical integration
\cite{LynchParkForwardReview}. They do not validate its human anatomy.
The separate \texttt{GolfModel.ztcf\_trajectory} routine can support an unforced
extension; \texttt{SwingAnalysis} does not call it.

\section{A Reproducible Synthetic Example}

The listing prescribes a smooth path, evaluates its analytic first and second
derivatives, and calculates $\tau=M\ddot q+C\dot q+g$. These are required
model torques for that path, with no imposed actuator limits. They replace an
unrelated illustrative torque profile. Inverse-dynamic consistency tests an
algebraic relationship; a separate forward integration and step-refinement
comparison would test trajectory reproduction.

For duration $T>0$ and maximum sample spacing $h>0$, the grid contains
$\lceil T/h\rceil+1$ samples including both endpoints. Its actual spacing is
$T/\lceil T/h\rceil$, which need not equal $h$. Analytic rates avoid confusing
those two intervals. With $\sigma=t/T$, the prescribed path is
\begin{equation}
 q(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}
 -\pi/2+\pi\sigma^2\\
 (\pi/3)\sin(\pi\sigma)\\
 -(\pi/2)\cos(\pi\sigma/2)
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its nonzero endpoint rates are allowed; this example is not a rest-to-rest
trajectory. Doubling $T$ at fixed path halves rates and quarters accelerations;
gravity torque does not scale that way. This distinguishes changing the path,
changing its timing and changing the mechanical plant.

\section{Connecting Mechanics, Control and Measurement}

This example implements model construction, prescribed-motion inverse dynamics,
tip kinematics and same-state joint decomposition. Optimization, feedback,
robustness certificates, muscle estimation and variability analysis are
\emph{extensions}, each with a distinct evidence requirement.

For experimental inverse dynamics, start with synchronized measurements and a
model that specifies coordinates, inertial parameters and external wrenches.
Choose sampling and filtering from the signal bandwidth, noise and derivative
accuracy needed; there is no universal adequate camera rate. In a whole-body
model, unmeasured contact forces cannot simply be omitted without changing the
inference. Report assumed forces, constraints and residuals, especially in
unactuated base coordinates. Motion alone does not generally identify all
external forces and internal torques simultaneously.

Net joint torque also does not uniquely specify muscle force. Moment arms,
activation and contraction dynamics, passive tissues, force bounds and the
chosen recruitment criterion affect that estimate. EMG can constrain an
activation model; it is not a direct force or torque measurement. Refer to the
biological actuation and inverse-dynamics treatments in Volume~III before
interpreting recruitment. A later torque response can also depend on earlier
control through the current velocity, geometry and stored elastic energy.

For the ideal conservative rigid model, total mechanical energy satisfies
$\dot E=\dot q^{\mathsf T}\tau$. Internal inertial coupling redistributes
motion and energy; it is not an additional energy supply. A joint can have a
large acceleration contribution while doing little instantaneous work if its
rate is small. Peak ordering, acceleration attribution and joint power therefore
answer different questions. Specify the system boundary before interpreting
energy transferred across it.

A feedback study adds a declared policy, disturbances, sensor information and
actuator constraints. A finite-time funnel must bound a set of initial states
through an interval and into a defined terminal set; a calculation only at the
nominal impact instant is insufficient. If contact is included, the guard and
reset map must be part of the argument. A contraction condition or successful
simulation should be reported only over the domain where its hypotheses or
numerical checks hold.

For variability, an ensemble and a task map are essential. At a reference state,
$D h$ separates first-order task-changing directions from its null space.
Variance depends on alignment, coordinates, metric, rank and normalization by
subspace dimension. Artificial torque perturbations describe the simulated
ensemble; they do not establish a human neural synergy. If the task is a peak
over time, its differentiability and event alignment require care. A fixed-time
endpoint map is a simpler starting point.

\section*{Final Exercises}

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Kinematic and Dynamic Consistency.} Run the example and compare
 the two configurations above. Verify analytic endpoint rates and the residual
 $M\ddot q+C\dot q+g-\tau$. Explain why the latter does not independently
 validate the model or its forward integrator.
 \item \textbf{Two Parameter Studies.} First hold the prescribed joint motion
 fixed and vary club length and mass separately. Mass changes required torque
 but not tip kinematics; length changes both. Then construct a forward study
 with the same initial state and declared input policy. Compare outcomes and
 explain why these are different experiments.
 \item \textbf{Signed Contributions.} Set $\tau=b$ at a chosen state. Check
 cancellation in joint space and compute the remaining $\dot J_p\dot q$ tip
 acceleration. Contrast vector, norm, tangential-acceleration and power measures.
 \item \textbf{Release Experiment.} Integrate driven and zero-torque systems
 from a shared state over a declared interval. Refine the timestep, monitor
 energy where appropriate, and compare endpoint velocity vectors as well as
 speeds. Repeat for a different release time without treating either result as
 a universal contribution percentage.
 \item \textbf{Muscle and Robustness Extensions.} Specify a muscle model,
 recruitment objective and feasibility checks, or a feedback policy and terminal
 set. Implement one bounded extension and report which conclusions depend on
 its assumptions. An impact study must define contact and reset behavior.
 \item \textbf{Variability Extension.} Generate an ensemble under an explicit
 noise model, align samples and analyze a fixed-time task Jacobian. Report
 sample-size sensitivity and dimension-normalized variances. Explain what
 additional data would be needed to make a claim about human coordination.
\end{enumerate}
