\chapter{Building a Multibody Model}
\label{ch:building-models}

A model file encodes a hypothesis about geometry, mass and permitted motion.
An attractive animation does not establish that these quantities are correct.
Before building a whole golfer, construct a body whose kinematics and dynamics
can be checked analytically. Then add anatomy and contacts while preserving
the frames, parameters and question the model is meant to answer.

\section{Formats and Their Boundaries}

URDF means \emph{Unified Robot Description Format}. It describes links and
joints, including explicit frame transforms, inertia, and visual and collision
geometry. Joint origins are not Denavit--Hartenberg parameters; convert and
verify transforms when starting from a DH model. The standard urdfdom joint
parser recognizes revolute, continuous, prismatic, fixed, floating and planar
joints, not a type named \texttt{ball}. Individual importers may support only
a subset \cite{URDFPhysicalReview,URDFJointParserReview}.

MJCF is MuJoCo's distinct native XML format, not an extension of URDF syntax.
It includes MuJoCo-specific actuator, tendon and contact descriptions. MuJoCo
also imports URDF with documented conversion behavior and optional extensions;
importing a rigid-body tree does not automatically transfer a complete muscle
or controller model \cite{MuJoCoModelingReview}.

\section{Frames, Inertia and Physical Consistency}

Each joint needs a parent and child, its transform at zero configuration, and
allowed motion. Every non-root link in a tree has one parent joint, and all
links connect to a declared root. The word \texttt{world} is not an automatically
declared link. Specify whether the importer fixes the root or adds a free base.
Closed loops require additional constraints beyond a simple tree.

Mass, COM position and inertia must refer to compatible frames. If $I_C$ is
inertia about the COM in its principal frame, $R$ rotates that frame into the
reporting frame, and $r$ runs from reporting point $O$ to the COM, then
\begin{equation}
 I_O=R I_C R^T+m\big[(r^Tr)I_3-rr^T\big].
\end{equation}
For nondegenerate three-dimensional bodies, COM inertia is symmetric positive
definite. Principal moments also satisfy triangle inequalities, such as
$I_1+I_2\geq I_3$; positive definiteness alone does not describe a physically
possible mass distribution. Units are kg\,m$^2$, not kg or m$^2$. The numerical
identity matrix is not a neutral inertia default.

URDF's inertial origin locates the COM and its inertia frame; its six tensor
entries are about that origin. Visual and collision origins are independent.
In the parser, omitting an origin defaults to identity and omitting a joint
axis defaults to $+x$. These defaults may parse while describing the wrong
mechanism. Revolute joints require limits; an inertial element needs both mass
and inertia data \cite{URDFPhysicalReview,URDFJointParserReview,URDFLinkParserReview}.

\section{A Complete Cylinder Pendulum}

Use Chapter~\ref{ch:platform}'s parameters: $m=L=1$ in SI units, $r=0.02$ m
and $b=0.05$ N\,m\,s/rad. At zero angle, the cylinder extends from the hinge
down world $-z$. Geometry and COM are centered at $z=-0.5$ m, and the hinge axis
is $+y$. The transverse COM moments are $0.0834333333$ kg\,m$^2$; the axial
moment is $0.0002$ kg\,m$^2$. The fixed root below is a reference frame, not an
extra moving mass.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=XML, caption={Complete URDF for the Analytic Cylinder Pendulum}]
<?xml version="1.0"?>
<robot name="cylinder_pendulum">
  <link name="base"/>
  <link name="rod">
    <inertial>
      <origin xyz="0 0 -0.5" rpy="0 0 0"/>
      <mass value="1.0"/>
      <inertia ixx="0.0834333333333333"
               iyy="0.0834333333333333" izz="0.0002"
               ixy="0" ixz="0" iyz="0"/>
    </inertial>
    <visual>
      <origin xyz="0 0 -0.5" rpy="0 0 0"/>
      <geometry><cylinder length="1.0" radius="0.02"/></geometry>
    </visual>
    <collision>
      <origin xyz="0 0 -0.5" rpy="0 0 0"/>
      <geometry><cylinder length="1.0" radius="0.02"/></geometry>
    </collision>
  </link>
  <joint name="pivot" type="revolute">
    <parent link="base"/>
    <child link="rod"/>
    <origin xyz="0 0 0" rpy="0 0 0"/>
    <axis xyz="0 1 0"/>
    <limit lower="-3.14" upper="3.14" effort="20" velocity="30"/>
    <dynamics damping="0.05"/>
  </joint>
</robot>
\end{lstlisting}

Fix the base and set gravity to $(0,0,-9.81)$ in the target engine. The analytic
comparison assumes no contact or limit engagement. Limit and effort metadata
do not prove every importer enforces those bounds identically. The file adds
no actuator; the passive example needs none.

At angle $q$ and rate $v$, the expected COM position and scalar dynamic terms are
\begin{align}
 p_C&=(-\tfrac12\sin q,\ 0,\ -\tfrac12\cos q),\\
 M&=\tfrac13+\tfrac{0.02^2}{4},\qquad
 g_q=4.905\sin q,\qquad \tau_{\rm pass}=-0.05v.
\end{align}
Here $g_q$ is on the left in $M\dot v+g_q=\tau_{\rm pass}$. These checks
distinguish a correct XML tree from correct interpreted physics. Repository
tests parse the actual listing and, when MuJoCo is installed, load it to check
mass, coordinates, COM, inertia, gravity and damping. That single-engine test
does not certify import by Drake or Pinocchio, or a time-integrated comparison.

\section{Extending to a Seven-Segment Golf Model}

A possible reduced tree is a declared world reference, then pelvis, trunk,
upper arm, forearm, hand, club shaft and club head. Connect the head to the
shaft with a fixed joint; an orphan head loses its inertial contribution or
causes import failure. For pelvis and trunk hinges, a three-DOF shoulder, elbow
and wrist hinges, and fixed grip and head joints, the unconstrained mechanism
has $1+1+3+1+1=7$ velocity degrees of freedom. A native quaternion shoulder
gives eight stored configuration components with a unit-norm constraint; it
does not create an eighth physical degree of freedom.

This describes one arm with a prescribed base support model. A second hand on
the club generally adds a closed-loop constraint; legs and foot-ground
interaction change the mechanism and external-force model. The seven-body
sketch is not a whole-body golfer. Native spherical joints or a validated
three-hinge decomposition are possible implementation choices. A decomposition
has different coordinate singularities and intermediate-link requirements;
zero-mass dummy links are not universally valid across engines.

For each segment, record parameter source, population or subject, anatomical
landmarks, units, scaling rule, COM frame and inertia convention. Club properties
need their own measurements or assumptions. Generic numbers labeled ``an 80 kg
golfer'' are not an anthropometric calibration, and a partial upper-body chain
need not sum to total body mass. Preserve parameter uncertainty and test its
effect on the observable.

Muscle attachment points alone do not supply muscle mechanics. An extension
needs paths and moment arms, activation and contraction states,
force-length/velocity assumptions and parameter evidence. Frame errors affect
Jacobians and moment arms; inertial errors affect required torques; unmodeled
contacts affect inverse-dynamics residuals. All can change an apparent control
strategy. Successful animation, exact inverse dynamics and an anatomically
plausible explanation therefore represent different levels of evidence.

\section*{Exercises}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Frame Audit.} Perturb a joint origin, then an inertial origin.
 Predict which kinematic and dynamic checks change before running the model.
 \item \textbf{Mass Distribution.} Keep mass and length fixed while changing
 cylinder radius. Derive changes in hinge inertia and small-angle frequency.
 \item \textbf{Three-Link Arm.} Declare each frame, COM and tensor. Check tree
 connectivity, mass, positive kinetic energy and gravity before control.
 \item \textbf{Golf Extension.} Specify constraints and external measurements
 needed for a second hand and ground contact. Distinguish measurements,
 illustrative assumptions and fitted quantities.
\end{enumerate}
