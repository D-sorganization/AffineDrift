\chapter{Recursive Algorithms}
\label{ch:recursive_algorithms}

\epigraph{%
  You do not solve the equations of motion for a 15-DOF robot in a single monolithic formula. You walk from the base to the tip, and then from the tip back to the base.
}{}

\begin{laymansbox}{Context \& Use Case: Beating the Math Explosion}{context_box}
\textbf{The Math You Will See:} We will introduce the \textbf{Adjoint Matrix} ($[\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}}]$), the \textbf{Spatial Inertia Matrix} ($\mathcal{I}$), and the two-pass \textbf{Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA)}.
\textbf{Why We Need It:} If you try to write down the exact algebra connecting the shoulder motor of a robot to the tip of its finger, the equation takes up a whole page. If you try to write the equation for a 15-joint humanoid robot, the trigonometric math expands to fill \emph{tens of thousands} of pages of dense algebra. No computer can solve that in real-time. Instead, we use "Recursive Algorithms" that only ever look at two bones at a time. By chaining these tiny, fast calculations together sequentially, we can simulate complex physics at 1,000 frames per second.
\textbf{All Variables Defined:} $\mathcal{V}_i$ is the velocity of link $i$, $\mathcal{F}_i$ is the force on link $i$, and $\mathbf{T}_{i, i-1}$ is the relative pose between them.
\end{laymansbox}

\section{The Curse of Dimensionality in Dynamics}

As systems scale in complexity—from a simple pendulum (1 DOF) to a fully modeled anatomical golf swing (15+ DOF) or a humanoid Atlas robot (30+ DOF)—the monolithic, closed-form equations of physics become mathematically intractable.

In standard Lagrangian mechanics (which we review in a later chapter), the equations of motion are written as a massive matrix equation:
\begin{equation}
   \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf{M}$ is the mass matrix, $\mathbf{C}$ contains Coriolis and centrifugal forces, $\mathbf{G}$ is gravity, and $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ are the motor torques. 

If you attempt to write the analytical Coriolis matrix explicitly for a 15-DOF human model on a piece of paper, the resulting trigonometric expansion ($sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)cos(\theta_3)\dots$) will literally fill thousands of pages.

To control complex mechanisms in the real world at thousands of Hertz, modern engines (such as MuJoCo, Drake, Pinocchio, and UpstreamDrift) completely abandon building these massive equations explicitly. Instead, they rely entirely on \textbf{Recursive Algorithms} executing purely numerical matrix multiplications in $O(N)$ time.

\section{The Adjoint Map ($[\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}}]$)}

Before we can compute recursive mechanics, we need to know how to shift a 6D spatial Twist ($\mathcal{V}$) or 6D Wrench ($\mathcal{F}$) from one reference frame to another. This is done using the \textbf{Adjoint Representation} of $SE(3)$.

If you know the homogeneous transformation $\mathbf{T}$ between Frame $A$ and Frame $B$, the $6 \times 6$ Adjoint Matrix $[\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}}]$ translates velocities seamlessly between them:
\begin{equation}
   [\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}}] = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R} & \mathbf{0} \\ [\mathbf{p}]\mathbf{R} & \mathbf{R} \end{bmatrix} \in \Reals^{6 \times 6}
\end{equation}
Where $\mathbf{R}$ is the $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix, $\mathbf{p}$ is the translation vector, and $[\mathbf{p}]$ is its skew-symmetric matrix.

If Frame $A$ has a spatial velocity (Twist) of $\mathcal{V}_A$, the exact exact same physical velocity, as measured by Frame $B$, is simply:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{V}_B = [\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}_{BA}}] \mathcal{V}_A
\end{equation}
(Note: Wrenches $\mathcal{F}$ transform using the transpose of the Adjoint inverse). This $6 \times 6$ matrix multiplication is the absolute workhorse of all robotics frameworks.

\section{Recursive Forward Kinematics}

Suppose we want to find the exact spatial position and velocity of the golf clubhead at the very end of a 15-link robotic golfer. 

Instead of deriving a massive equation relating the base of the feet directly to the clubhead, we use our $4 \times 4$ Transforms and $6 \times 6$ Adjoints, and we propagate them sequentially outwards.

\begin{laymansbox}{The Breadcrumb Strategy}{recursive_intuition}
  To find the absolute speed of the clubhead relative to the ground, we don't jump straight from the ground to the club. 
  
  We ask: How fast is the pelvis moving relative to the feet? \dots Then, we add the speed of the torso relative to the pelvis. \dots Then we add the speed of the shoulder relative to the torso \dots chain by chain, link by link.
\end{laymansbox}

Mathematically, if $i$ is the current link, and $i-1$ is its parent bone:
\begin{equation}
   \mathcal{V}_i = [\text{Ad}_{\mathbf{T}_{i, i-1}}] \mathcal{V}_{i-1} + \mathcal{S}_i \dot{q}_i
\end{equation}

This equation takes the absolute velocity of the parent link ($\mathcal{V}_{i-1}$), shifts it physically down the bone to the child link using the Adjoint Matrix ($[\text{Ad}]$), and then simply adds the new velocity generated by the child's own motor spinning ($\mathcal{S}_i \dot{q}_i$).

By running a simple loop \texttt{for i=1 to 15}, a computer solves the exact kinematics of the entire chain blazingly fast, with zero trigonometric explosions.

\section{The Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA)}

While Forward Kinematics gives us position and velocity, controlling physical motion requires solving for forces and torques. \emph{Inverse Dynamics} asks: If we know the trajectory the robot is currently taking ($\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}}, \ddot{\mathbf{q}}$), how much torque $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ must the motors output at this exact millisecond to execute it?

This is solved by the crowning jewel of computational mechanics: the \textbf{Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA)}. It executes in exactly two sweeps.

\subsection{The Outward Pass: Velocity and Acceleration}

Just like Forward Kinematics, we cast a sweeping algorithm from the heavy base (the ground) outwards to the lightest tip (the end-effector).
For every link $i$, we compute its absolute velocity $\mathcal{V}_i$ and its absolute spatial acceleration $\dot{\mathcal{V}}_i$.

This outward pass perfectly calculates the exact inertial, Coriolis, and centrifugal accelerations dragging on every single link of the mechanism as it flails through space.

\subsection{Spatial Inertia ($\mathcal{G}_i$)}
Before we compute force, we must define mass. In 3D space, an object doesn't just have a scalar mass $m$; it has a complete $3 \times 3$ rotational inertia tensor $\mathbf{I}_c$. We combine these into a unified $6 \times 6$ \textbf{Spatial Inertia Matrix} $\mathcal{G}_i$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_c & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & m\mathbf{I}_{3 \times 3} \end{bmatrix} \in \Reals^{6 \times 6}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Inward Pass: Forces and Torques}

Once the outward pass finishes at the tip, we reverse direction. We perform a second loop starting from the tip of the robot back down to the ground.

At the very tip, we compute the inertial force $F = ma$ created by the spatial acceleration we just calculated in the first pass:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_i = \mathcal{G}_i \dot{\mathcal{V}}_i - [\text{ad}_{\mathcal{V}_i}]^T (\mathcal{G}_i \mathcal{V}_i)
\end{equation}

Where $\mathcal{F}_i$ is the Spatial Wrench (the combined 6D Force/Torque vector required to make link $i$ move). The second term handles all the complex gyroscopic and Coriolis forces automatically!

Then, we recursively drag that Wrench backwards down the arm. The shoulder joint has to support the force of the arm, plus the new forces generated by the shoulder itself. The torso has to support the forces from the shoulder. 
The torque ($\tau_i$) physically required at any specific motor $i$ is extracted by projecting the total required Wrench back onto the single designated scalar axis of its mechanical joint:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_i = \mathcal{F}_i^T \mathcal{S}_i
\end{equation}

\begin{principle}{Algorithmic Efficiency}{algo_eff}
  The Recursive Newton-Euler algorithm allows the inverse dynamics for a system with $N$ joints to be solved in absolutely perfect $O(N)$ operations. The time to compute scales linearly, regardless of complexity. This algorithmic breakthrough is the fundamental mathematical reason robotics and physics engines work in the real world.
\end{principle}

\section{Summary Structure}

Throughout the subsequent volumes of \textit{The Geometry of Motion}, physical phenomena like Contraction Metrics, Sums-of-Squares Programming, and LQR-Trees are discussed dynamically.
