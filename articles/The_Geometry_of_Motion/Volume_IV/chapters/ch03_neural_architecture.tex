\section{Anatomy Constrains Models Without Identifying an Algorithm}

A motor command emerges from interacting circuits and acts through a mechanical
body. A useful architecture must explain both the observed motion and how
sensory evidence changes subsequent commands. An anatomical connection supports
a possible information pathway; it does not by itself specify a cost function,
state estimator, learning rule, or controller frequency. The same movement can
be compatible with several computational accounts.

\subsection{Cortical Motor Areas}

M1 is a major source of descending commands, rather than an obligatory final
cortical stage through which all voluntary commands must pass. Tracing studies
identified corticospinal projections from several premotor areas as well as
M1~\cite{DumStrick1991Review}. A projection to spinal cord is not necessarily a
monosynaptic connection to a motoneuron. In rhesus-monkey forelimb experiments,
Rathelot and Strick distinguished direct cortico-motoneuronal cells concentrated
in caudal M1 from pathways involving spinal interneurons~\cite{RathelotStrick2009Review}.
Thus a drawing containing only cortex followed by motoneurons omits consequential
processing. Neither the corticospinal population nor its direct motoneuronal
subset should be equated with the giant Betz-cell class.

Somatotopic maps are useful, but the forelimb populations in that tracing study
also overlapped. This organization is compatible with coordinated multijoint
output; it does not prove a fixed, universal synergy matrix. Similarly, labels
such as premotor cortex and supplementary motor area should not be read as a
hard separation of external-cue and internal-cue computations. Testing such a
functional distinction requires a task, recording or intervention, and an
operational definition of the proposed computation. A fixed preparatory interval
of 100--200 ms cannot be assigned to all M1 activity without those conditions.

\subsection{Population Coding and Its Interpretation}

Georgopoulos and colleagues studied directional tuning and population decoding
in monkey reaching~\cite{Georgopoulos1982,Georgopoulos1986}. A weighted combination
of broadly tuned neurons can predict direction much better than a single cell.
This is evidence about a relationship between activity and movement in the
recorded task. Successful decoding does not establish that downstream circuits
perform that exact vector calculation, that every M1 neuron has cosine tuning,
or that the decoded vector is a joint torque or muscle-force command.

To make the mathematics explicit, consider a two-dimensional manufactured
population with preferred directions
$d_i=(\cos\phi_i,\sin\phi_i)^T$ and rates
\begin{equation}
 r_i=b+g\cos(\theta-\phi_i),\qquad b\ge g\ge0.
\end{equation}
For eight uniformly spaced $\phi_i=2\pi i/8$, define the baseline-corrected
population vector
\begin{equation}
 P=\sum_{i=0}^7(r_i-b)d_i
   =g\left(\sum_{i=0}^7 d_id_i^T\right)
        \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta\\\sin\theta\end{bmatrix}
   =4g\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta\\\sin\theta\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:ch3:population-vector}
\end{equation}
The last equality follows from equal angular sampling: the sum of outer
products is $4I_2$. For $b=5$, $g=2$, and $\theta=30^\circ$, the result is
$P=(4\sqrt3,4)^T$ in the rate units used for $r_i$. Its direction is
$30^\circ$; its magnitude is eight, not eight metres per second. Calibration
would be needed to infer a physical velocity. If $g=0$, $P=0$ and no direction
can be recovered; reporting an angle for this zero vector would be misleading.

Uneven sampling changes the decoder. With preferred directions
$(0,0,\pi/2)$, the outer-product sum is $\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$.
For the same cosine-tuned construction at $30^\circ$, the decoded angle is
$\arctan(\tan30^\circ/2)\approx16.1^\circ$, even after correct baseline
subtraction. Noise, baseline estimation, gain heterogeneity, and correlated
activity add further estimation problems. This example explains why a decoder
must be evaluated on held-out observations and why its sampling assumptions
matter; it is not a reanalysis of the original recordings.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={A Baseline-Corrected Planar Decoder}]
import numpy as np


def population_vector(preferred_angles, rates, baseline):
    """Decode finite, nonnegative rates; zero is legitimate."""
    angles = np.asarray(preferred_angles, dtype=float)
    rates = np.asarray(rates, dtype=float)
    if angles.ndim != 1 or angles.size == 0:
        raise ValueError("Angles must be a nonempty vector")
    if rates.shape != angles.shape:
        raise ValueError("One rate per preferred angle")
    if (not np.all(np.isfinite(angles))
            or not np.all(np.isfinite(rates))
            or np.any(rates < 0)):
        raise ValueError("Finite angles, nonnegative rates")
    if (np.ndim(baseline) != 0
            or not np.isfinite(baseline) or baseline < 0):
        raise ValueError("Finite nonnegative scalar baseline")
    directions = np.column_stack((np.cos(angles),
                                  np.sin(angles)))
    return (rates - baseline) @ directions
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Cerebellar Learning: Evidence and Hypotheses}

Classical cerebellar theories motivate adaptive combinations of basis
functions~\cite{Marr1969,Albus1971,Kawato1987}. These are computational proposals,
not an identification of the cerebellum with a single forward model. Forward
prediction, inverse compensation, state estimation, timing, and learning are
distinct functions that must be distinguished by their inputs, outputs, and
experimental consequences. Describing the granule-cell layer as an expansion
in a basis-function model is also different from claiming that anatomy fixes
a particular radial-basis network or a compressed state dimension.

In Smith and Shadmehr's force-field reaching study, trial-to-trial adjustment
was impaired in the cerebellar group but preserved in the Huntington's-disease
groups. Their interpretation combines this result with earlier evidence about
within-movement correction to argue for a dissociation between two uses of
error~\cite{SmithShadmehr2005}. This supports a contribution of cerebellar
integrity to the studied adaptation. It does not locate every learning
operation in one synapse or establish that all online corrections are intact
in every cerebellar disorder. Nor does it identify a unique forward versus
inverse model for golf.

At another scale, Suvrathan and colleagues found region-dependent timing
requirements for parallel-fiber/Purkinje-cell plasticity, including tuning
near 120 ms in the flocculus in the studied oculomotor conditions~\cite{Suvrathan2016}.
A synaptic timing interval, accumulated behavioral learning across trials, and
closed-loop response latency are different measurements. The first cannot be
used as a universal cortical sampling period or a golf feedback delay. Likewise,
a particular climbing-fiber teaching signal does not establish that all such
activity is the same sensory prediction error, or that LTD is the sole motor
learning mechanism.

\section{Prediction Is Not a Complete Kalman Filter}

For a linear discrete-time engineering model, write
\begin{align}
 x_{j+1}&=Ax_j+Bu_j+w_j, & y_j&=Cx_j+v_j,\\
 \hat x^-_{j+1}&=A\hat x^+_j+Bu_j, &
 \nu_{j+1}&=y_{j+1}-C\hat x^-_{j+1},\\
 \hat x^+_{j+1}&=\hat x^-_{j+1}+K_{j+1}\nu_{j+1}.
\end{align}
The state prediction has state units; the innovation $\nu$ has measurement
units; the gain maps measurement residuals into state corrections. Under the
usual known linear-model, independent zero-mean white-noise assumptions with
covariances $Q,R$, a Kalman filter also propagates
\begin{align}
 P^-_{j+1}&=AP^+_jA^T+Q,\\
 S_{j+1}&=CP^-_{j+1}C^T+R, &
 K_{j+1}&=P^-_{j+1}C^TS_{j+1}^{-1}.
\end{align}
Assume $S$ is invertible; numerical implementations solve the linear system.
The posterior covariance must also be updated, for example in Joseph form,
$(I-KC)P^-(I-KC)^T+KRK^T$. Joint Gaussian assumptions make the estimate the
conditional mean; without them, linear least-squares claims require their own
second-moment assumptions. For nonlinear models, the prediction and update
require an explicitly chosen approximation.

A biological prediction-error signal is therefore not, on its own, an
innovation \emph{update}. It does not identify $C$, $K$, covariance propagation,
or the assumptions that justify optimality. The equations offer experimentally
useful questions about uncertainty weighting; they are not anatomical labels.

\section{Basal Ganglia and Spinal Circuits}

Action selection is one useful account of basal-ganglia function, but a literal
``direct pathway on, indirect pathway off'' switch is too simple. Cui and
colleagues observed activity increases in both pathways around action initiation
in a mouse operant task~\cite{Cui2013Review}. This observation challenges an
exclusive activation story; calcium activity associated with movement is not
by itself proof of causal necessity or a complete alternative algorithm.
Neither competitive-selection language nor these data establish that the basal
ganglia solve a receding-horizon trajectory optimization problem.

Spinal motor pools, interneuronal circuits, sensory feedback, and descending
commands act together. Pattern generation and reflex pathways can contribute
to coordination (Chapter~\ref{ch:cpg}), but lower motor neuron output is not a joint
torque: activation, muscle geometry, contraction dynamics, and tendon mechanics
intervene (Volume~III). Contact then couples the body to its environment.
This is why a neural account of a golf swing must specify how its proposed
signals generate forces and how those forces alter subsequent sensory evidence.

 example, and repeat with the nonuniform population. Distinguish a zero-vector
 failure from a biased nonzero direction estimate.
 \item For a scalar estimator with prior variance four, measurement variance
 one, and $C=1$, compute the gain and posterior variance. Explain which

 models with similar unperturbed clubhead trajectories. Specify neural or
