\section{Translate a Biological Idea Into a Testable Design}

The useful transfer from biology is a question about organization: how can
mechanics, prediction, feedback, and learning share the work? A robot permits
controlled comparisons, but a successful robot controller does not identify
the controller used by a human. Conversely, failure of one implementation does
not refute every biological account with a similar diagram.

For a golf-like movement, first declare the plant: coordinates and velocities,
actuator dynamics, club flexibility if relevant, contact constraints, and the
measured task. Then define which signals the controller can observe, their
latencies and uncertainty, and its force or torque limits. Only after those
choices can an architecture be assessed for tracking, effort, loading, or
robustness. A block called ``cerebellum'' supplies none of these contracts.

\begin{insight}{Conditional Design Principles}{bio-robot-design}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Use favorable mechanics.} Compliance can store energy or shape
 a useful response, but it also adds deflection, modes, and uncertainty.
 Choose stiffness and damping for the task; rigidity and compliance are not
 universal good and bad alternatives.
 \item \textbf{Measure the computation.} Network width and depth affect cost,
 expressiveness, and implementation. Chapter~4 does not justify prescribing
 two or three layers for every real-time task. Measure worst-case latency,
 memory, approximation error, and closed-loop behavior on the target hardware.
 \item \textbf{Restrict commands deliberately.} Choose synergy rank from the
 task and retained feedback authority, not a universal count of four to eight.
 Chapter~2 distinguishes input reduction from state reduction.
 \item \textbf{Predict with an error model.} Delay compensation requires a
 plant model, delay specification, and robustness analysis. A Smith-predictor
 analogy is not a general solution for uncertain nonlinear contact systems.
 \item \textbf{State the stability domain.} Contraction or a Lyapunov
 certificate applies under its metric, domain, inputs, and model assumptions.
 For autonomous periodic motion, transverse/orbital conditions differ from
 contraction of all state directions. Impact resets require separate treatment.
 \item \textbf{Keep constraints in the objective.} Task-insensitive variation
 may still affect future feasibility, joint loads, actuator saturation, or
 balance. A funnel certificate is conditional on its verified region and
 disturbance bounds, not a promise attached to any reference trajectory.
\end{enumerate}
\end{insight}

\section{An Adaptive Law With a Complete Sign Contract}

Basis-function learning provides a useful engineering analogy to classical
cerebellar theories~\cite{Marr1969,Albus1971}. In those theories, granule-cell
expansion is not a compressed low-dimensional state representation. Purkinje
activity is not a commanded joint torque, and a continuous parameter update is
not a literal account of discrete spikes or synaptic LTD. Chapter~3 separates
these computational ideas from the experimental evidence.

To see why signs cannot be judged without a plant, consider the scalar model
\begin{equation}
 \dot x=\theta_*\phi(x,t)+u,\qquad e=x-x_d,
 \label{eq:ch11:adaptive-plant}
\end{equation}
with constant unknown $\theta_*$, known input gain $+1$, differentiable reference
$x_d$, and known feature $\phi$. Choose $k,\gamma>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 u=\dot x_d-ke-\hat\theta\phi,
 \qquad \dot{\hat\theta}=\gamma\phi e.
 \label{eq:ch11:cerebellar-control}
\end{equation}
Define $\tilde\theta=\theta_*-\hat\theta$. Then
\begin{align}
 \dot e&=-ke+\tilde\theta\phi, &
 \dot{\tilde\theta}&=-\gamma\phi e,\\
 V&=\tfrac12 e^2+\frac{\tilde\theta^2}{2\gamma}, &
 \dot V&=e(-ke+\tilde\theta\phi)
       -\tilde\theta\phi e=-ke^2\le0.
 \label{eq:ch11:adaptive-energy}
\end{align}
The matching cross terms cancel. Reversing the update sign while keeping this
plant, error, and control law unchanged loses that cancellation. Changing the
control or parameter-error convention can change the required sign; there is
no universally correct standalone matrix update.

The state, input, and feature units must also match. For dimensionless $\phi$,
$\theta_*$ and $u$ have units of $x$/time, $k$ has units of inverse time, and
$\gamma$ has units of inverse time squared. Both terms of $V$ then have units
of $x^2$. A vector generalization needs compatible input and error maps; one
cannot multiply arbitrary state errors into torque-weight updates and assume
a stability proof survives.

On an interval where solutions exist, $V(t)\le V(0)$ bounds both the tracking
and parameter errors. With bounded reference, bounded feature along the
trajectory, appropriate regularity and continued existence, $\dot e$ is
bounded and the square-integrable tracking error is uniformly continuous;
then $e(t)\to0$. This argument assumes exact model structure, no input
saturation, and no unmodeled disturbance. Those departures require additional
analysis, not silent reuse of the identity.

Tracking convergence does not imply parameter identification. If $\phi=0$,
then $e(t)=e(0)e^{-kt}$ while $\hat\theta$ never changes. Parameter convergence
requires sufficient excitation and the other identification assumptions; for
a scalar regressor, a typical excitation condition is
$\int_t^{t+T}\phi(\tau)^2\,d\tau\ge\alpha>0$. Even this condition is not a
universal finite-trial learning guarantee. The exact-model Lyapunov bound also
prevents unbounded parameter drift here without requiring excitation; noise
and model mismatch are separate reasons to consider projection, leakage, or
other robust modifications. Fifty successful trials is an experimental result
to measure, not an outcome that follows from naming a controller adaptive.

\section{A Rhythmic Torque Policy With Explicit Authority}

A periodic command is not necessarily a coupled central pattern generator.
Consider coefficients
\begin{equation}
 c_i(t)=\tfrac12[1+\sin(2\pi f t+\varphi_i)],\qquad
 \tau=Wc(t)-Ke,
 \label{eq:ch11:rhythmic-policy}
\end{equation}
where $f>0$ is in hertz, $W\in\mathbb R^{n_j\times k_c}$ has torque units,
$e=(q-q_d,v-v_d)\in\mathbb R^{2n_j}$ is a local actuated-joint error, and
$K\in\mathbb R^{n_j\times2n_j}$ has the corresponding feedback units.
The example assumes compatible local joint coordinates, not a floating-base
quaternion error representation. $W$ and $K$ are supplied explicitly; neither
random initialization nor zeros should be described as learned or stabilizing.

The feedback term acts directly in joint-torque space. If it were instead
$WK_c e$, every correction would lie in $\operatorname{col}W$. Retaining a
full joint-space $K$ can preserve additional torque directions, but it does
not prove that a particular gain stabilizes a plant. A floating base is not
directly actuated by these joint torques; contact and internal dynamics must
transmit their effect.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={A Supplied Rhythmic Policy, Without a Plant}]
from dataclasses import dataclass
import numpy as np


@dataclass
class RhythmicPolicy:
    """Finite torque weights, joint feedback, phases, Hz."""
    weights: np.ndarray
    feedback: np.ndarray
    phases: np.ndarray
    frequency_hz: float

    def __post_init__(self):
        self.weights = np.array(self.weights, dtype=float,
                                copy=True)
        self.feedback = np.array(self.feedback, dtype=float,
                                 copy=True)
        self.phases = np.array(self.phases, dtype=float,
                               copy=True)
        if self.weights.ndim != 2 or min(self.weights.shape) < 1:
            raise ValueError("Nonempty joint-by-basis weights")
        n, k = self.weights.shape
        if (self.feedback.shape != (n, 2*n)
                or self.phases.shape != (k,)):
            raise ValueError("Incompatible policy dimensions")
        if not all(np.all(np.isfinite(a)) for a in
                   (self.weights, self.feedback, self.phases)):
            raise ValueError("Policy arrays must be finite")
        if (np.ndim(self.frequency_hz) != 0
                or not np.isfinite(self.frequency_hz)
                or self.frequency_hz <= 0):
            raise ValueError("Frequency must be positive Hz")

    def command(self, time, error):
        """Return joint torques for finite local joint error."""
        error = np.asarray(error, dtype=float)
        n = self.weights.shape[0]
        if error.shape != (2*n,) or not np.all(np.isfinite(error)):
            raise ValueError("Finite position/velocity error")
        if np.ndim(time) != 0 or not np.isfinite(time):
            raise ValueError("Time must be finite seconds")
        phase = 2*np.pi*self.frequency_hz*time + self.phases
        coefficients = 0.5*(1 + np.sin(phase))
        return self.weights @ coefficients - self.feedback @ error
\end{lstlisting}

At $t=0$, $W=I_2$, phases $(0,\pi/2)$, and
$K=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&0&0\\0&0&1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
the error $e=(1,2,3,4)^T$ gives $\tau=(-1.5,-2)^T$. This checks the sign and
matrix mapping, not physical gain selection. With $W=(1,0)^T$ instead, the
same second feedback row can still command the second joint, outside the
feedforward subspace.

There is a further dimensional trap. All coefficients share one frequency, so
$c(t)=a+b\sin(2\pi ft)+d\cos(2\pi ft)$ for fixed vectors $a,b,d$.
The uncentered sampled coefficient matrix has rank at most three; after
subtracting the sample mean, rank is at most two. Feedforward torque rank is
also bounded by $\operatorname{rank}W$. Increasing the number of phase-shifted
channels does not automatically increase temporal rank. Nonlinear rectification,
multiple frequencies, coupled oscillator states, or feedback can change that
conclusion and must be analyzed separately.

This listing has no oscillator coupling, phase resetting, actuator saturation,
contact model, or plant integration. It neither demonstrates walking nor
certifies robustness. A proposed locomotion controller must add those missing
pieces and assess orbital stability and constraint feasibility under specified
perturbations. Open-loop periodic signals alone do not prove either stability
or inevitable immediate falling for every possible mechanical system.

\section{Connect the Levels Through Measured Interfaces}

A practical hierarchy can separate task planning, state estimation, trajectory
or rhythmic generation, and actuator feedback. These are engineering
responsibilities, not a one-to-one cortical/cerebellar/spinal map. Choose update
periods from measured computation, sensing, actuator bandwidth, delay, and
stability margins. The numbers 1, 10, 50, and 100 ms are not universal biological
clock rates. A slower layer must define how commands are held or interpolated
and what a faster layer does when a plan becomes infeasible.

For golf, an experiment should compare controllers on the same body and club
model with the same initial-state uncertainty, contact assumptions, and torque
limits. Report clubhead delivery together with joint loading, tracking error,
actuator work, constraint violations, and recovery from perturbations. Favorable
mechanics can reduce intervention while prediction and feedback retain needed
corrections; those effects are intertwined rather than interchangeable measures
of success. A low-dimensional nominal swing alone cannot identify which effect
produced it.

Volume~V discusses candidate software workflows. Availability of a dynamics
engine or a companion page is not evidence that this policy has been integrated
and tested in that engine. Such a claim needs a specific model, version, runnable
configuration, solver settings, and recorded results.

 \item Derive Eq.~\eqref{eq:ch11:adaptive-energy}, including units, and give the
 $\phi=0$ counterexample to parameter identification. Explain why tracking and
 learning are different outcomes.
 \item Verify the rhythmic-policy numerical example and the two temporal-rank
 bounds. Add a second frequency and examine which bound changes.
 \item Compare full joint feedback with feedback restricted through $W$ on a
 declared linear plant. Check controllability or stabilizability and actuator
 limits before interpreting a tracking plot.
 \item Design a reproducible nonlinear comparison with a baseline controller.
 Declare disturbances, contact resets, delays, metrics, and failure criteria.
 Treat successful simulation as model-specific evidence, not proof of a
 biological control mechanism.
\end{enumerate}
