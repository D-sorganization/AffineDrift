\section{Choosing the Required Mechanical Resolution}
Rigid links can approximate gross motion while failing to describe local strain,
stored elastic energy or load-dependent geometry. Whether that matters depends on
the question. Clubhead orientation may require shaft bending and torsion;
internal tissue stress requires appropriate constitutive laws, geometry and boundary
conditions. Adding finite elements alone does not validate stress estimates,
damage thresholds or biological material parameters.

A model may combine a moving rigid reference with small elastic deformation.
Small local strain does not imply small overall rigid rotation. Derive the coupled
kinetic energy and forces consistently: appending independent elastic oscillators
to rigid-body equations generally misses inertial coupling and reactions.
This connects flexible modeling to force and parameter inference in
Chapters~\ref{ch:inverse-problems} and~\ref{ch:inference}.

\section{A Stationary Planar Euler--Bernoulli Beam}
Let $x\in[0,L]$ follow an initially straight, stationary beam, with small transverse
deflection $w(x,t)$. Plane sections remain normal to the centerline; shear deformation
and cross-section rotary inertia are neglected. Let $B(x)=E(x)I(x)>0$ be bending
rigidity in $\mathrm{N\,m^2}$ and $\mu(x)>0$ mass per length in $\mathrm{kg/m}$.
For linear elasticity without damping, axial preload or initial curvature,
\begin{equation}
 T=\tfrac12\int_0^L\mu\dot w^2\,dx,\qquad
 U=\tfrac12\int_0^LB(w'')^2\,dx.
\end{equation}
The weak balance against an admissible virtual displacement $\delta w$ is
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^L\mu\ddot w\,\delta w\,dx+
 \int_0^LBw''\delta w''\,dx=\int_0^Lf\delta w\,dx,
\end{equation}
with point-load work if present. Integrating twice in space gives
\begin{equation}
 (B(x)w'')''+\mu(x)\ddot w=f(x,t).
 \label{eq:ch9:euler-bernoulli}
\end{equation}
Here $f$ is force per length in $\mathrm{N/m}$. The constant-rigidity form
$B\partial_x^4w+\mu\ddot w=f$ is a special case. Weak formulations and cubic Hermite
interpolation are treated in the TU Delft beam notes~\cite{DelftBeamReview}.

For a clamped root, $w(0)=w'(0)=0$. A bare unloaded tip has $Bw''(L)=0$ and
$(Bw'')'(L)=0$. A point mass $m_t$ adds $\tfrac12m_t\dot w(L)^2$ to kinetic energy
and $m_t\ddot w(L)\delta w(L)$ to the weak balance. The resulting boundary condition
is $(Bw'')'(L)=m_t\ddot w(L)$ under this energy convention. For
$w=\phi e^{i\omega t}$ it becomes $(B\phi'')'(L)=-m_t\omega^2\phi(L)$.
No offset center of mass or head rotary inertia is included in this point-mass model.

\subsection{Finite Elements and Modal Coordinates}
On an element of length $h$, let $\xi=x/h$ and
\begin{align}
 N_1&=1-3\xi^2+2\xi^3,&N_2&=h(\xi-2\xi^2+\xi^3),\\
 N_3&=3\xi^2-2\xi^3,&N_4&=h(-\xi^2+\xi^3).
\end{align}
The nodal vector is $(w_1,w'_1,w_2,w'_2)^T$; translation and slope entries have
different units. Substituting $w=N d_e$ gives
\begin{equation}
 K_e=\int B(N'')^TN''\,dx,\qquad M_e=\int\mu N^TN\,dx.
\end{equation}
The listing integrates these products exactly for constant element properties.
A supplied linear rigidity taper is approximated by its midpoint value on each
element. This is not exact integration of the variable-rigidity curvature product.
Mass per length is independently constant. A geometry-based taper requires
consistent $I(x)$ and $A(x)$ rather than assuming they vary identically.

After imposing root constraints, solve $K\phi_j=\omega_j^2M\phi_j$ with
$\phi_i^TM\phi_j=\delta_{ij}$. The symmetric generalized eigensolver uses this
normalization~\cite{SciPyEighBeam}. Returned columns include the two zero root
entries. Plot even rows for transverse displacement; odd rows are slopes.
Mode sign is arbitrary and magnitude depends on normalization. Negative eigenvalues
must not be converted into frequencies using absolute values. This positive clamped
model rejects nonpositive results, which may indicate invalid assembly, numerical
conditioning or a model outside the stated assumptions.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Stationary Planar Cantilever With Optional Point Tip Mass.}]
from dataclasses import dataclass
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import eigh

@dataclass
class BeamParameters:
    """SI parameters of a stationary planar cantilever, not a full club."""
    length: float
    rigidity_root: float
    rigidity_tip: float
    mass_per_length: float
    tip_mass: float = 0.0

def beam_matrices(parameters, n_elements):
    """Assemble Hermite elements with piecewise-constant midpoint rigidity."""
    values = np.array([parameters.length, parameters.rigidity_root,
                       parameters.rigidity_tip, parameters.mass_per_length,
                       parameters.tip_mass], dtype=float)
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(values)) or np.any(values[:4] <= 0) or values[4] < 0:
        raise ValueError("Positive beam properties and nonnegative tip mass required")
    if isinstance(n_elements, bool) or not isinstance(n_elements, (int, np.integer)):
        raise ValueError("Element count must be an integer")
    if n_elements < 2:
        raise ValueError("At least two elements required")
    length, root, tip, density, tip_mass = values
    step = length / n_elements
    local_k = np.array([[12, 6*step, -12, 6*step],
        [6*step, 4*step**2, -6*step, 2*step**2],
        [-12, -6*step, 12, -6*step],
        [6*step, 2*step**2, -6*step, 4*step**2]]) / step**3
    local_m = density * step / 420 * np.array([
        [156, 22*step, 54, -13*step],
        [22*step, 4*step**2, 13*step, -3*step**2],
        [54, 13*step, 156, -22*step],
        [-13*step, -3*step**2, -22*step, 4*step**2]])
    rigidity = root + (tip-root) * (np.arange(n_elements)+0.5) / n_elements
    dofs = 2*np.arange(n_elements)[:, None] + np.arange(4)
    stiffness = np.zeros((2*n_elements+2, 2*n_elements+2))
    mass = np.zeros_like(stiffness)
    indices = (dofs[:, :, None], dofs[:, None, :])
    np.add.at(stiffness, indices, rigidity[:, None, None] * local_k)
    np.add.at(mass, indices, np.broadcast_to(local_m, (n_elements, 4, 4)))
    mass[-2, -2] += tip_mass
    return stiffness, mass

def shaft_modal_analysis(parameters, n_elements=20):
    """Return up to six frequencies and full nodal mass-normalized modes."""
    stiffness, mass = beam_matrices(parameters, n_elements)
    eigenvalues, vectors = eigh(stiffness[2:, 2:], mass[2:, 2:])
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(eigenvalues)) or np.any(eigenvalues <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Cantilever eigenvalues must be finite and positive")
    count = min(6, eigenvalues.size)
    modes = np.zeros((mass.shape[0], count))
    modes[2:] = vectors[:, :count]
    return np.sqrt(eigenvalues[:count]) / (2*np.pi), modes
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Verification Before Interpreting a Shaft}
For a uniform bare cantilever, separation and the four boundary conditions give
\[\cos\beta_j\cosh\beta_j=-1.\]
The first dimensionless roots are $1.87510407$,
$4.69409113$, $7.85475744$, so
\begin{equation}
 f_j=\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\pi L^2}\sqrt{B/\mu}.
\end{equation}
Manufactured $L=1\,\mathrm m$, $B=1\,\mathrm{N\,m^2}$ and
$\mu=1\,\mathrm{kg/m}$ give approximately $0.5596$, $3.5069$ and
$9.8194\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Mesh refinement must approach these values. Multiplying
$B$ by four doubles frequencies; doubling $L$ quarters them for a uniform beam
with unchanged $B,\mu$. These checks test more than reproduction of a single plot.

At fixed $K$, adding nonnegative point mass increases the mass quadratic form.
The Rayleigh quotient $d^TKd/(d^TMd)$ cannot increase for any fixed admissible
nonzero $d$; the variational eigenvalue characterization makes ordered frequencies
nonincreasing. This does not determine a universal driver frequency. Grip
compliance, head inertia and offsets, bending plane, geometry, preload and shaft
construction change the eigenproblem.

\subsection{Deflection, Forcing and Timescale}
For a uniform beam under static tip force $F$, solving the equilibrium equation
with tip moment zero gives $w(L)=FL^3/(3B)$. This is a linear static benchmark,
not an impact-deflection prediction. During a swing, transverse gravity components,
base acceleration and rotation, head inertia and center-of-mass offsets can all
contribute. Toe-down and lead/lag labels require declared club axes. Assigning
droop entirely to gravity or to one inertial term without a load balance is unjustified.

With fixed boundaries, truncate $w=\sum_{j=1}^r\phi_j\eta_j$. Under an appropriate
diagonal damping model, mass-normalized coordinates satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \ddot\eta_j+2\zeta_j\omega_j\dot\eta_j+\omega_j^2\eta_j=Q_j.
\end{equation}
For a distributed load alone, $Q_j=\int_0^L\phi_j(x)f(x,t)\,dx$; point loads add their modal work terms. In a finite element model, $Q_j=\phi_j^Tf_{\rm nodal}$.
For sinusoidal forcing at angular frequency $\Omega$, neglecting inertia requires
$\Omega/\omega_j\ll1$, with damping and transient terms also negligible relative
to stiffness. Counting natural cycles during a downswing alone does not justify
a quasi-static approximation. Initial vibration, impact and rapidly changing forcing
may remain important.

\section{Beyond the Beam Approximation}
The assembled equation $M\ddot d+C\dot d+Kd=f$ describes a particular linearized
model with specified damping and boundaries. Large deformation, nonlinear material
response and changing contact generally produce state-dependent internal forces
and possibly constraints. Mesh convergence verifies discretization of a chosen
model; it cannot establish the constitutive law or supply missing measurements.
Shaft compliance changes clubhead motion and interaction loads, so rigid-body
inference and flexible dynamics need consistent geometry, frames and force conventions.
