\section{What the Data Can Identify}
Joint geometry, tissue properties, external loads and control inputs jointly shape
motion. Estimating one while treating the others as known can transfer their errors
into the fitted parameter. Some quantities can be measured or constrained by
additional experiments; others require inference through a measurement model.
A fitted parameter is conditional on the model, measurement protocol, initial state
and calibration, rather than a direct reading of biological truth.

Write a forward prediction as
\begin{equation}
 \dot x=f(x,u,\theta),\qquad
 \hat y_k=H_k(x(t_k),\theta),\qquad y_k=\hat y_k+\epsilon_k.
\end{equation}
Here $H_k$ includes the measured quantity, frame, sensor locations and known
processing. Unknown initial conditions and calibration constants belong among the
unknowns or must be propagated as uncertainty. Numerical differentiation of motion
does not create an independent force measurement.
Chapter~\ref{ch:experimental} explains why processing and synchronization matter.

\subsection{Structural and Practical Identifiability}
For a declared input and observation experiment, structural identifiability asks
whether distinct admissible parameter vectors can produce exactly the same ideal
observations. Global uniqueness is stronger than uniqueness near one vector.
Practical identifiability asks how reliably finite noisy data constrain those
parameters. A well-conditioned optimizer is neither definition.

Consider the manufactured free decay
\begin{equation}
 m\ddot q+c\dot q+kq=0,\qquad m>0.
\end{equation}
With known initial conditions and only $q(t)$ observed, replacing $(m,c,k)$ by
$(sm,sc,sk)$, $s>0$, leaves the equation unchanged after division by $m$.
Thus the experiment cannot identify their absolute scale. It can constrain $c/m$
and $k/m$ only if the trajectory is informative; a stationary zero trajectory does
not even determine those ratios. A measured applied force or independently known
inertia can break the scale ambiguity in a suitably designed experiment.
More samples of the identical free decay cannot do so.

Stack predictions into $\hat y(\theta)$ and examine sensitivity
$S=\partial\hat y/\partial\theta$. Under fixed Gaussian noise with stacked covariance
$\Sigma$, $S^T\Sigma^{-1}S$ describes local data information. Null or small singular
directions expose missing or weak sensitivity after sensible parameter scaling.
Full local rank does not exclude distant equivalent solutions or establish that
the chosen physical model is correct.

\section{Likelihood, Prior and Numerical Objective}
Assume independent $d$-channel errors $\epsilon_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$ with a known,
fixed, symmetric positive-definite $R$. The Gaussian density gives
\begin{equation}
 -\log p(y\mid\theta)=\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^N r_k^TR^{-1}r_k+
 \tfrac N2\log\det R+\tfrac{Nd}{2}\log(2\pi),
 \quad r_k=y_k-\hat y_k(\theta).
\end{equation}
The normalization and covariance requirements follow the multivariate Gaussian
model~\cite{SciPyGaussianInference}. Only terms constant in the optimized parameters
may be dropped. Estimating $R$, comparing model evidence, or using parameter-dependent
noise requires retaining the appropriate normalization. Time-correlated errors
require a joint covariance or an explicit stochastic model, rather than treating
filtered samples as independent replicates. For the independent case,
$\Sigma=I_N\otimes R$.

For a fixed Gaussian prior with mean $\mu$ and covariance $P\succ0$, the negative
log-posterior up to an additive constant is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(\theta)=\tfrac12\sum_k r_k^TR^{-1}r_k+
 \tfrac12(\theta-\mu)^TP^{-1}(\theta-\mu).
 \label{eq:ch8:map-objective}
\end{equation}
Minimizing this objective gives a mode candidate. A prior constrains an ambiguous
fit using additional assumptions; it does not make the measurements identify an
unobserved quantity. Document independent prior evidence and check sensitivity to
its strength and form.

\subsection{A Checked Fixed-Covariance Listing}
The listing requires an $(N,d)$ observation matrix and predictions of exactly the
same shape. Scalar time series must be explicitly shaped $(N,1)$. Cholesky factors
whiten residuals without repeatedly constructing covariance inverses. Singular or
parameter-dependent covariances need a different likelihood implementation.
The forward callback must be deterministic, valid throughout the search domain,
and accurate enough for numerical optimization. This listing omits an analytic
gradient, so the optimizer uses finite differences; forward-solver error must be
small enough to resolve those perturbations.

Optional finite box bounds describe a Gaussian prior restricted to that box;
its fixed normalization does not change the mode. Bounds encode the physical domain,
not a repair for an invalid model. A Gaussian on log-parameters specifies a different
prior. Modes depend on parameterization: transforming a density requires its
Jacobian, so exponentiating a mode in log-space need not give the physical-coordinate
mode. The optimizer reports a local candidate, not a global-uniqueness certificate.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Checked Fixed-Covariance Gaussian MAP Estimation.}]
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Callable
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import solve_triangular
from scipy.optimize import minimize

@dataclass
class GaussianProblem:
    """Fixed covariances; observations have shape (samples, channels)."""
    observations: np.ndarray
    forward_model: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray]
    prior_mean: np.ndarray
    prior_cov: np.ndarray
    noise_cov: np.ndarray
    bounds: list[tuple[float, float]] | None = None

def covariance_factor(value, size):
    """Require a finite symmetric positive-definite covariance."""
    matrix = np.asarray(value, dtype=float)
    if matrix.shape != (size, size) or not np.all(np.isfinite(matrix)):
        raise ValueError("Invalid covariance shape or entries")
    if not np.allclose(matrix, matrix.T, rtol=1e-12, atol=0):
        raise ValueError("Covariance must be symmetric")
    try:
        return np.linalg.cholesky(matrix)
    except np.linalg.LinAlgError as error:
        raise ValueError("Covariance must be positive definite") from error

def gaussian_objective(problem):
    """Factor fixed covariances once and return a checked objective."""
    data = np.asarray(problem.observations, dtype=float)
    mean = np.asarray(problem.prior_mean, dtype=float)
    if data.ndim != 2 or 0 in data.shape or not np.all(np.isfinite(data)):
        raise ValueError("Observations must be a finite nonempty matrix")
    if mean.ndim != 1 or mean.size == 0 or not np.all(np.isfinite(mean)):
        raise ValueError("Prior mean must be a finite nonempty vector")
    noise = covariance_factor(problem.noise_cov, data.shape[1])
    prior = covariance_factor(problem.prior_cov, mean.size)
    def objective(theta):
        predicted = np.asarray(problem.forward_model(theta), dtype=float)
        if predicted.shape != data.shape or not np.all(np.isfinite(predicted)):
            raise ValueError("Prediction must match observations exactly")
        residual = solve_triangular(noise, (data - predicted).T, lower=True)
        deviation = solve_triangular(prior, theta - mean, lower=True)
        value = 0.5 * (np.sum(residual**2) + deviation @ deviation)
        if not np.isfinite(value):
            raise ValueError("Nonfinite objective")
        return float(value)
    return objective

def map_estimate(problem, initial):
    """Return a local mode candidate; raise on invalid input or failure."""
    objective = gaussian_objective(problem)
    start = np.asarray(initial, dtype=float)
    if start.shape != np.shape(problem.prior_mean) or not np.all(np.isfinite(start)):
        raise ValueError("Initial parameters must match prior mean")
    if problem.bounds is not None:
        limits = np.asarray(problem.bounds, dtype=float)
        if limits.shape != (start.size, 2) or not np.all(np.isfinite(limits)):
            raise ValueError("Bounds must be a finite (parameters, 2) matrix")
        if np.any(limits[:, 0] >= limits[:, 1]):
            raise ValueError("Lower bounds must be below upper bounds")
        if np.any(start < limits[:, 0]) or np.any(start > limits[:, 1]):
            raise ValueError("Initial parameters must lie inside bounds")
    objective(start)
    result = minimize(objective, start, method="L-BFGS-B", bounds=problem.bounds)
    if not result.success:
        raise RuntimeError(str(result.message))
    if result.x.shape != start.shape or not np.all(np.isfinite(result.x)):
        raise RuntimeError("Optimizer returned invalid parameters")
    objective(result.x)
    return result.x
\end{lstlisting}

\section{A Mode Is Not a Posterior Distribution}
For manufactured scalar observations $y_k=\theta+\epsilon_k$, take $y=(1,3)$,
$R=1$, $\mu=0$, $P=1$. Completing the square yields
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(\theta)=\tfrac12[(1-\theta)^2+(3-\theta)^2+\theta^2]
 =\tfrac32(\theta-4/3)^2+\text{constant}.
\end{equation}
The exact unbounded posterior has mean and mode $4/3$, variance $1/3$.
With a box $[0,1]$, the constrained mode is instead $1$; the unbounded Gaussian is
no longer the posterior distribution. A boundary optimum need not have zero
unconstrained gradient.

For nonlinear predictions, an interior mode may support a local Gaussian
approximation using the Hessian of $\Phi$, if positive definite and locally adequate.
The data Hessian contains both sensitivity products and residual-weighted second
derivatives of predictions. Replacing it by $S^T\Sigma^{-1}S$ is a Gauss--Newton
approximation, not an identity in general. Multimodality, weak directions and boundary
mass can invalidate a single Gaussian summary. Posterior sampling or other
approximations also need convergence checks.

\section{From Identification to a Testable Golf Model}
Perturbation experiments can constrain effective joint impedance, but posture,
feedback delay, contact and activation all affect the response. Fitting a stiffness
term does not separate tendon elasticity from active feedback. Force-angle fitting
may constrain combinations of force scale and moment arm.
Chapter~\ref{ch:inverse-problems} explains why a net load does not uniquely allocate
individual muscle forces. Report the quantities and predictions that the actual
experiment supports.

A learned predictor or physics-informed loss adds modeling assumptions. A finite
penalty on dynamics residuals does not enforce exact dynamics, and exact equation
satisfaction would not prove correct parameters. Evaluate residuals on independent
points and predictions on held-out trials or perturbations, including measurement
uncertainty and differences between subjects. A useful golf comparison asks whether
a changed grip, load or timing condition distinguishes competing mechanisms,
rather than whether each can fit the same swing.
