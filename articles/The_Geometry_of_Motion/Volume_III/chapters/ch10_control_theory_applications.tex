\section{A Forward Model Must Declare Its State}
Forward simulation predicts motion conditional on a mechanical model, initial state,
external loads and control law. In general coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dot q=N(q)v,\qquad
 M(q)\dot v+h(q,v)=R_v(q)F+J_c(q)^T\lambda+Q_{\rm known}.
 \label{eq:ch10:forward-dynamics}
\end{equation}
Here $h$ contains the declared velocity and gravity terms, $R_v$ maps muscle forces
to generalized forces conjugate to $v$, and $J_cv$ is the velocity conjugate to
contact wrench $\lambda$. Configuration dimension need not equal velocity dimension;
a quaternion-based floating body is one reason to retain $N(q)$.

Activation, fiber length and other internal states require equations consistent with
the force law. Rigid-tendon force models and compliant-tendon state models are
different contracts. A promised muscle-force callback must enter the dynamics.
Using $F_i=F_{0i}a_i$ instead is an idealized actuator law without force-length,
force-velocity or tendon compliance. Chapter~\ref{ch:muscle-models} develops those
additional assumptions.

Maintained rigid contacts require compatible motion and solved reaction forces;
reduced coordinates may yield a smooth ODE within a fixed contact mode. Contact
creation, separation and impacts need a declared contact law and transition treatment.
An unconstrained integrator does not supply those mechanics. Numerical stiffness
and discontinuities determine solver choices; neither explicit nor implicit
integration alone establishes physical validity.

\section{A Reproducible Antagonist-Driven Rotor}
Consider one unwrapped angle $q$ with velocity $v$, constant inertia $J>0$, equal
force scales $F_0>0$ and opposite constant moment arms $+r,-r$. Let $b\ge0$ be
viscous damping and $\gamma\ge0$ the coefficient of a restoring gravitational
torque. Use one common activation time $\tau_a>0$:
\begin{align}
 \dot q&=v, &J\dot v&=rF_0(a_1-a_2)-bv-\gamma\sin q,\\
 \dot a_i&=(u_i-a_i)/\tau_a,&0\le u_i&\le1.
\end{align}
Units are $J$ in $\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$, $r$ in meters, $F_0$ in newtons, $b$ in
$\mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}$, $\gamma$ in $\mathrm{N\,m}$ and $\tau_a$ in seconds.
A hanging mass distribution would impose a physical relation between $J$ and
$\gamma$; here they are independently declared toy parameters. There is no contact,
fiber state, elastic antagonist stiffness or metabolic model.

At $a=0$ the activation derivative is nonnegative; at $a=1$ it is nonpositive.
The continuous equation preserves the interval for admissible input and initial
state. This proof does not guarantee exact numerical bounds. The listing checks
returned samples with a declared $10^{-8}$ numerical allowance and retains raw
results. It uses smooth input, checks completion and returns scalar $q,v,\tau$
arrays and an $(N,2)$ activation matrix. For discontinuous input, split integration
at known switches; adaptive sampling can miss narrow input features. Solver success
is a numerical status~\cite{SciPyIVPRotor}, not an anatomical conclusion.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Antagonist-Driven Rotor With First-Order Activation.}]
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Callable
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

@dataclass
class RotorParameters:
    """SI constants for an illustrative antagonist-driven rotor."""
    inertia: float
    moment_arm: float
    force_scale: float
    activation_time: float
    damping: float = 0.0
    gravity_scale: float = 0.0

def rotor_inputs(times, initial, parameters):
    """Validate the finite time grid, state and physical parameters."""
    grid = np.asarray(times, dtype=float)
    state = np.asarray(initial, dtype=float)
    values = np.array([parameters.inertia, parameters.moment_arm,
        parameters.force_scale, parameters.activation_time,
        parameters.damping, parameters.gravity_scale], dtype=float)
    if grid.ndim != 1 or grid.size < 2 or not np.all(np.isfinite(grid)):
        raise ValueError("Times must be a finite vector with at least two entries")
    if np.any(np.diff(grid) <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Times must increase strictly")
    if state.shape != (4,) or not np.all(np.isfinite(state)):
        raise ValueError("State must be [q, velocity, activation1, activation2]")
    if np.any(state[2:] < 0) or np.any(state[2:] > 1):
        raise ValueError("Initial activations must lie in [0, 1]")
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(values)) or np.any(values[:4] <= 0) or np.any(values[4:] < 0):
        raise ValueError("Invalid rotor parameters")
    return grid, state

def simulate_rotor(excitation: Callable, times, initial, parameters):
    """Integrate a smooth-input toy rotor; no contact or fiber dynamics."""
    grid, state = rotor_inputs(times, initial, parameters)
    scale = parameters.moment_arm * parameters.force_scale
    def dynamics(time, current):
        control = np.asarray(excitation(time), dtype=float)
        if control.shape != (2,) or not np.all(np.isfinite(control)):
            raise ValueError("Excitation must be a finite two-vector")
        if np.any(control < 0) or np.any(control > 1):
            raise ValueError("Excitation must lie in [0, 1]")
        position, velocity = current[:2]
        activation = current[2:]
        torque = scale * (activation[0] - activation[1])
        acceleration = (torque - parameters.damping * velocity
                        - parameters.gravity_scale * np.sin(position)) / parameters.inertia
        rate = (control - activation) / parameters.activation_time
        return np.r_[velocity, acceleration, rate]
    solution = solve_ivp(dynamics, (grid[0], grid[-1]), state,
                         t_eval=grid, rtol=1e-9, atol=1e-11)
    if not solution.success:
        raise RuntimeError(str(solution.message))
    if solution.y.shape != (4, grid.size) or not np.all(np.isfinite(solution.y)):
        raise RuntimeError("Incomplete or nonfinite solution")
    activation = solution.y[2:].T
    if np.any(activation < -1e-8) or np.any(activation > 1+1e-8):
        raise RuntimeError("Numerical activation bounds violated")
    return {"t": solution.t, "q": solution.y[0], "qdot": solution.y[1],
            "a": activation, "tau": scale * (activation[:, 0] - activation[:, 1])}
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Exact Activation, Motion and Mechanical Power}
For constant excitation $\bar u$, with elapsed time $s=t-t_0$,
\begin{equation}
 a_i(s)=\bar u_i+(a_i(0)-\bar u_i)e^{-s/\tau_a}.
\end{equation}
If $b=\gamma=0$, set $A=rF_0/J$, $d=\bar u_1-\bar u_2$ and
$c=a_1(0)-a_2(0)-d$. Direct integration gives
\begin{align}
 v(s)&=v_0+A[d s+c\tau_a(1-e^{-s/\tau_a})],\\
 q(s)&=q_0+v_0s+A[d s^2/2+c\tau_a\{s-\tau_a(1-e^{-s/\tau_a})\}].
\end{align}
These are independent trajectory benchmarks, including nonzero initial activation.
For the full declared rotor, differentiate its mechanical energy:
\begin{equation}
 E=\tfrac12Jv^2+\gamma(1-\cos q),\qquad
 \dot E=rF_0(a_1-a_2)v-bv^2.
 \label{eq:ch10:power}
\end{equation}
Activation is a command filter with no declared energy storage, so it is absent
from $E$. Actuator mechanical power does not determine metabolic expenditure.
Compare energy change with integrated power and refine both ODE and quadrature
errors. Agreement checks model consistency, not a human energy budget.

Equal antagonist activation gives zero net torque here. It does not increase
mechanical stiffness, because force scales and moment arms have no position
dependence. Biological co-contraction can affect stiffness through additional
force, geometry, compliance and feedback mechanisms that must be modeled.
This connects to the stiffness analysis of Chapter~\ref{ch:multibody-bio}.

\section{Prediction, Optimization and Optimality}
A trajectory optimization may minimize
\begin{equation}
 J[u]=\Psi(x(T))+\int_0^T L(x,u)\,dt,
 \qquad \dot x=f(x,u),\quad u\in U,
\end{equation}
subject to declared initial, terminal and path constraints. Tracking observations
and predicting motion from a task-level cost answer different conditional questions.
An effort penalty is a hypothesis, not automatically a metabolic law or the
objective used by the nervous system.

Pontryagin's maximum principle supplies necessary conditions under its regularity
and problem assumptions. In a normal smooth case, with maximizing convention
$H=p^Tf-L$, they include $\dot x=H_p$, $\dot p=-H_x$ and Hamiltonian maximization
along a candidate, with appropriate endpoint conditions. Abnormal cases retain a
cost multiplier; path constraints and hybrid transitions require corresponding
extensions. Neither existence of an optimum nor global optimality of a computed
candidate follows from these conditions alone~\cite{LiberzonControlReview}.
Existence arguments and numerical convergence are separate tasks. Discretized
solutions need mesh, feasibility and sensitivity checks; the method of
characteristics is not a general guarantee for a constrained biological optimizer.

\section{What a Golf-Swing Analysis Can Conclude}
Coordinate count depends on the chosen segments and joints. Ground contact
constrains motion and supplies external wrench; whether it creates a kinematic loop
depends on the whole contact and joint graph. Shaft bending changes clubhead
orientation and transmitted loads. Moment arms couple geometry to force and power.
These connections prevent independent fits from automatically forming one
consistent mechanical explanation.

Motion capture estimates kinematics under reconstruction assumptions. Force plates
measure support wrenches under calibration and contact assignments. EMG measures
electrical activity processed into a signal related to excitation; it does not
measure force or mechanical activation directly. Inverse dynamics estimates net
generalized loads conditional on those inputs. Muscle allocation adds capacity and
objective assumptions. Forward reproduction tests consistency, but reusing a fitted
trial does not provide independent predictive validation.

A wrist-release trajectory can arise from interacting active and passive terms.
Demonstrating passive release in a simplified model establishes a possible mechanism
under its conditions, not its exclusive cause in a golfer. Compare interventions,
measured loads and out-of-sample predictions to distinguish mechanisms. For assistive
devices, impedance or tracking controllers also need actuator, sensor, contact,
delay and human-interaction evaluation. A simulated result does not establish
suitability for a person.
