\section{The Hill-Type Muscle Model}

A joint moment is not yet a muscle model. To predict how excitation becomes
force, we must specify activation, fiber length and velocity, tendon stretch,
and the geometry through which tension acts on the skeleton. These quantities
evolve together: joint motion changes the muscle path, while muscle tension
changes joint motion.

Hill's shortening experiments motivate a force--velocity relation
\cite{Hill1938}; modern Hill-type models combine phenomenological constitutive
curves with selected mechanical and physiological states. They form a family,
not one universal equation. We use a massless, undamped equilibrium architecture,
constant-thickness pennation, and explicitly declared fiber curves.
Thelen's formulation and Millard's equilibrium, damped-equilibrium and
rigid-tendon alternatives make different numerical choices
\cite{Thelen2003,millard2013flexing}. A force evaluator is not by itself a
simulation of any of these complete systems.

\subsection{Model Architecture}

The three-element network used here has the following components.
The diagram shows mechanical connectivity, not literal fiber orientation:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Contractile Element (CE)}: represents the active force-generating
        capacity of the muscle fibers. Its force depends on activation $a(t)$, fiber
        length $\ell^M$, and fiber velocity $v^M = \dot{\ell}^M$.
  \item \textbf{Parallel Elastic Element (PE)}: represents the passive elastic
        properties of the muscle belly (titin filaments, connective tissue). In this particular curve, passive tension begins above optimal fiber length;
        that threshold is a modeling assumption, not a universal resting length.
  \item \textbf{Series Elastic Element (SE)}: represents the tendon, which is in
        series with the parallel CE--PE combination. Modeled here as a lossless nonlinear spring with a
        slack length $\ell_T^s$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Force-Length Relationship}

The isometric (zero velocity) force of the contractile element depends on the
normalized fiber length $\tilde{\ell} = \ell^M / \ell_0^M$, where
$\ell_0^M$ is optimal fiber length. Let $F_0$ be the full-activation,
optimal-length isometric \emph{active fiber} force scale:

\begin{definition}{Active Force-Length Curve}{fl-curve}
  The normalized active force-length relationship is:
  \begin{equation}
    f_L(\tilde{\ell}) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\tilde{\ell} - 1)^2}{\gamma}\right),
    \label{eq:ch3:force-length}
  \end{equation}
  where $\gamma \approx 0.45$ \cite{Thelen2003} controls the width of the
  curve. Maximum force ($f_L = 1$) occurs at optimal fiber length ($\tilde{\ell} = 1$).
  This Gaussian is strictly positive at finite lengths. At both
  $\tilde{\ell}=0.5$ and $1.5$ it gives $0.574$, not nearly zero.
  The half-height interval is
  $|\tilde{\ell}-1|<\sqrt{\gamma\ln 2}$, approximately $(0.442,1.558)$
  for this width. These are properties of the approximation, not measured
  anatomical operating limits.
\end{definition}

\begin{quote}
  \textit{Parameter and evidence scope.}
  Thelen's Appendix uses
  $\gamma=0.45$, $A_f=0.25$, $k^{PE}=5$, passive reference strain $\varepsilon_0=0.6$,
  and the activation-dependent law in \eqref{eq:ch3:force-velocity}
  \cite{Thelen2003}.
  These are model parameters, not universal human constants. Millard uses
  separately constructed smooth curves rather than establishing these
  numbers as shared defaults \cite{millard2013flexing}.
\end{quote}

Filament overlap helps explain length-dependent active tension. Gordon,
Huxley and Julian studied tetanized single frog fibers and found a plateau
and distinct ascending and descending regions \cite{GordonHuxleyJulian1966}.
That evidence supports length dependence; it does not identify this Gaussian
as a microscopic law or validate its extrapolation to every human muscle.

The passive curve is
\begin{equation}
 f_{PE}(\tilde{\ell})=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&0<\tilde{\ell}\leq1,\\
 \dfrac{\exp[k^{PE}(\tilde{\ell}-1)/\varepsilon_0]-1}
       {\exp(k^{PE})-1},&\tilde{\ell}>1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:ch3:passive-force}
\end{equation}
It is continuous, nonnegative, and equals one at
$\tilde{\ell}=1+\varepsilon_0$. Its derivative changes at the slack threshold.
The exponential has no finite global maximum, so a force surface needs a
declared length domain.

\subsection{A Constitutive Evaluator With Declared Inputs}

The listing takes fiber length and velocity as inputs and returns tendon-axis
projections. It does \emph{not} infer those inputs from joint motion or enforce
tendon equilibrium. The stored tendon slack length becomes relevant when
the series element and path geometry are supplied. The biceps force and length
scales reproduce \texttt{BIClong} in the pinned OpenSim Arm26 model
\cite{OpenSimArm26Pinned}; they do not describe both biceps heads or an
individual golfer. The full-activation speed scale is explicitly chosen as
$10\ell_0^M/\mathrm{s}$ for this example.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Prescribed-Fiber Constitutive Evaluation.}]
from dataclasses import dataclass
import math

def finite_values(values: tuple[float, ...]) -> None:
    """Reject nonfinite scalar inputs."""
    if not all(math.isfinite(value) for value in values):
        raise ValueError("Inputs must be finite.")

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class MuscleParameters:
    """Fiber constitutive parameters; SI units, radians."""
    F0: float
    l0_M: float
    v0: float
    l_T_slack: float
    alpha0: float
    gamma: float = 0.45
    Af: float = 0.25
    kPE: float = 5.0
    e0: float = 0.6

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        values = (self.F0, self.l0_M, self.v0,
                  self.l_T_slack, self.gamma,
                  self.Af, self.kPE, self.e0)
        finite_values((*values, self.alpha0))
        if min(values) <= 0 or not 0 <= self.alpha0 < math.pi / 2:
            raise ValueError("Positive scales and 0 <= alpha0 < pi/2 required.")

    @property
    def v_max(self) -> float:
        """Full-activation speed scale in fiber lengths/s."""
        return self.v0 / self.l0_M

def force_length_active(l_tilde: float, gamma: float = 0.45) -> float:
    """Normalized active fiber force at zero velocity."""
    finite_values((l_tilde, gamma))
    if l_tilde <= 0 or gamma <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Positive length and width required.")
    return math.exp(-((l_tilde - 1.0) ** 2) / gamma)

def force_length_passive(l_tilde: float, kPE: float = 5.0,
                         e0: float = 0.6) -> float:
    """Passive fiber tension, normalized by F0."""
    finite_values((l_tilde, kPE, e0))
    if l_tilde <= 0 or kPE <= 0 or e0 <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Positive length and shape scales required.")
    if l_tilde <= 1.0:
        return 0.0
    try:
        return math.expm1(kPE * (l_tilde - 1.0) / e0) / math.expm1(kPE)
    except OverflowError as error:
        raise ValueError("Constitutive extrapolation exceeds numeric range.") from error

def force_velocity(v_tilde: float, a: float, Af: float = 0.25,
                   Flen: float = 1.4) -> float:
    """Thelen interior multiplier; v_tilde = v_M / v0."""
    finite_values((v_tilde, a, Af, Flen))
    if not 0 <= a <= 1 or Af <= 0 or Flen <= 1:
        raise ValueError("Require a in [0,1], Af > 0, Flen > 1.")
    speed = 0.25 + 0.75 * a
    if v_tilde < -speed:
        raise ValueError("Shortening exceeds the declared interior branch.")
    w = v_tilde / speed
    if w <= 0:
        return (1.0 + w) / (1.0 - w / Af)
    shape = (2.0 + 2.0 / Af) / (Flen - 1.0)
    # Algebraic rearrangement avoids multiplying two large velocities.
    return Flen - (Flen - 1.0) / (1.0 + shape * w)

def muscle_force(activation: float, l_M: float, v_M: float,
                 params: MuscleParameters) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Tendon-axis projections for PRESCRIBED fiber state, in N.

    Returns total, active and passive projections. This does not
    solve tendon equilibrium or determine the fiber state.
    """
    finite_values((activation, l_M, v_M))
    if not 0 <= activation <= 1:
        raise ValueError("Activation must lie in [0,1].")
    thickness = params.l0_M * math.sin(params.alpha0)
    if l_M <= thickness:
        raise ValueError("Require fiber length greater than thickness.")
    cos_alpha = math.sqrt(1.0 - (thickness / l_M) ** 2)
    length = l_M / params.l0_M
    active = (activation * force_length_active(length, params.gamma)
              * force_velocity(v_M / params.v0, activation, params.Af))
    passive = force_length_passive(length, params.kPE, params.e0)
    scale = params.F0 * cos_alpha
    result = ((active + passive) * scale, active * scale, passive * scale)
    finite_values(result)
    return result

# Long-head biceps geometry/force scales from OpenSim Arm26 BIClong.
# v0 and curve parameters are declared teaching choices.
biceps = MuscleParameters(F0=624.3, l0_M=0.1157,
                          v0=10 * 0.1157,
                          l_T_slack=0.2723, alpha0=0.0)
total, active, passive = muscle_force(0.5, biceps.l0_M, 0.0, biceps)
print(f"Prescribed isometric fiber: {total:.2f} N "
      f"(active {active:.2f}, passive {passive:.2f})")
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The Force-Velocity Relationship}

Let $v_0>0$ be the dimensional full-activation shortening-speed scale and
$\tilde v=v^M/v_0$, with negative velocity denoting shortening.
Here $A_f=0.25$ controls the shortening branch and $F_{\rm len}=1.4$
is the dimensionless lengthening asymptote used by the listing.
For the interior branches obtained from Thelen's equations, define
\[
 c(a)=0.25+0.75a,\qquad w=\tilde v/c(a),\qquad
 K=\frac{2+2/A_f}{F_{\rm len}-1}.
\]
Solving the velocity-as-a-function-of-force relation for its multiplier gives
\begin{equation}
 f_V(\tilde v,a)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{1+w}{1-w/A_f},&-1\leq w\leq0,\\[3pt]
 \dfrac{1+K F_{\rm len}w}{1+K w},&w>0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:ch3:force-velocity}
\end{equation}
Thus $f_V(0,a)=1$, unloaded shortening occurs at $v^M=-c(a)v_0$,
and the lengthening branch approaches $F_{\rm len}$ asymptotically.
At half activation the zero-force speed is $-0.625v_0$, not $-v_0$.
The multiplier is continuous at zero velocity, but the right slope is twice
the left slope. An optimizer must not assume a continuously differentiable join.

This evaluator rejects shortening outside its declared branch rather than
returning negative active tension. The current OpenSim implementation adds
continuations and state restrictions \cite{OpenSimThelenPinned}; this listing
is not a replacement for that solver. An inverse equilibrium calculation also
requires $a f_L>0$ and an attainable force ratio below $F_{\rm len}$.
At zero activation the forward active force is zero, but dividing by activation
to recover velocity is undefined.

\begin{principle}{Force, Velocity, and Power}{fv-asymmetry}
 Shortening reduces active force below its isometric value; lengthening
 increases the multiplier above one, at the same activation and fiber length.
 This does not say that eccentric force always exceeds the full-activation,
 optimal-length reference force $F_0$.

 Isometric \emph{fiber} force includes active and passive contributions.
 Zero fiber velocity need not mean zero joint velocity; fixed joints need
 not imply isometric fibers while a tendon stretches. Active mechanical
 output is $-F_{CE}v^M$, with $F_{CE}$ the active fiber force,, positive during shortening and negative during
 lengthening. Its sign does not determine metabolic expenditure.
\end{principle}

\subsection{Activation Dynamics}

The neural command from the central nervous system does not instantly produce
muscle force. The activation dynamics models the excitation-contraction coupling:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{a}(t) = \frac{u(t) - a(t)}{\tau(u, a)},
  \label{eq:ch3:activation-dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $u(t) \in [0, 1]$ is the neural excitation, $a(t) \in [0, 1]$ is the
activation level, and $\tau$ is a time constant that differs for activation
and deactivation:
\begin{equation}
  \tau(u, a) = \begin{cases}
    \tau_{\text{act}}(0.5 + 1.5a) & \text{if } u > a \text{ (activation)}, \\
    \tau_{\text{deact}} / (0.5 + 1.5a) & \text{if } u \leq a \text{ (deactivation)}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ch3:tau-activation}
\end{equation}
The exercise uses $\tau_{\rm act}=15$~ms and $\tau_{\rm deact}=50$~ms,
as in the young-adult parameter set of \cite{Thelen2003}. These base
parameters are not observed force-rise or neural-conduction delays.
At $a=1$ the two effective branch times are $30$ and $25$~ms, respectively;
the base constants do not imply faster activation at every state.

The vector field is continuous at $u=a$ because both branches give zero there;
its derivatives generally differ across that surface. Continuous-time
trajectories preserve $[0,1]$: the derivative points inward at its boundaries.
Numerical integration still needs suitable tolerances and checked states.

With $a(0)=0$ and a step to $u=1$, separation of variables gives
\[
 t(a)=\tau_{\rm act}\{-1.5a-2\ln(1-a)\}.
\]
Activation reaches $0.95$ after approximately $68.50$~ms.
For deactivation from $a(0)=1$ with $u=0$,
\[
 t(a)=2\tau_{\rm deact}
 \ln\!\left(\frac{0.5+1.5a}{2a}\right),
\]
so $a=0.05$ occurs at approximately $174.92$~ms.
These are activation-state calculations; tendon force follows the coupled
fiber and series-elastic dynamics.

\section{Tendon Mechanics}

The tendon connects the muscle belly to bone. It is modeled as a nonlinear elastic
element with a resting (slack) length $\ell_T^s$:
\begin{equation}
  F_T(\varepsilon_T) = \begin{cases}
    0 & \varepsilon_T \leq 0, \\
    F_0 \cdot \frac{e^{k_T \varepsilon_T} - 1}{e^{k_T \varepsilon_{0T}} - 1} &
    \varepsilon_T > 0,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ch3:tendon-force}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_T$ is current tendon length and
$\varepsilon_T=(\ell_T-\ell_T^s)/\ell_T^s$,
$k_T>0$ and $\varepsilon_{0T}>0$. This single-exponential tendon is a
\emph{declared teaching choice}, not Thelen's toe-region/linear tendon or a
full Millard curve. It gives $F_T=F_0$ at the reference strain for \emph{every}
$k_T$; that reference point cannot identify the shape parameter.
A second measurement, such as tangent stiffness, is needed:
\[
 \frac{dF_T}{d\ell_T}
 =\frac{F_0 k_T e^{k_T\varepsilon_T}}
 {\ell_T^s(e^{k_T\varepsilon_{0T}}-1)}
 \quad(\varepsilon_T>0).
\]
For $F_T>0$, its inverse is
\[
 \varepsilon_T=\frac{1}{k_T}
 \ln\!\left[1+\frac{F_T}{F_0}
 (e^{k_T\varepsilon_{0T}}-1)\right].
\]
Force is continuous at slack engagement but stiffness changes there.

\begin{principle}{Tendons as Energy Storage Elements}{tendon-energy}
 The lossless teaching spring stores
 $U_T=\ell_T^s\int_0^{\varepsilon_T}F_T(e)\,de$ above slack.
 It returns that energy on unloading; it creates none. Biological
 viscoelasticity can dissipate energy and needs an additional constitutive law.

 Elastic recoil can separate the timing of fiber work from joint power.
 Alexander's account concerns locomotor mechanisms \cite{Alexander2002};
 it does not establish a metabolic saving or tendon-recoil strategy in a
 particular golf swing. Larger instantaneous joint power alone cannot
 identify its muscular, elastic, or external source.
\end{principle}

\section{Musculotendon Equilibrium}

Neglecting fiber and series-element inertia, the declared axial balance is
\begin{equation}
 F_T=(F_{CE}+F_{PE})\cos\alpha,\qquad
 F_{CE}=a f_L f_V F_0,\quad F_{PE}=f_{PE}F_0.
 \label{eq:ch3:mt-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
This is a model assumption, not a claim that real accelerating muscle has no
inertia. Any added damping force belongs in this balance and the energy account.

Let $\alpha$ be current pennation angle and $\alpha_0$ its value at optimal
fiber length. For constant fiber-bundle thickness $h=\ell_0^M\sin\alpha_0$
and total musculotendon path length $\ell^{MT}$,
\begin{equation}
 x=\ell^M\cos\alpha=\sqrt{(\ell^M)^2-h^2},\qquad
 \ell^{MT}=\ell_T+x,\qquad \ell^M>h.
 \label{eq:ch3:mt-length}
\end{equation}
An impossible length is not repaired by clipping $\cos\alpha$ to zero.
The limit $\ell^M=h$ is singular.

Given activation, fiber length and total path length, compute tendon length and
tension, then solve for fiber velocity. Define the required active-force multiplier
\[
 r=\frac{F_T/(F_0\cos\alpha)-f_{PE}}{a f_L}.
\]
The independent inverse of the declared interior law is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{v^M}{v_0}=
 \begin{cases}
 c(a)\dfrac{r-1}{1+r/A_f},&0\leq r\leq1,\\[3pt]
 c(a)\dfrac{(r-1)(F_{\rm len}-1)}
 {(2+2/A_f)(F_{\rm len}-r)},&1<r<F_{\rm len}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:ch3:inverse-velocity}
\end{equation}
Negative residual active-force demand, a ratio at or above the eccentric
asymptote, zero activation, or zero active-length multiplier lies outside this
inverse. Near the asymptote small force errors imply large velocity changes.
A solver must report failure or use an explicitly different regularized model,
not silently clamp the answer.

\subsection{Initialization and Integration}

Fiber length is a state. Activation and total path length alone do not determine
both fiber length and velocity. Specify $a(0)$ and $\ell^M(0)$, or an explicit
initialization rule. For an assumed initially isometric fiber, search for a root
of \eqref{eq:ch3:mt-equilibrium} with $v^M=0$ in an admissible interval.
The nonlinear curves may admit multiple roots or none; record the interval,
residual and selected root rather than assuming uniqueness.

At each evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Check states and geometry; derive $\ell_T=\ell^{MT}-x$.
 \item Evaluate tendon and passive-fiber forces.
 \item Check $a f_L\cos\alpha$ and the attainable ratio, then invert for $v^M$.
 \item Integrate activation and fiber length with error control, monitoring
 the force residual and domain boundaries.
\end{enumerate}
For this undamped inverse, a slack tendon with positive passive fiber tension
has no admissible nonnegative active-force balance. Damped or constrained
models can behave differently; their equations and state policy must be declared.

\section{From Fiber Power to Joint Mechanics}

With $v^{MT}=\dot\ell^{MT}$ and $v_T=\dot\ell_T$,
differentiating the geometry gives
\[
 v^{MT}=v_T+\frac{v^M}{\cos\alpha}.
\]
The force projection then gives
$F_T(v^{MT}-v_T)=(F_{CE}+F_{PE})v^M$.
Projecting force and also multiplying fiber velocity by $\cos\alpha$
would count the geometry incorrectly.

Let $\bm q$ be generalized coordinates and $\bm Q_M$ the generalized muscle
load. For a frictionless path $\ell^{MT}(\bm q)$, virtual work gives
\[
 \bm Q_M=-F_T\nabla_{\bm q}\ell^{MT},\qquad
 \bm Q_M^T\dot{\bm q}=-F_Tv^{MT}.
\]
For lossless tendon and passive fiber, let $U_{PE}$ be passive-fiber stored
energy, so $\dot U_{PE}=F_{PE}v^M$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \bm Q_M^T\dot{\bm q}
 =-F_{CE}v^M-\dot U_{PE}-\dot U_T.
 \label{eq:ch3:power-balance}
\end{equation}
Active output and changing stored energy jointly produce skeletal power.
A tendon can deliver power with nearly isometric fibers, or absorb power
while active fibers shorten. Neither observation uniquely identifies excitation,
chemical-energy use, or the cause of segment deceleration.
Frictional wraps would require an additional dissipation account.

\subsection{Coactivation, Equilibrium, and Stiffness}

Index muscles by $i$, with tendon force $F_i$ and path length $\ell_i^{MT}$.
For a joint coordinate $q$, define $\ell_i'=d\ell_i^{MT}/dq$ and
$\ell_i''=d^2\ell_i^{MT}/dq^2$. Write joint moment as $\tau_q$
to distinguish it from the activation time scale.
A declared quasistatic path-force law at fixed activation gives
\[
 \tau_q=-\sum_i F_i\ell_i',\qquad
 K_q=-\frac{d\tau_q}{dq}
 =\sum_i\left[k_i(\ell_i')^2+F_i\ell_i''\right],
 \quad k_i=\frac{dF_i}{d\ell_i^{MT}}.
\]
Material response and changing moment arms both contribute.
Define how fiber and tendon equilibrate when calculating $k_i$.
Frozen states, quasistatic relaxation and dynamic impedance are different
experiments; activation dynamics, frequency and feedback matter to the last.

Equal excitation of unequal antagonists does not generally balance their
moments. Symmetric force capacities and opposite matched moment arms can
preserve equilibrium under balanced coactivation, but that is a controlled
example, not a conclusion about biceps and triceps. The active descending
length branch and geometric term also prevent a universal positive-stiffness
guarantee.

\section{The Hill Model as a Coupled Dynamical System}

For the declared equilibrium model,
\begin{align}
 \dot a&=(u-a)/\tau(u,a),\label{eq:ch3:state-activation}\\
 \dot\ell^M&=v^M(a,\ell^M,\ell^{MT}),\label{eq:ch3:state-fiber}
\end{align}
using \eqref{eq:ch3:inverse-velocity} only on its admissible domain.
Pennation, tendon geometry, constitutive ranges and invertibility restrict
the state space; it is not the unrestricted product $[0,1]\times\Reals_{>0}$.

With prescribed joint motion, path length is an input signal. In forward
dynamics it is $\ell^{MT}(\bm q)$ inside a feedback coupling: excitation
changes muscle state, tension changes skeletal loads, and motion changes
muscle length. Zero net joint moment therefore does not imply inactive
muscles, and identical joint trajectories can coexist with different
coactivation and stored-energy histories.

\begin{principle}{Model Choice Follows the Question}{muscle-control}
 Distinguish prescribed-fiber evaluation, compliant equilibrium simulation,
 damped equilibrium simulation, and rigid-tendon approximation.
 Each answers a different mechanical question.

 Millard and colleagues compared computational behavior and force fits in
 particular preparations, including rat and cat soleus \cite{millard2013flexing}.
 Those results do not rank every model for every human task.
 Validation should name the muscle, population, loading protocol, predicted
 quantity, parameter estimation and independent test data.
 A fit to swing kinematics cannot alone validate individual muscle force.
\end{principle}

 have different stiffness. These choices do not calibrate an Achilles tendon.

 \item \textbf{Force Surface.} Plot active, passive and total \emph{fiber}
 force over $0.5\leq\tilde\ell\leq1.6$ and $0\leq a\leq1$, at zero fiber
 velocity and zero pennation. Locate bounded-domain maxima.
 Explain why extrapolating the passive exponential to unbounded length gives
 no finite global total-force maximum.

 \item \textbf{Equilibrium Simulation.} Implement the checked procedure for a
 prescribed path and excitation. Declare initial states, constitutive laws,
 root selection and domain policy. Verify force residual and power balance;
 report step-size convergence. Demonstrate a clear failure for an inadmissible
 state rather than a clipped trajectory.

 \item \textbf{Antagonists and Geometry.} Begin with matched antagonists,
 opposite constant moment arms and a balanced equilibrium. Calculate torque
 and tangent stiffness as common activation changes. Then change one force
 capacity and introduce curved paths. Identify the symmetry assumptions
 needed to preserve equilibrium; distinguish tangent stiffness from impedance.

 \item \textbf{Model Comparison.} Compare specified Thelen and Millard
 implementations using the same data and initialization. Report force error,
 solver cost and domain failures. Distinguish numerical regularization from
 physiological validation and identify evidence needed for a golf-swing claim.
\end{enumerate}
