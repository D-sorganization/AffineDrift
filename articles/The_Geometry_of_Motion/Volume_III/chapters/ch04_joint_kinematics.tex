\section{What an Ideal Joint Represents}

A revolute joint admits one relative rotation about a fixed line. A spherical
joint admits three relative rotations about a fixed center. These are declared
model reductions, not literal descriptions of every displacement of articular
surfaces. Their usefulness depends on the motion, loading and output being studied.
A model sufficient for clubhead trajectory can still be inadequate for estimating
joint contact or ligament loads. Conversely, adding coordinates without observations
or constitutive information need not improve an estimate.

Three questions must remain distinct: what relative motion was observed, what
motions the model permits, and what forces produced that motion. Joint geometry
links these questions through moment arms, constraints and contact conditions, but
kinematics alone does not answer the force question. Chapters~5--7 develop those
links to multibody dynamics, inverse inference and measurement uncertainty.

\section{The Instantaneous Screw Axis}

Let $T(t)=(R(t),\bm p(t))$ describe one rigid segment relative to a reference
segment. All following derivatives are relative to that reference frame, not
absolute laboratory velocities unless the reference is stationary there. A
material point with fixed moving-segment coordinates $\bm X$ has
$\bm x=R\bm X+\bm p$. Define $[\bm\omega]_\times=\dot R R^T$ and
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\bm x}=\bm v_s+\bm\omega\times\bm x,
 \qquad \bm v_s=\dot{\bm p}-\bm\omega\times\bm p.
 \label{eq:ch4:spatial-velocity}
\end{equation}
Thus $\bm v_s$ is the translational component of the spatial twist in the selected
reference frame. It is the velocity field evaluated at that frame's origin,
which need not contain an actual material point. It is generally not
$\dot{\bm p}$, the velocity of the moving frame origin. A measured point velocity
$\bm u_P$ at $\bm r_P$ gives $\bm v_s=\bm u_P-\bm\omega\times\bm r_P$.
These definitions agree with $[V_s]=\dot T T^{-1}$; a body twist
$[V_b]=T^{-1}\dot T$ uses a different frame representation
\cite{ModernRoboticsTwistsJoint}.

\begin{definition}{Finite Instantaneous Screw Axis}{isa-bio}
For $\norm{\bm\omega}>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\bm s}=\frac{\bm\omega}{\norm{\bm\omega}},\qquad
 h=\frac{\bm\omega\cdot\bm v_s}{\norm{\bm\omega}^2},\qquad
 \bm q=\frac{\bm\omega\times\bm v_s}{\norm{\bm\omega}^2}.
\end{equation}
The axis is the entire line $\bm q+\lambda\hat{\bm s}$, for real $\lambda$.
The chosen $\bm q$ is the point on that line nearest the reference origin:
$\bm q\cdot\bm\omega=0$. At every point of the line, the velocity field equals
$h\bm\omega$. The pitch $h$ is translation per angular displacement, in metres
per radian when angular speed is expressed in radians per second.
\end{definition}

Indeed, substituting the formula for $\bm q$ into
$\bm v_s+\bm\omega\times\bm q$ leaves exactly the component of $\bm v_s$
parallel to $\bm\omega$. The axis formula applies to nonzero pitch as well as
pure rotation. When $\bm\omega=0$ and $\bm v_s\ne0$, the motion is a pure
translation with no unique finite screw line; its direction is still defined.
When both vanish, no motion axis is identified. Near zero angular speed,
axis location is sensitive to velocity errors. A numerical threshold prevents a
singular calculation but does not establish a measurement's signal-to-noise ratio.
A finite-displacement helical axis inferred between two poses is also not generally
the instantaneous axis at either endpoint.

\subsection{A Reproducible Frame Example}

Take $\bm\omega=(0,0,2)$ rad/s, $\bm q=(0.03,-0.02,0)$ m and
$h=0.005$ m/rad. Then $\bm v_s=(-0.04,-0.06,0.01)$ m/s. At
$\bm p=(0.10,0.20,-0.10)$ m, the moving-origin velocity is
$\dot{\bm p}=(-0.44,0.14,0.01)$ m/s. Recovering $\bm v_s$ first gives the
correct axis. Passing $\dot{\bm p}$ directly instead gives
$(-0.07,-0.22,0)$ m: a different line. This is a reference-point mistake,
not evidence of anatomical axis migration.

The listing accepts finite three-vectors in one frame and rejects motions for
which it does not report a finite axis. The default angular threshold is a
numerical example in rad/s; select a justified threshold from the actual
measurement uncertainty before interpreting experimental axes.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Finite Screw Axis From a Declared Spatial Twist}]
import numpy as np


def compute_isa(
    omega: np.ndarray,
    v: np.ndarray,
    angular_threshold: float = 1e-8,
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, float]:
    """Return nearest axis point, direction and pitch.

    omega: rad/s; v: spatial twist component in m/s.
    Both are finite (3,) vectors in the same frame.
    The threshold is positive, finite and in rad/s.
    Translation, rest and subthreshold rotation raise.
    """
    omega = np.asarray(omega, dtype=float)
    v = np.asarray(v, dtype=float)
    if omega.shape != (3,) or v.shape != (3,):
        raise ValueError("Expected two three-vectors")
    if not np.isfinite(omega).all() or not np.isfinite(v).all():
        raise ValueError("Twist must be finite")
    if not np.isfinite(angular_threshold) or angular_threshold <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Invalid angular threshold")
    speed = np.linalg.norm(omega)
    if not np.isfinite(speed) or speed <= angular_threshold:
        raise ValueError("No resolved finite screw axis")
    direction = omega / speed
    point = np.cross(direction, v) / speed
    pitch = float(np.dot(direction, v) / speed)
    if not np.isfinite(point).all() or not np.isfinite(pitch):
        raise ValueError("Axis calculation exceeds numeric range")
    return point, direction, pitch
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Migration Must Be Derived From Motion}

For a transparent planar example, prescribe $R=R_z(\theta)$ and
$\bm p=(a\theta,0,0)$, where $a$ is a constant length and $\dot\theta\ne0$.
Then $\bm\omega=(0,0,\dot\theta)$ and
$\bm v_s=(a\dot\theta,-a\theta\dot\theta,0)$, so
\begin{equation}
 \bm q=(a\theta,a,0),\qquad h=0.
\end{equation}
The axis migrates even though the motion has just one independent coordinate.
The moving frame origin follows a different path, $(a\theta,0,0)$. This example
is derived from a specified rigid transform; it is not a knee model and $a$ is
not a measured rolling-to-sliding ratio. A curve drawn directly as ``ISA position''
without its velocity field cannot establish a joint's rolling or sliding mechanics.

\section{Biological Geometry and Model Mobility}

\subsection{The Knee}

Descriptions of tibiofemoral motion must identify which segment moves relative to
which, the anatomical coordinate convention, flexion interval and loading protocol.
Surface-contact location, material-point velocity and instantaneous screw-axis
location are different quantities. In a contact model, rolling and sliding are
assessed using the relative tangential velocities of contacting material points;
the migration of a contact location alone does not separate them.

A quasistatic knee model can solve relative pose from prescribed loading,
articular geometry, ligament paths and constraints. Blankevoort and Huiskes's
model explicitly made such assumptions and included ligament wrapping
\cite{Blankevoort1991}. Its assumptions and specimen geometry do not supply a
universal axis path or a universal axial rotation angle during a golf swing.
A changing ISA is compatible with several possible joint models and does not,
by itself, identify a ligament's force or stiffness. For this reason, this chapter
does not prescribe a generic numerical screw-home curve as anatomical evidence.

\subsection{The Shoulder Complex}

The glenohumeral, acromioclavicular and sternoclavicular articulations participate
in shoulder motion; scapulothoracic motion describes the scapula relative to the
thorax. A model may approximate some articulations with spherical joints, but
then their admitted rotations, suppressed translations and connections must be
explicit. A translation magnitude is not a count of degrees of freedom.

For a free rigid scapula with six local coordinates, one maintained frictionless
point-on-surface equation $g(q)=0$ removes one freedom if its Jacobian has rank
one, leaving five locally. Two independent such equations leave four. Requiring
a particular surface normal or orientation adds different conditions. These are
model choices, not consequences of the phrase ``surface contact.'' A unilateral
contact can also open; the equality describes only its maintained-contact mode.
The clavicle and the rest of the shoulder chain can add further constraints.

Seth and colleagues instead parameterized scapulothoracic motion using four
coordinates: two for position on an ellipsoidal surface, upward rotation and
winging. Their formulation permits the medial border to lift away from the
surface \cite{Seth2016ScapulaJoint}. This is a concrete counterexample to the claim
that scapulothoracic motion necessarily consists of two translations with no
lift-off. It remains a particular model, not a universal anatomical mobility count.

\section{Ligament Force, Length and Stiffness}

For an elastic tensile line element, let $\ell_0>0$ be its zero-load length and
$\varepsilon=(\ell-\ell_0)/\ell_0$. A continuous quadratic-to-linear law is
\begin{equation}
 F(\varepsilon)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\varepsilon\le0,\\
 k\varepsilon^2/(4\varepsilon_1),&0<\varepsilon\le2\varepsilon_1,\\
 k(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_1),&\varepsilon>2\varepsilon_1,
 \end{cases}
 \quad k>0,\quad\varepsilon_1>0.
 \label{eq:ch4:ligament-force}
\end{equation}
This line-element approximation appears in the knee-model formulation of
Blankevoort and Huiskes \cite{Blankevoort1991}. Here $k$ has units of newtons,
because strain is dimensionless. Both branches at $2\varepsilon_1$ give
$F=k\varepsilon_1$ and $dF/d\varepsilon=k$; at zero both force and slope vanish.
Thus the law is $C^1$, although its second derivative changes at the joins.
Independent coefficients in these two branches would generally destroy continuity.

The length tangent is $dF/d\ell=(dF/d\varepsilon)/\ell_0$, in N/m.
For the manufactured values $k=1000$ N, $\varepsilon_1=0.03$ and
$\ell_0=0.10$ m, strains $0.03$, $0.06$ and $0.09$ give forces
$7.5$, $30$ and $60$ N and length tangents $5000$, $10000$ and $10000$ N/m.
The number $0.03$ is an example parameter, not a universal tissue transition.
An anatomical reference pose may already be prestrained; its path length is not
necessarily $\ell_0$.

Path geometry also matters. With $r=-d\ell/dq$ for one coordinate, the generalized
ligament load is $Q=rF$. Its restoring tangent is
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{dQ}{dq}=r^2\frac{dF}{d\ell}-F\frac{dr}{dq}.
\end{equation}
A joint stiffness therefore cannot be obtained from the material force slope
alone. Wrapping changes path length and its derivatives. This connects directly
to Chapter~5's geometric stiffness term. The line-element law omits rate dependence,
hysteresis, damage and distributed stress; it cannot independently predict failure.

\section{A Local Model of Spinal Compliance}

Representing adjacent vertebrae as rigid bodies permits six relative motion
components; a constitutive law couples their resisting loads. This is distinct
from granting six unresisted motions. An isolated disc model and a complete
functional spinal unit, including other structures, require different parameters.
The following is a declared local elastic abstraction, not a calibrated spine model.

About a specified reference pose and preload, choose small translational and
rotational increments $\delta=(\bm u,\bm\phi)$ in one frame and at a stated
reduction point. Order the conjugate restoring wrench increment as
$\Delta W=(\Delta\bm f,\Delta\bm m)$ so that incremental power is
$\Delta W^T\dot\delta$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \Delta W=-K\delta,\qquad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}K_{uu}&K_{u\phi}\\K_{\phi u}&K_{\phi\phi}\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:ch4:disc-stiffness}
\end{equation}
The minus sign describes the resisting load on the displaced segment; the
external load required to hold it has the opposite sign. The block units are
N/m, N/rad, N\,m/m and N\,m/rad, respectively. For a conservative local quadratic
energy $U=\tfrac12\delta^TK\delta$, $K$ is symmetric in these conjugate
coordinates; positive definiteness describes a strictly stable elastic reference
for this abstraction. This need not hold for an arbitrary fitted, preloaded,
nonconservative or incompletely specified model. Finite rotation increments,
frame changes and shifted moment reference points require consistent transformations.

The same principles govern a golf model: select enough joint motion to address
the question, declare the elastic and contact assumptions, and carry the same
frames into force and power calculations. Reproducing a swing trajectory alone
does not identify the tissue parameters that support it.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Frame Recovery.} Reproduce the numerical screw example. Verify
 the velocity at three points along its axis. Translate the reference origin by
 $\bm c=(0.02,0.01,0)$ m without rotating axes: show
 $\bm v_s'=\bm v_s+\bm\omega\times\bm c$. Compare the same geometric axis
 line in both frames, accounting for the new nearest-point representative.
 \item \textbf{Motion and Axis Paths.} Set $a=0.03$ m in the manufactured planar
 transform. Plot the origin and ISA paths for $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$. Verify the
 computed axis using numerical derivatives of $T$ at interior points. Explain why
 an axis path is neither a contact path nor evidence of a biological mechanism.
 \item \textbf{Constraint Rank.} Write the scalar equation for a body-fixed point
 lying on a sphere of radius $b$. Linearize it with respect to the body's six
 local pose increments. State when its rank is one and why two coincident copies
 of the equation do not remove two freedoms.
 \item \textbf{Ligament Continuity.} Derive force and strain tangent at both joins;
 reproduce the three numerical force/length-tangent pairs. For a varying moment
 arm, derive the additional geometric stiffness term and explain its sign.
 \item \textbf{Local Elastic Energy.} With translations in metres and rotations in
 radians, take the following values in the corresponding block units:
 \[
 \begin{gathered}
 K=\operatorname{diag}(1000,1000,2000,10,10,20),\\
 \delta=(0,0,0.001,0,0,0.01).
 \end{gathered}
 \]
 Compute the restoring wrench and
 $U$; check that their mechanical powers obey $W^T\dot\delta=-\dot U$.
\end{enumerate}
