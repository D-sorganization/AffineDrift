# Bibliographic Analysis: Affine Control Interpretation of the Golf Swing — Part 3

## A) Concept Map

- **Theoretical Foundations**

  - **Drift Invariance**: The property where passive dynamics ($f(x)$) are independent of control inputs ($u$).
  - **Affine Control Systems**: Systems linear in control input ($\dot{x} = f(x) + g(x)u$).
  - **Geometric Control**: Analyzing dynamics via vector fields on manifolds.

- **Force Taxonomy (Causal Decomposition)**

  - **Configuration Drift**: Static loads (gravity, stiffness) independent of motion.
  - **Velocity Drift**: Dynamic loads (Coriolis, centrifugal, damping) dependent on motion.
  - **Input Forces**: Active forces generated by torques.
  - **Mixed Interaction**: Nonlinear coupling effects between drift and input.

- **Counterfactual Analysis**

  - **Zero Velocity Counterfactual (ZVCF)**: Isolating configuration-dependent forces.
  - **Zero Torque Counterfactual (ZTCF)**: Isolating total passive drift.

- **Biomechanics & Constraints**
  - **Induced Acceleration Analysis**: (Related standard method) Attributing motion to specific forces.
  - **Input Constraints**: State-dependent limits on available torque ($\mathcal{U}(x)$).
  - **Passive vs. Active**: Fundamental separation in motor control.

## C) Reading Paths

### Path 1: Fast Ramp (The Core Physics)

_Target: Understand why "drift" matters in swinging mechanics._

1.  **Lynch & Park (2017)** - _Modern Robotics_ (Ch 8: Dynamics). A clean modern intro to the equations we use.
2.  **McGeer (1990)** - _Passive dynamic walking_. The classic proof that "dumb" physics can look like "smart" control.
3.  **Sprigings & Neal (2000)** - _Importance of wrist torque_. Shows the interplay of active torque vs passive release in golf.
4.  **Zajac & Gordon (1989)** - _Determining muscle's force..._. The biomechanics standard for figuring out "what force caused what motion".
5.  **Sharp (2009)** - _Physical limitations..._. Real-world constraints on the math.

### Path 2: Deep Technical (Rigorous Decomposition)

_Target: The advanced math of affine systems and geometric control._

1.  **Bullo & Lewis (2004)** - _Geometric Control of Mechanical Systems_. The definitive reference for the $\dot{x} = f(x) + g(x)u$ structure on manifolds.
2.  **Khalil (2002)** - _Nonlinear Systems_. Standard tools for analyzing stability and properties of these systems.
3.  **Bloch (2003)** - _Nonholonomic Mechanics and Control_. Advanced mechanics if we consider more complex constraints.
4.  **Murray, Li, Sastry (1994)** - _Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation_. Essential for the geometric view of rigid body motion.
5.  **Shabana (2020)** - _Dynamics of Multibody Systems_. Crucial for adding the flexible shaft (modal analysis) to the rigid golfer.
6.  **Tedrake (2023)** - _Underactuated Robotics_. Modern view on exploiting passive dynamics (drift).
7.  **Todorov & Jordan (2002)** - _Optimal feedback control..._. Moves from "mechanics" to "brain" – how the CNS exploits the drift we calculated.
8.  **Bernstein (1967)** - _Coordination and regulation..._. The historical root of the "degrees of freedom problem" that drift invariance helps solve.

### Path 3: Implementation (Simulation & Optimization)

_Target: Tools to calculate the taxonomy._

1.  **CasADi (Andersson et al.)** - The best tool for Optimal Control problems (finding $u(t)$).
2.  **Pinocchio (Carpentier et al.)** - Efficient rigid body dynamics library (C++/Python).
3.  **Drake (Tedrake et al.)** - Toolbox for dynamics and control. Excellent for trajectory optimization.
4.  **SciPy (Virtanen et al.)** - Standard stack for integrating the ODEs.
5.  **OpenSim (Delp et al.)** - For comparing our minimal model to full musculoskeletal simulations.
