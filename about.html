About the researcher behind AffineDrift

Dieter Olson

Chemical Engineer & Golf Researcher

I am a chemical engineer with a deep passion for golf research, particularly focused on the interpretation of dynamics of the golf swing. My work bridges the gap between engineering principles and biomechanical analysis, applying rigorous mathematical frameworks to understand one of sport's most complex movements.

Through AffineDrift, I explore the mathematical structure underlying the golf swing by modeling it as an affine controllable system. This approach allows for a principled decomposition of forces into drift (passive) and input (active) components, providing new insights into how the golf swing actually works.

My research interests include:

  • Control-affine systems and nonlinear control theory
  • Biomechanical modeling of human movement
  • Force decomposition and causal analysis in multibody systems
  • Mathematical modeling of sports dynamics

This website serves as a repository for my research, insights, and resources at the intersection of mathematics, control theory, and golf biomechanics. I welcome collaboration, discussion, and feedback from fellow researchers, golfers, and enthusiasts interested in the mathematics of motion.
