## Scientific Decisions

1. **Declare the Inverse Problem and Rank Limits.** Separate global/local structural identifiability, generic versus exceptional parameter values, and practical precision under an explicit observation map, inputs, domain and nuisance variables. Six observations cannot generically identify 12 free continuous parameters, but a regression rank does not prove the simulator's nonlinear structural properties. Full column rank is locally sufficient, not universally necessary; the cubic and square examples expose different failure modes. A bounded feasible family need not be unique.
2. **Describe the Provider Estimator Accurately.** The inspected implementation computes standardized least-squares coefficients over 24 cases, yielding a 6-by-12 coefficient matrix. It is not a local Jacobian or a noise-based estimation analysis. Saved-array reconstruction reproduces six singular values and threshold-sensitive ranks; leave-one-out ranking instability is retained. PRCC is distinguished from variance decomposition and causal attribution. Polynomial examples verify that fitted slopes and local derivatives differ.
3. **State Which Hand-Load Map Has a Null Mode.** Distinguish planar point forces (rank3/nullity1), separated spatial point forces (5/1), coincident spatial point forces (3/3), and two full spatial wrenches (6/6). This is an unconstrained instantaneous map. Contact feasibility, kinematic constraints and parameter identifiability over time are different objects; independent constitutive information may restrict allocations.
4. **Replace Sensor Sufficiency With a Worked Measurement Model.** The former stiffness/timing story asserted discrimination without a demonstrated forward sensor model. A manufactured sum/difference example now proves what one extra independent channel can establish and derives estimator covariance. Bilateral sensors can constrain measured resultants without identifying intra-hand pressure, muscles or neural strategy. Gaussianity is unnecessary for the covariance propagation itself.
5. **Expose Nuisance Compensation and Inferential Assumptions.** Show the difference between a fixed-parameter slice and a reoptimized profile. Independent scalar minimization reproduces the profile and optimal nuisance path. Distinguish frequentist coverage, Bayesian probability, prior/penalty contributions and numerical search bounds. The Raue paper's unbounded-interval definition is explicitly distinguished from an application-specific precision criterion.
6. **Bound Ensemble Sign and Mechanical-Control Claims.** An engineering-ensemble fraction is not automatically a population probability, and 50% is not a universal mechanical null. Robust sign does not identify a mechanism. Zero separation removes an equal-and-opposite force-pair moment but does not eliminate independent couples; reversal controls require compatible conventions and stated interventions.
7. **Separate Human Recovery, Synthetic Holdout and Prediction.** Remove the unsupported McPhee attribution about parameter recovery. The chapter establishes no new participant recovery experiment. Freeze preprocessing, tuning and measurement selection before evaluation; split by participant for new-person claims. Parameter ambiguity and decision-relevant prediction uncertainty can differ. Preserve the instructional experiment-design exercise without presenting it as completed research.
8. **Correct Figure Geometry and Evidence Labels.** Fix the clipped observation box/text, match margins, and account for rounded-box padding. Only `make_sensitivity` changes in the shared generator; other AST nodes are identical. Both diagrams are explicitly conceptual; the retained many-paths curves do not prove indistinguishability of complete motion histories. Source links are immutable provider links.
