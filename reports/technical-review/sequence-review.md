## Scientific Decisions

1. **Rigid Velocity Convention.** The hand-plus-rotation identity requires absolute club angular velocity, two material points on a rigid club and a common inertial frame. A hand point additionally requires compatible no-slip contact. Perpendicular axis radius, not arbitrary length, belongs in the scalar speed relation. A moving-frame deformation term makes the rigid assumption visible. Two one-metre links at angles (0, pi/2), rates (2, 3) yield (-5, 2) m/s by independent central differences; substituting relative rate alone yields the wrong (-3, 2). The manufactured fixture verifies kinematics, not golf dynamics.

2. **Signed Speed and Energy.** A joint Jacobian contribution includes all affected downstream links and differs from a hand-translation allocation. Signed projections along nonzero endpoint velocity sum to speed and may be negative. Rates (2, -5) yield contributions (-2, 2) and (5, 0), total (3, 2), projections -2/sqrt(13) and 15/sqrt(13). For a unit point mass, actual energy 6.5 J differs from separate squares 16.5 J by the -10 J cross term. This does not allocate human segment energy or actuator work. Partition choice matters; a common orthogonal rotation preserves dot products.

3. **Vector Peak Condition.** A nonzero smooth angular-speed norm has derivative omega dot alpha divided by its norm. Its interior maximum need not have zero vector angular acceleration. The dimensionless (1-t^2)(cos t, sin t, 0) example has norm maximum at t=0 and nonzero alpha=(0,1,0). Differentiability, nonzero norm, endpoint/plateau rules and the insufficiency of a stationary derivative are explicit.

4. **Sequence Methodology.** Absolute vector norms, relative joint rates and anatomical components are distinct observables. Rotating the axes of one physical vector leaves its norm invariant; changing the observable or preprocessing can change a reported peak. Peak ordering does not identify transfer time. Compatible wrench-point power, relative joint power and segment energy changes must have declared boundaries. Integrate powers and compare the resulting work with energy changes; do not integrate stored energy as if it were power. Uncertain near ties and filters are part of the reported event, not grounds to choose the prettiest ordering.

5. **Empirical Scope.** Tinmark 2010 reports 45 golfers and pelvis/upper-torso/hand angular-speed magnitudes, not a measured club energy ledger or a torque intervention. The 47-person earlier conference account is not substituted for that journal cohort. Putnam 1991 concerns four subjects and lower limbs in kicking/walking/running. Putnam 1993 is a broader explanatory review. Milburn's abstract presents a double-pendulum analysis; it is not counted as independent universal-human causal proof. The revision does not claim these papers contain no other measurements: full original papers were not accessible/read for this review.

6. **Finite Candidate Tradeoffs.** Independently rebuilt all five held-out objectives from the pinned saved 8x6x6 outputs, exactly matching the saved objective matrix within 1e-14 tolerance. All eight are nondominated in that finite set. Speed alone uniquely selects early_restrain at q10=4.705878716075947 m/s. This is fixed-time delivery at 0.24 s in a planar moving-base/two-hand/flexible-club surrogate, not ball impact or calibrated full-swing human speed. Finite nondominance cannot establish a continuous frontier, compulsory tradeoffs against all possible controls, or different human peak sequences. No dynamics was rerun.

7. **Intervention and Identifiability.** Removing a finite actuator torque at a copied state with unchanged constraints preserves immediate endpoint velocity, while accelerations and subsequent trajectories can differ. Not every acceleration component is guaranteed to change. Impulses, contact changes and prescribed motion require separate handling. Bilateral wrenches constrain external allocations but do not uniquely identify muscle forces or neural commands; EMG does not remove this distinction. Repeatability, kinetic predictions and perturbations make sequence a bounded empirical constraint rather than a coaching law.

Removed the shading, normalized all chosen peaks to their own maxima, added distinct line styles and declared schematic axes/title/caption/alt text. It encodes chosen timing and overlap only, not speed ratios, Jacobian contributions or transfer.
