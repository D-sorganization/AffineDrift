## Scientific Decisions

1. **Complete State and Input:** Copy every modeled state and the time/phase convention for an input-only intervention. Distinguish delivered torque, upstream command, retained feedback policy and replayed command trace. Activation, transmission, flexible storage and controller memory can preserve mechanical output after command removal. Changing an internal state is a different intervention that needs compatibility and stored-energy accounting. Hold a contact mode fixed only if it remains admissible; otherwise resolve and declare the mode change. An unchanged state-dependent compliant law gives the same initial contact force and interface power despite a driver change.

2. **Finite Effect and Decomposition:** Define baseline-minus-altered differences explicitly. Fixed-state affine input effects are exact under the stated model/mode, but nonlinear command removals need not equal derivatives or add as separate contributions. The manufactured rotor has inertia 2 kg m^2 and torque 3 Nm times (u1+u2)^2 for dimensionless commands. At u1=u2=1, acceleration is 6 rad/s^2; each separate removal reduces it by 4.5, while combined removal reduces it by 6 rather than 9. A tangent prediction for full removal gives 12 rather than 6. These are invented identities, not muscle calibration. Avoid double-counting solved constraint effects and percentages of near-zero totals.

3. **Cuts, Events and Horizons:** Separate instantaneous attribution, evolving copied-state futures, changed preparation, mechanism ablation and compensated optimization. Name fixed-clock versus each-branch event outcomes, retain non-reaching branches, and distinguish geometric delivery from ball collision. Finite torque steps are not impulses; a one-sample feature requires investigation rather than automatic rejection. State the observable, input sampling and event convention behind refinement. Matching two steps does not establish general convergence order. State triggers must use information available at the trigger time.

4. **Saved Planar Evidence:** Recompute the 96 baseline grid and 12 whole-model variant grid from the archived JSON, not new dynamics. All ten reported timestep-sensitivity maxima reproduce exactly. At 1 ms, the work extrema are -30.87602844327142 and +66.16902040271255 J, at cuts 0.32/0.22 s over 0.12 s; corresponding speed differences are +3.418004486054784/+14.196490598979008 m/s. Within the first comparison, less wrist-force work coexists with greater head speed. Other work channels and task kinematics matter. Whole-model gravity/damping variants have different pre-cut histories and are not matched-state partitions. Report saved numerical evidence with the detected dependency-version gap, not as reproduced dynamics at the selected revision.

5. **Geometry and Coordinates:** Derive the moment about O as (c-O) cross (F1+F2) plus (d/2) cross (F1-F2). Uniformly bounded forces make the differential term vanish with separation; offset-resultant moments and direct grip couples may remain. The manufactured example gives 1+16=17 Nm; coincident contacts retain 16 Nm and adding a direct 2 Nm yields 18 Nm. Reversing separation with midpoint fixed differs from reversing all arms about the reference. A re-solved trajectory may change forces. Under a proper coordinate rotation, moment components can change sign while a consistently transformed physical projection and rotational power remain invariant. Common observables must actually exist in both model tiers.

6. **Spatial Driver Evidence:** The saved carriage study removes the whole grounded driver at 0.180 s; compliant contact moment remains endogenous. Its reported minimum near -0.409 Nm and 37.5 ms negative run are archived reduced-model results. The duration counts contiguous negative samples times timestep, not exact zero-crossing localization. Its geometry controls move both points to the club reference or negate both arms there while retaining achieved forces, without a new trajectory. MuJoCo/Pinocchio vary rigid-body operators while sharing contact and update code; this does not independently validate those shared components, anatomy or human strategy. Prior external work remains part of the inherited state history.

7. **Ablation and Compensation:** Persistence after a valid removal refutes necessity in that tested setting; disappearance supports dependence in that comparison, not universal necessity. Changed state spaces or task feasibility limit the inference. Zero dead zone does not remove activation delay; equal shaft stiffness does not remove flexibility. Rigidification needs a state mapping and energy treatment. A feasible compensated policy establishes a model alternative within declared bounds; failed optimization does not prove physical impossibility. Compare hidden hand allocation, internal loads, work routes and sensitivity as well as visible task outcome.

8. **Human Evidence and Publication:** Grip spacing, equipment and instruction can alter force strategy and feedback, so geometry alone does not predict a proportional human response. Distinguish synthetic parameter holdouts from participants. The registered protocol and saved synthetic readiness contain no new human measurements. Revise the branch manifest and two-input-path figure/caption to match the actual comparison. Only make_counterfactual_fork changes in the shared generator. Preserve accessible links between state, causality, power, feasibility and evidence without claiming a whole-book or biological validation.
