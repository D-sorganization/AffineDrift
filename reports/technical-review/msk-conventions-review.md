# Musculoskeletal Conventions Review

Issue #4850 belongs to epic #4009 and the original-source corpus #4021. Scope is the complete Geometry of Motion Volume III Chapter 2, its printed code and exercises, the regenerated Volume III PDF, and two bibliography additions. This checkpoint is provisional pending full regression and source binding. It does not certify all of Volume III, anatomical measurements, clinical signs for either limb, or a deployed web page.

## Scientific Findings and Decisions

1. **Reference and Calibration Frames.** Anatomical position supplies directional terminology, not a universal model zero. A fixed laboratory frame, a marker-cluster technical frame and a calibrated anatomical frame are distinct. The former attributed ISB femoral origin and plane-normal axis were incorrect: Wu et al. (2002), Section 4.4, places the origin at the hip center, y from the epicondyle midpoint toward it, z within the landmark plane and perpendicular to y pointing right, and x along y cross z. Moving the origin to the knee requires explicit transport. Functional hip-center estimation depends on adequate relative motion; anthropometric regression estimates rather than directly observes the center.
2. **Relative Transform Direction.** The chapter now states the active segment-to-global point map, the distal-to-proximal relative transform and its translation. A common laboratory-frame change cancels; anatomical recalibration changes the coordinates and requires consistent inertial transport. Right is not lateral on both limbs.
3. **Angle Extraction and Clinical Meaning.** For the declared active intrinsic Rz(alpha) Rx(beta) Ry(gamma), the middle sine is R[2,1]. The original code returned (60,-5,10) for (60,5,10) degrees. The corrected mathematical ZXY listing recovers mixed angles, checks real finite proper rotations and rejects singular/near-singular configurations. It deliberately does not claim clinical Grood–Suntay signs: directed anatomical axes, side, calibration, neutral offsets and branches require a separate mapping. Identity alone would not expose the original defect. The 1e-8 singularity guard is illustrative numerical conditioning, not measurement accuracy.
4. **Rotation and Screw Representations.** Matrix orthonormality plus positive determinant, quaternion double cover, logarithm/angle branches and clinical-chart singularities have different meanings. Finite displacement and instantaneous velocity screws are distinguished. Pure translation and zero motion have separate normalization/axis limits. Rotation alone does not define a full rigid displacement; the exercise now supplies translation. A screw description neither imposes a fixed biological hinge nor identifies a neural control law.
5. **Body-Segment Parameter Evidence.** Population estimates are not individual measurements. Removed unsupported universal gold-standard language and replaced the unverified demographic catalog with narrowly supported de Leva, Pavol and Jensen descriptions. Changing segment endpoints changes a COM fraction's meaning; changing axes rotates inertia; changing origins also invokes parallel-axis transport. These operations do not repair an unsuitable population or rigid-segment assumption. Removed Dempster/Zatsiorsky sample counts are not declared false; their original studies were not available for sufficient direct verification.
6. **Spatial Inertia Signs and Origin.** For angular-first body motion V=(omega,v_O), c directed from O to COM, and C=[c]cross, v_C=v_O-C omega. Expanding direct COM kinetic energy gives blocks (I_C+m C C^T, m C; m C^T, m I). The printed cross blocks had the opposite signs. Its top-left I_i was already about the origin; adding parallel-axis transport twice would introduce a second error. The manufactured regression case gives 1.17 J under the old print versus direct 1.01 J. Independent point-mass momentum and shifted-origin energy checks validate the corrected blocks. Symmetry or positive definiteness alone cannot detect the reversed COM sign. Physical central principal moments also satisfy triangle inequalities; degenerate bodies may be semidefinite.
7. **Connection to Generalized Dynamics.** With consistent body Jacobians, segment energy sums to M=sum(J^T G J) and virtual power gives generalized loads J^T F. Euler-angle rates are not Cartesian angular velocity. Different reported angles or moments in a golf swing may reflect calibration, model parameters or coordinate choices; a coordinate correction is not evidence of a different muscle or neural strategy.
8. **Exercises, Attribution and Presentation.** Replaced an unsupported attributed epigraph with the chapter's model/measurement argument. Exercises now require mixed-angle recovery, frame invariance, the middle-angle singular set, translation for SE(3), and explicit inertia input conventions. The 4 kg, 0.20/0.22 m example is manufactured: balancing moments are 7.848 and 8.6328 Nm, a 10 percent increase; neither identifies muscle force without additional anatomy/load-sharing assumptions. Improved the chapter title, figure label separation and global-frame definition pagination.

## Primary Reading and Its Limits

- [Wu et al. 2002, ISB Part I](https://media.isbweb.org/images/documents/standards/isb_jcs_part_i.pdf): all six scanned pages, five figures, metadata and references visually read. Sections 4.2 and 4.4 support the hip/femur corrections. No experimental reanalysis.
- [Grood's ISB Joint Attitude Debate](https://isbweb.org/students/29-standards-documents): complete first response of 17 February 1990 and the relevant excerpt of the 1992 response read. These support two body-fixed axes, their common perpendicular and geometric rather than temporal interpretation. The original 1983 Grood–Suntay paper was not fully read: publisher access failed, and an author-posted scan could not be obtained readably. It remains a historical citation, not certification of an implemented clinical side/sign convention.
- [Featherstone mcI source](https://royfeatherstone.org/spatial/v1/sourceText/mcI.txt): complete short function and relevant documentation entry read. It accepts central inertia and confirms angular-first signs. The local `src/affine_control/dynamics.py::spatial_inertia` was also inspected and already implements the correct convention; no production function change or fresh upstream execution is claimed.
- [Modern Robotics, Exponential Coordinates of Rigid-Body Motion](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/3-3-3-exponential-coordinates-of-rigid-body-motion/): official main transcript read for finite displacement, body/space descriptions and pure-translation normalization. Existing bibliography entry reused.
- [Harrington et al. 2007 excerpt](https://www.analisedemarcha.com/papers/biomecanica/PLVC/2007_Prediction%20of%20the%20hip%20joint%20centre_Harrington.pdf): the available file contains only journal pages 595 and 602, not the complete eight-page article. Abstract/opening and final scope read; no full-paper, table or figure review claimed.
- [de Leva 1996](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9290(95)00178-6>): publisher abstract inspected via search; full-page retrieval denied. Used only for young-adult male/female parameter and landmark-adjustment scope, not detailed tables.
- [Pavol et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11955511/): abstract and bibliographic metadata read; supports an older-adult estimation method combining body measurements and existing sources. No full-paper or regression-table review.
- [Jensen 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3733761/): all displayed abstract text read, explicitly truncated at 400 words. Supports boys-only growth/model scope; no universal pediatric data or parameter tables claimed. A 1989 abstract excerpt was orientation only and not added as a citation.

The later author-uploaded Zatsiorsky compilation is not treated as the original 1983 study; a Dempster catalogue entry is not treated as reading the report. The two new bibliography notes state the abstract-only limits. Citation resolution is separate from scientific support.
