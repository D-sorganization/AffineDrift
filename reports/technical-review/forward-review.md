## Scientific Decisions

1. **Fixed-Mode Dynamics and Reaction Meaning.** State the local velocity convention, disjoint force accounting and regularity assumptions. Positive-definite symmetric mass and full-row-rank bilateral constraint Jacobian suffice for a unique acceleration/multiplier solution. Multipliers depend on constraint normalization; they are not automatically physical hand-force components. Scaling one row and its acceleration prescription by seven divides its multiplier by seven, preserving generalized reaction and acceleration. Separate residuals with different physical units or state explicit normalization. Do not extend a fixed-mode solve to arbitrary contact transitions.
2. **Integration, Events and Initial Compatibility.** Accuracy, stability and closure are separate. Step refinement can expose convergence over a declared range without monotonically reducing every residual. A Verlet-shaped loop with velocity-dependent acceleration and projection does not establish formal order or symplecticity by name. Distinguish the two-DOF model's interpolated club-angle event from the planar two-hand fixed horizon; neither resolves ball collision. Moving holonomic constraints require Jv = -Phi_t. The declared energy ledger must identify whether it starts before or after initial projection.
3. **Numerical Projection Versus Physical Work.** At fixed configuration, mass-metric projection is v* = v - M^-1 J^T (J M^-1 J^T)^-1 Jv for a stationary full-rank constraint. With masses 2/1 kg, v=(3,-1) m/s and J=(1,1), v*=(7/3,-7/3) m/s; kinetic energy falls from 9.5 to 49/6 J. The loss 4/3 J equals one-half the squared defect weighted by the inverse Schur complement. This is a manufactured numerical correction, not measured muscle work, damping or a justified impact law. The saved provider's absolute work-energy residual (0.0962448 J) and accumulated projection-energy change (-0.777652 J) must remain separate: cancellation can hide numerical errors.
4. **Inputs, Memory and Counterfactuals.** A finite applied torque step is not an impulse. An ideal 2 kg m^2 rotor driven by 3 N m from 0.4 rad/s has acceleration 1.5 rad/s^2 and continuous speed, reaching 0.55 rad/s after 0.1 s. Its 0.1425 J energy gain equals torque times 0.0475 rad displacement. Filtering, hard rate limits, delay and preload are distinct. The pinned planar example maps six commands directly to torques, but actuator states would change that interpretation. Copy the complete state for a forward intervention and re-solve reactions. With Q=Q0+Bu and coefficients fixed at one state, a nonsingular fixed-mode KKT system maps input differences linearly to acceleration differences; u need not be an actuator command. Resetting velocity is a different-state construction, not another input at the original state.
5. **System Boundaries and Moving-Constraint Power.** A stationary ideal internal constraint has zero net whole-system power while transferring energy between bodies. In the manufactured moving-boundary example, masses 2/3 kg satisfy q1-q2=s(t), with sdot=0.5 m/s and sddot=0.2 m/s^2. With v=(2,1.5) m/s and no other loads, acceleration=(0.12,-0.08) m/s^2, multiplier=0.24 N and reaction=(0.24,-0.24) N. Power is 0.12 W, equal to the kinetic-energy derivative; a zero-velocity reset violates the retained boundary condition. Compliant storage/dissipation, applied work and numerical corrections need distinct accounting. Moving-point angular momentum needs transport terms.
6. **Evidence and Validation Scope.** Saved provider timestep results at 2/1/0.5 ms show raw work-energy residuals 0.695/0.373/0.191 J; position closure is not strictly monotone. This is inspection of stored results and their source hashes, not a fresh dynamics run. Finite direct tests and release checks do not prove general convergence, global positive definiteness, dynamics parity across engines or human validity. Inverse/forward replay can be algebraically consistent without being an independent prediction. Net loads do not uniquely identify individual muscles or redundant internal forces. Define the reported planar force-generated couple as summed grip-force moments about the club center, excluding direct wrist torques, under the provider's sign convention.
7. **Representations, Contact and Falsification.** Minimal/maximal coordinates and spatial-vector algorithms are different choices, not mutually exclusive formulations. Compare mapped physical observables, bases and wrench points; kinetic energy need not agree between relatively moving observers. Human contact capacities bound the interpretation of an unconstrained bilateral reaction. Active sets, complementarity, compliant contact and impact laws need their own mode/transition equations. A mismatched frame is an error; a consistent frame transform, documented damping removal or delay can be a valid alternative model. Positive energy growth with supplied work is not itself a failure. Coincident offsets eliminate force-generated moments about that point; offset reversal with re-solved reactions need not reverse the resultant sign.
8. **Reproducibility and Figure Contract.** Record complete model/controller state, exact revision, input law, event definitions and numerical settings. Hidden mutable callbacks can produce inconsistent stepping and diagnostic histories; the pinned prescribed-time control law supports the narrower deterministic interpretation. Shared versioned model outputs for educational interfaces are a recommendation, not an assertion about every existing frontend. Only make_solver_loop changes in the shared generator. Its schematic now explicitly shows a fixed bilateral mode, complete state, candidate next state and separate closure/work/projection audits; refinement compares separate runs. Manufactured tests establish illustrative identities, not a validated golfer model.
