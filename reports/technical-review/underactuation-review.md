## Differential Decay and Publication Corrections

The original inequality omitted an explicit positive rate and did not define its perturbation state. All four chapter copies now define the selected smooth state and nominal variational system, a differentiable symmetric positive metric and a constant positive rate. Differentiating the quadratic error gives its exponential estimate; a lower metric bound throughout the interval and an upper bound at the initial time give the coordinate-error estimate. This is a sufficient certificate, not a claim that every convergent system must satisfy these particular bounds. A finite downswing interval does not establish asymptotic decay beyond that interval.

The manufactured diagonal-flow checks show why the distinction matters: the coordinate norm can grow while the metric error shrinks. A check along a nominal trajectory is not by itself a nonlinear contraction region or a finite-disturbance funnel. Event comparisons must also differentiate arrival time and any reset. These distinctions connect feasible nominal motion, perturbation response and observed club delivery without inferring neural mechanisms from a simplified passive model.
