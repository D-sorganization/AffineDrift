## Corrected Findings

1. **Ground contact and momentum.** Ideal stationary no-slip contacts can supply impulse with zero boundary work. Distinguish material contact velocity from center-of-mass pseudopower and center-of-pressure migration. Include all external loads in momentum balances and name the fixed inertial reference for angular momentum. Qualify deformation, slip and moving supports. The opening figure now depicts interactions rather than a universal ground-to-ball energy supply.
2. **Rigid and flexible power.** Transport a wrench and the same observer's rigid velocity field together, with the correct moment-shift sign. Changing observer is a different operation. Flexible contact power may remain nonzero even with zero resultant wrench. Distinguish relative joint angular velocity from a body's angular velocity and net joint power from individual muscle work.
3. **Energy boundaries.** Total energy conservation does not prevent muscles from converting internal chemical energy into mechanical work. Contact-pair cancellation requires consistent velocities and explicit compliant/sliding accounting. Count gravity once, retain all kinetic modes, and treat shaft return as internal storage conversion. Splitting the shaft does not turn strain energy into a flux; cut traction power and moving-cut transport require their own terms.
4. **Point mass and impact response.** Replace impact effective mass in the kinetic-energy example with declared physical point masses. State the missing rigid-head rotational and distributed club terms. A selected interface's work is only one entry in the full balance; neither peak power nor equal head speed determines total work or energy.
5. **State and transmission history.** Future inputs or input laws are needed to determine evolution. Activation is not energy or measured metabolic cost. Matching desired summed moments does not guarantee identical internal trajectories or transmitted histories. The archived twelve-case allocation comparison has nine positive-dead-zone advantages and three zero-width equivalents for its selected metric; absence of a dead zone does not eliminate force-development time.
6. **Two-hand moments and negative power.** Give the midpoint force-moment formula, distinguish that midpoint from the club center of mass, and include the resultant's moment arm when transporting between them. Pure couples are point-independent; general moments are not. One signed moment component does not determine the spatial power dot product. Already acquired kinetic energy is a state quantity, not an additional power source.
7. **Archived evidence and numerical closure.** Name the 0.20 s inherited-state branch, its 0.05 s sample interval, zero ideal applied joint torques, retained gravity and ideal constraints. Verify stored arrays and provider source hashes. Separate near-machine algebraic wrench-power closure from nonzero timestep-dependent work-energy residuals. No new upstream solver execution, inactive-muscle finding or scapular identification is claimed.
8. **Intervention and verification.** A finite same-state removal need not equal a local derivative; a fixed-mode affine input map is the special exact case. Forward interventions must specify commands/policies, compatible state and event versus fixed-time outcomes. Force-couple collapse requires bounded forces and does not remove free moments. Representation identities, numerical convergence and mechanism interventions answer different questions. Added model complexity does not guarantee reduced uncertainty.
9. **Human inference and practical framing.** Attribute kinematic findings to the measured variables and cohorts; peak sequence does not identify work or causality. Keep mechanical possibilities, archived model results, hypotheses and participant evidence distinct. Clarify drift, state, energy, work and ZTCF glossary conventions without claiming a universal human swing strategy.
