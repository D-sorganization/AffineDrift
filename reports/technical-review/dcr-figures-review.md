## Corrected Findings

1. **Translated Acceleration Set.** The old ellipse was centered at drift but labeled as the input-only set. It mixed current state, tangent displacement, velocity and acceleration without declared scaling. The corrected example declares normalized acceleration coordinates, Euclidean norm, W=I, epsilon=0, drift (2,0.5), input map diag(1,0.5) and a unit input disk. The input ellipse is centered at zero acceleration; the total set is its drift translation. Input (-0.5,0.5) supplies acceleration (-0.5,0.25), giving total (1.5,0.75). Available norm capacity is one and DCR is sqrt(4.25). The diagram shows no finite-time reachable set.
2. **Finite-Horizon Projections.** The old graphic used an arbitrary 1.6-power curve, invented millisecond/centimeter limits, steered trajectories and a strike window. The replacement declares qdot=v, vdot=u, |u|<=1, q(0)=v(0)=0 and no state/rate constraints. Integrating gives velocity integral(u) and position integral((T-s)u). The unit input bound and nonnegative kernel yield the sharp marginal intervals [-T,T] and [-T^2/2,T^2/2]; constant controls +/-1 attain their extremes. These projections do not make every pair in their enclosing rectangle reachable. Acceleration drift and acceleration-space DCR are zero; full-state drift is (v,0). The example is not golfer dispersion or an impact-window estimate.
3. **Nonmonotone Speed Family.** The old phase plot manufactured a physiological trend toward impact, directly contradicting the adjacent reviewed counterexample. The replacement uses its existing scalar Lagrangian: I=exp(2q), U=-exp(2q)/2, hence qdd=1-v^2+exp(-2q)u. At q=0 with unit input bound and scalar absolute-value metric, available capacity is one and DCR=|1-v^2|. Separate states at speeds 0,1,2,3 give 1,0,3,8. This is a connected parameterized family of states, not a trajectory keeping q fixed with nonzero velocity. The graph contains no empirical capacity envelope or universal physiological trend.

The examples explain why instantaneous magnitude, direction, evolving dynamics and available time answer different parts of a correction problem. None establishes golfer-level effects without a declared physical model and empirical evidence. Captions, alt text and expanded descriptions now state those distinctions beside each figure.
