## Adjudicated Findings

1. **Force and Moment Bookkeeping.** Specify a common frame and reference point. The common force is exactly half the resultant, not merely a dominant contribution. Its moment vanishes at the contact midpoint but generally not elsewhere. The differential force produces `d × F_d`; axial differential force is invisible to the rigid resultant. Contact moments do not uniquely identify wrist joint moments.
2. **Rank and Feasibility.** Prove rank five/nullity one for distinct point-force contacts and rank three/nullity three for coincident contacts. A scalar measurement must observe the null direction. Full bilateral wrenches have rank six/nullity six. Planar point-force contacts also have one null direction; only the full-wrench comparison increases nullity from three to six. Algebraic freedom must intersect contact, friction and actuator constraints. Full rank does not establish good conditioning.
3. **Power and Boundaries.** Pair the same observer's wrench and rigid velocity field. Internal loads can have zero rigid power yet nonzero metabolic cost or deformation power. Opposite interface forces do not imply zero paired power under relative motion. Shaft loads are internal to a whole-club boundary and external to a cut subsystem. Negative swing-normal moment alone determines neither full moment power nor total kinetic-energy change. Inertial coordinate terms are not an additional energy source.
4. **Controls and Passivity.** Force-couple collapse/reversal is a fixed-force geometry identity. Free moments survive; bounded-force assumptions matter. Reintegrating changed geometry is a different intervention. State-dependent reactions can transmit active commands or stored energy, so they do not identify passive muscles.
5. **Spatial Archive.** Read the pinned spatial-forward-contact record. Native dynamics differ between engines, but the contact law and stepping scheme are shared. Hands are translational carriages. The driver switch at 0.18 s retains a negative projected couple for 0.0375 s with minimum about -0.4093 N m. Geometry checks retransport achieved forces rather than reintegrating changed contacts. No anatomical or human-phase identification follows.
6. **Sensor Archive.** Component-wise six-channel RMSE differs from the percentile of error-vector norms. The ten-percent perturbation is to cross-talk correction amplitude. Migration settings scale sinusoidal coordinates; the combined case uses untracked 4-mm amplitude, unlike the separate 8-mm case. No device calibration or solver rerun is claimed.
7. **Distributed Grip Archive.** Stiffness and damping totals are per hand and divided across its stations. The 288 trajectories combine twelve states, three station counts, two velocity branches, two steps and two engines; four horizons are nested samples. The transition counter observes any-active versus none-active, not individual station changes. Its zero value coexists with maximum open fraction 1.0. The 0.77 concentration uses station-force norms. The 0.00355 station-refinement diagnostic uses mixed stored coordinates and unit-dependent normalization; it does not prove physical convergence. Distinguish 1/0.5-ms steps from 4/10/25/50-ms horizons.
