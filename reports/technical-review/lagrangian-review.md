## Scientific Decisions

1. **Energy and State.** Energy functions, geometry, forces, constraints and initial conditions enter the equations of motion. Scalar energy totals do not specify a trajectory. Use local independent coordinates z, coordinate rates v, and a separate generalized velocity nu when position and velocity representations differ. Positive-definite inertia requires positive kinetic energy for every nonzero independent velocity; it does not follow merely from choosing notation. Internal memory can require state beyond position and velocity.
2. **Flexible Geometry.** Shaft deformation can change gravitational potential as well as elastic energy. Off-diagonal inertia changes the input-to-acceleration map even when a mode has no direct actuator. Specify modal reference, boundaries and validity; do not double-count an unexplained geometric potential. Tangent and prestress stiffness follow the declared force/constraint linearization and are not extra energy supplies.
3. **Force and Energy Accounting.** Count viscous damping once. A Rayleigh function is optional, and is not a representation of every dissipative law. The signed conservative load is minus the potential gradient. The declared Christoffel construction gives C+C-transpose=M-dot and the correct mechanical energy balance, including applied and retained force power minus viscous loss. Mechanical and metabolic power remain different quantities.
4. **Affine Input and Actuator State.** At a fixed complete state, input increments map linearly to vector-field increments. Neither total acceleration nor evolved trajectories, speed, energy or neural effort inherit that scaling. The declared input origin changes the drift/input split. Command-level and applied-torque-level interventions differ when activation or actuator memory remains; saturation and state-dependent impedance require a stated input law.
5. **Reactions and Contact.** Solve stationary ideal bilateral reactions with accelerations. The constrained acceleration map G-v-c is a projection of the unconstrained M-inverse-B map, not the generalized-force map B itself. Each counterfactual branch solves its own reactions. Nonholonomic ideal constraints admit Lagrange–d'Alembert treatment; arbitrary substitution into Hamilton's action is not equivalent. Friction, separation and impact can require different smooth modes, set-valued laws or velocity resets; not every mode change requires a reset.
6. **Moving Boundaries.** Differentiating Jv=-Phi-t gives Jv-dot=-J-dot-v-J-t-v-Phi-tt, with total and partial Jacobian derivatives distinguished. Reaction power is -lambda-transpose-Phi-t. A prescribed support can supply energy at zero separately declared drive. Adding a clock does not eliminate that boundary power. Reduced time-dependent kinematics require their own energy identity.
7. **Counterfactual Definition and Sign.** Distinguish pointwise, stitched, forward and branched zero-torque counterfactual constructions under the site's normative definitions. For an unconstrained or validly reduced mechanical model, ZVCF acceleration is -M-inverse-h(z,0), the signed drift load is -h and the formal holding load is +h. A holding load can lie outside the actuator's range. Explicit constraints require their acceleration/reaction solve. An all-zero velocity reset is undefined within a retained moving-constraint model when Phi-t is nonzero; freezing a boundary or resetting relative rates is another intervention.
8. **Golf Interpretation and Evidence.** Earlier work changes loading and geometry; these affect both subsequent drift and the response to current input. Reactions transfer power across chosen boundaries, and elastic release transfers energy within the whole-system ledger. These relationships support precise model comparisons, not muscle allocation, metabolic efficiency or a coaching benefit without separate measurements and causal evidence.
