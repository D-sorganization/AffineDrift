## Scientific Decisions

1. **Point Speed Versus Body Energy.** Replace the ambiguous positive-acceleration/negative-power claim with a rigid-body endpoint formula and a manufactured rod counterexample. The tip accelerates tangentially at 1 m/s² while the body's total kinetic-energy rate is -1 W; the translational loss exceeds its rotational gain. For a fixed-mass particle, increasing speed still requires positive net power. These are different system/quantity statements, not an exception to work-energy balance.
2. **Physical and Coordinate Energy.** Use center-of-mass translation plus tensor rotational energy; distinguish fixed-axis reduction, coordinate cross-terms and explicit moving-base terms. A physical segment's energy is defined after specifying body and observer; distributing cross-terms among generalized coordinates is a different convention. Do not add moving-grip rotational energy directly to COM translation. The manufactured coordinate-mixing test preserves 2.5 J while diagonal-only accounting misses 1 J.
3. **Power-Conjugate Motion.** Declare material-point velocity, matched wrench reference and observer. Separate a segment moment contribution from joint actuator power using relative angular velocity. Equal/opposite moments can supply a distal segment while the actuator absorbs net mechanical power. Include other force/moment channels before claiming net segment energy change; no muscle/metabolic inference follows.
4. **Storage and Boundary.** Correct the worked example: 15 J hand work minus 2 J aerodynamic work raises whole-club mechanical energy by 13 J. Releasing 8 J of internal strain raises K+V by 21 J, not total storage by 21 J. A linked shaft/head example explicitly assigns shaft K+V increase of 5 J and all aerodynamic loss to the head: shaft total change -3 J, interface work 18 J and head gain 16 J reconcile to the same 13 J total. These are synthetic assignments, not measured golf values.
5. **Impulse and Constraints.** State net external impulse, inertial observer and fixed angular-momentum reference conditions. Distinguish fixed-circle radial force from a force directed toward moving hands. Internal joint reaction power can cancel for the combined system while transferring energy between segments; acceleration-term splits are not automatically physical energy supplies.
6. **Sequence and Evidence.** Speed-peak order alone identifies neither segment energy loss nor its destination. Separate the pinned double-pendulum E1 comparison from the coupled uncertainty H4 result and its face/path tradeoff. Tinmark's published abstract concerns 45 skilled golfers' kinematics; Robertson/Winter's abstract concerns two walking subjects and a sagittal model; McPhee is a narrative review. None supplies a new human causal intervention here.
7. **Counterfactual Interpretation.** Match admissible state, controller memory, parameters and event rules. Finite refinement/uncertainty tests provide bounded support. Loss of a ranking does not uniquely identify preparation or parameters; numerical and event changes remain alternatives. Pin all upstream source links to the book's declared immutable revision.
8. **Figure Semantics.** Give both plotted quantities explicit dimensionless normalization. Label the bar example as translational energy at fixed speed and vary mass alone; do not claim it demonstrates rotational inertia. Only make_speed_energy changes in the shared generator; all other function ASTs are preserved.
