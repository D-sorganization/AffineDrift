## Scientific Decisions

1. **Net Joint Demand and Bounded Allocation.** Define signed musculotendon path derivatives, external-load accounting, and the separate passive-load convention. A force-map null space does not guarantee unlimited feasible allocations. The two-antagonist fixture has a bounded interval, with dynamic realizability as an additional condition; fully specified internal states can further constrain forces.
2. **Excitation, Force, and Power.** Distinguish the sampled electrical signal from activation state and force. Signed path power is 6 W for all feasible illustrated force pairs; it is neither individual fibre power nor metabolic expenditure. EMG alone cannot identify club power or eliminate unobserved force sources.
3. **Geometric Stiffness and Impedance.** Derive K = sum(k r^2 - F r-prime) for a declared local elastic model. Finite differences verify +1.8 versus -0.2 N m/rad with and without the geometric contribution, despite positive material stiffness. Distinguish local stiffness from a movement-curve slope and from full dynamic impedance. Do not infer whole-joint instability or universal co-contraction benefits.
4. **Elastic Energy and Transmission Timing.** Replace force storage with strain-energy storage; 50-to-100 N loading stores an additional 0.375 J in the stated linear example. Series stiffness does not itself define delay. Separate tendon slack length and short-range stiffness from the provider's phenomenological rotational dead zone; its zero-gap comparison bounds its claimed advantage.
5. **Anatomical Coordinates and Contact Loads.** Separate scapular/shoulder motion, forearm pronation, carpal wrist motion, contact moments and anatomical joint demand. Axis projection and work-conjugate generalized torque require the stated kinematic mapping. Coordinate recommendations and bone-tracking evidence do not identify a golf scapular driver.
6. **Study Attribution and Human Evidence.** Correct Kao/Pink/Jobe/Perry attribution. Preserve exact sample/measurement scope: 15 competitive men / four bilateral scapular muscles; 15 subelite right-handed men / bilateral ECU. Do not invent Kao electrode construction, interpret nonsignificance as equivalence, or claim identified force allocations.
7. **Full-Wrench Matching and Fixed Geometry.** Two synthetic bilateral allocations give the same 100 N resultant, 12 N m moment, and 236 W at a specified rigid twist. At fixed contact geometry, changing arm posture alone cannot change the common-force lever arm. Moving both contacts 2 cm relative to the same origin changes the moment to 14 N m unless other loads change. The provider's scalar control-induced angular-acceleration demand is weaker than full-wrench or full-trajectory matching.
8. **Discriminating Experiments and Presentation.** Match measurements to hypotheses; randomized cues do not randomize the adopted biological mediator. Preserve supported/contradicted/inconclusive/untested distinctions, practical uncertainty and participant holdout. Pin the provider references and bound the schematic as coupled anatomy rather than interchangeable independent actuators or measured pathways.
